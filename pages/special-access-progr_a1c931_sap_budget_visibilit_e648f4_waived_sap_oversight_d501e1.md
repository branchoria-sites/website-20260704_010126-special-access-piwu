## Introduction

Waived special access programmes (SAPs) represent the narrowest form of congressional budget reporting within the US Department of Defense. Unlike ordinary classified programmes, a waived SAP allows the Secretary of Defense to withhold elements of the statutory SAP reports from the full congressional defence committees when disclosure is judged likely to harm national security. Instead, the withheld information and the justification for withholding it are provided only to the chair and ranking minority member of each of the four congressional defence committees. In practice, that means detailed budget knowledge may be concentrated in as few as eight legislators rather than being available to the full committees that normally oversee defence spending.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&amp;num=0&amp;req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title10-section119" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uscode.house.gov">[U.S. Code]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uscode.house.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Code10 USC 119: Special access programs: congressional...... waiver, jointly to the chairman and ranking minority member of each of the...</span></span></span>

This arrangement is intended to protect exceptionally sensitive military capabilities, but it also creates a distinctive governance problem. The smaller the oversight circle, the harder it becomes to compare programmes, challenge assumptions, identify [duplication]({{ 'duplication/' | relative_url }}), or sustain institutional knowledge when key members leave office. The issue is therefore not whether [Congress]({{ 'congress/' | relative_url }}) is informed at all, but how narrowing the audience changes the nature of democratic oversight.

## How waived reporting narrows the audience

US law establishes detailed annual reporting requirements for Department of Defense SAPs, including programme descriptions, cost estimates and future funding projections. However, 10 U.S.C. §119 also authorises the Secretary of Defense to waive the inclusion of particular information on a case-by-case basis if including it in the normal committee report would adversely affect national security. When that authority is used, the omitted information is transmitted only to the chair and ranking minority member of each congressional defence committee, together with the justification for the waiver.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&amp;num=0&amp;req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title10-section119" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uscode.house.gov">[U.S. Code]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uscode.house.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Code10 USC 119: Special access programs: congressional...... waiver, jointly to the chairman and ranking minority member of each of the...</span></span></span>

The four committees involved are:

* House Appropriations Committee (Defence Subcommittee leadership)

Because each committee contributes only its chair and ranking minority member, the effective audience for the most sensitive budget information can be limited to eight members of Congress. Security training material used within the Department of Defense identifies waived SAPs as [unacknowledged SAPs]({{ 'unacknowledged/' | relative_url }}) for which the statutory reporting requirements have been waived under Section 119, reflecting their exceptional status within the SAP system.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cdse.edu/Portals/124/Documents/student-guides/SA001-guide.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cdse.edu">[CDSE]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cdse.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Code.Read more</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Student Guide Course: Special Access Program (SAP)...Waived SAPs are unacknowledged SAPs for which the Secretary of Defense has waiv...</span></span></span>

Importantly, this does not mean that the programmes are entirely exempt from congressional oversight. Rather, oversight becomes highly concentrated, with most committee members and staff excluded from the most sensitive budget details.

## What chair-and-ranking access changes

Concentrating access in committee leadership changes oversight in several practical ways.

First, it reduces the number of legislators who can independently scrutinise programme costs. Most defence appropriations are evaluated through committee deliberation, staff analysis and cross-comparison with related programmes. When only two members from each committee possess the complete picture, much of that collaborative review cannot occur.

Second, institutional memory becomes more fragile. Committee chairs and ranking members change with elections, retirements and shifts in party control. Although classified handovers occur, a very small oversight community is inherently more vulnerable to losing historical knowledge about why a programme was created, how its budget evolved or whether earlier concerns were resolved.

Third, professional [committee staff]({{ 'staff-access/' | relative_url }}) have more limited roles. Congressional oversight normally depends heavily on cleared staff members who conduct technical and financial analysis. Waived reporting reduces the extent to which those analytical resources can be applied because access follows the statutory reporting restrictions. Department of Defense SAP policy also centralises and tightly controls congressional interactions concerning SAP material, reinforcing the narrow dissemination model.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/DD/issuances/dodm/520507m1.PDF?ver=o_3_m4lDAtLKPOLQHatcYA%3D%3D" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">DoDM 5205.07, &quot;Special Access Program Security Manual...Jan 17, 2025 — Functions as the DoD single point of congressional liaison co...</span></span></span>

The practical consequence is that oversight relies more heavily on the judgement, continuity and diligence of a handful of elected officials than on the broader committee process used for most defence programmes.

## Oversight risks when knowledge is concentrated

Supporters of waived reporting argue that limiting distribution reduces the chance that adversaries can infer sensitive capabilities from budget patterns, technical descriptions or programme relationships. Even seemingly mundane financial information can reveal operational priorities, acquisition schedules or emerging technologies when combined with other [intelligence]({{ 'intelligence/' | relative_url }}).

Critics, however, identify several governance risks that arise when budget knowledge is concentrated.

* **Reduced cross-checking.** Fewer reviewers mean fewer opportunities to detect inconsistent cost estimates, overlapping missions or unrealistic programme assumptions.
* **Greater dependence on executive-branch information.** If only a small number of legislators receive the complete briefing, independent verification becomes more difficult because there are fewer informed participants able to question official explanations.
* **Weaker comparative budgeting.** Members without access cannot easily compare waived SAP spending against other classified or conventional defence priorities during appropriations debates.
* **Limited public [accountability]({{ 'accountability/' | relative_url }}).** While secrecy is intentional, the public cannot readily distinguish between programmes that are merely classified and those whose reporting has been further narrowed through a statutory waiver.

These concerns have appeared repeatedly in discussions of "black budget" oversight. The debate is generally not about whether secrecy is ever justified, but about where the balance should be struck between protecting operational security and preserving meaningful legislative scrutiny.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&amp;num=0&amp;req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title10-section119" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uscode.house.gov">[U.S. Code]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uscode.house.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Code10 USC 119: Special access programs: congressional...... waiver, jointly to the chairman and ranking minority member of each of the...</span></span></span>

## Why the eight-member model remains controversial

The eight-member reporting model illustrates a recurring tension in US national security governance. The same mechanism that minimises the risk of disclosure also limits the diversity of oversight.

Advocates view the arrangement as a carefully bounded exception rather than a wholesale exemption. The waiver must be made on a case-by-case basis, and the Secretary of Defense remains obligated to provide the withheld information and an accompanying justification to the designated congressional leaders. The statute therefore preserves a constitutional oversight channel while recognising that some programmes are considered too sensitive for wider dissemination.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&amp;num=0&amp;req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title10-section119" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uscode.house.gov">[U.S. Code]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uscode.house.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Code10 USC 119: Special access programs: congressional...... waiver, jointly to the chairman and ranking minority member of each of the...</span></span></span>

Sceptics counter that concentrating knowledge in eight legislators can reduce the effectiveness of Congress as an institutional check. Oversight is strongest when multiple members, supported by experienced cleared staff, can test assumptions, compare programmes and challenge executive-branch assertions over many years. Restricting access inevitably narrows those opportunities, even if the legal reporting requirements continue to exist in modified form.

Within the broader system of special access programmes, [waived SAP reporting]({{ 'waived-oversight/' | relative_url }}) therefore represents the point at which budget secrecy reaches its maximum extent inside Congress. The central policy question is not whether information is reported, but whether oversight performed by a very small leadership group can provide the same level of accountability as oversight exercised by the full congressional defence committees.
