## Introduction

A Special Access Program (SAP) [read-in]({{ 'read-in/' | relative_url }}) is the formal security briefing that takes place after an individual has been approved for access to a specific programme and demonstrated a genuine need to know. Its purpose is not simply to explain that information is classified. Instead, it teaches the precise rules, protected facts, programme-specific terminology and daily security practices that allow someone to perform their assigned role without accidentally revealing the programme, its activities or even, in some cases, its existence. The briefing also establishes a personal legal and professional obligation through signed non-disclosure and indoctrination agreements that remain in force after access ends.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cdse.edu/Portals/124/Documents/student-guides/SA002-guide.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cdse.edu">[CDSE]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cdse.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Special Access Program Security Annual Refresher</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Special Access Program Security Annual RefresherFebruary 2, 2022 — Remember, by signing the SAPIA in both the indoctrination briefing...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 2, 2022</span></span></span>



<img src="{{ "/assets/images/special-access-progr_a1c931_read_in_rules_f043eb_sap_read_in_briefing_1b8334-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="A Special Access Program (SAP) Read In illustration 1" data-theme-src-dark="{{ "/assets/images/special-access-progr_a1c931_read_in_rules_f043eb_sap_read_in_briefing_1b8334-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{ "/assets/images/special-access-progr_a1c931_read_in_rules_f043eb_sap_read_in_briefing_1b8334-Illustration-1-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">

Unlike general security awareness training, a SAP read-in is tailored to one compartment. The emphasis is practical: what the individual may know, what they may discuss, how programme information is identified, who may receive it, and how seemingly harmless details can expose sensitive [operations]({{ 'operations/' | relative_url }}).

## From approval to controlled knowledge

A read-in begins only after the programme's access [approval authority]({{ 'approval-authority/' | relative_url }}) has authorised the individual's access request. The briefing marks the point where a person moves from being merely eligible for classified information to being authorised for one specific compartment. During this process, the individual signs the applicable [SAP indoctrination]({{ 'sapia/' | relative_url }}) agreement and is introduced to the programme's Security Classification Guide (SCG) and local security procedures. These documents define exactly what information is classified, at what level, and how it must be handled.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cdse.edu/Portals/124/Documents/student-guides/SA002-guide.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cdse.edu">[CDSE]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cdse.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Special Access Program Security Annual Refresher</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Special Access Program Security Annual RefresherFebruary 2, 2022 — Remember, by signing the SAPIA in both the indoctrination briefing...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 2, 2022</span></span></span>

An important lesson taught at this stage is that access is deliberately limited. Being read into one SAP does not authorise access to another SAP, even if both concern similar technology or share the same classification level. Every disclosure still depends on continuing need to know within the person's assigned duties.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/DD/issuances/dodm/520507m1.PDF" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[WHS ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">WHS ESDDoDM 5205.07, &quot;Special Access Program Security Manual...17 Jan 2025 — Personnel read into at least one SAP and, with a demonstra...</span></span></span>

The briefing therefore establishes three practical boundaries:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* what information the individual is authorised to receive;
* which programme personnel are authorised discussion partners;
* the operational limits of that authority as assignments change.

</div>

## Role-specific briefing content

Although administrative elements are standardised, the substance of a SAP read-in varies according to the person's role.

An engineer may receive guidance on technical documentation, design discussions and laboratory procedures. An operator may be briefed on mission planning restrictions and communications practices. A programme manager may receive additional instruction on contracting, visitor controls or cross-programme coordination. The principle is consistent: individuals learn only the information necessary for their assigned responsibilities rather than the programme in its entirety.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/DD/issuances/dodm/520507m1.PDF" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[WHS ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">WHS ESDDoDM 5205.07, &quot;Special Access Program Security Manual...17 Jan 2025 — Personnel read into at least one SAP and, with a demonstra...</span></span></span>

The briefing commonly covers practical questions such as:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* which facilities may be used for programme work;
* approved methods for discussing programme matters;
* where programme documents may be created, stored and transmitted;
* reporting procedures for security incidents or suspicious contacts;
* responsibilities for protecting information during travel, meetings and remote work where authorised.

</div>

Rather than presenting abstract security theory, the briefing explains how these rules apply during ordinary work so that security becomes part of routine professional practice.

## Codewords, markings and indirect disclosure

One of the most distinctive elements of a SAP read-in is instruction on programme-specific identifiers.

Personnel learn the approved nickname, codeword or control marking associated with their compartment, together with the document markings that indicate special handling requirements. They are taught how banner markings, [portion markings]({{ 'portion-markings/' | relative_url }}) and programme caveats identify protected information, and how those markings affect copying, transmission, storage and discussion.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cdse.edu/Portals/124/Documents/student-guides/shorts/SAS0030-guide.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cdse.edu">[cdse.edu]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cdse.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Please note that all markings seen in this Short are for training</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Special Access Programs: Markings Short Student GuideJuly 1, 2025 — In this Short, you will learn to recognize and apply control mark...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 1, 2025</span></span></span>

Equally important is learning what *not* to reveal.

The briefing typically warns against indirect disclosure—revealing sensitive information without explicitly stating classified facts. Examples include:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* mentioning programme participation to someone outside the compartment;
* identifying protected facilities or meeting locations;
* referring to programme nicknames in unsecured conversations;
* combining individually harmless facts that collectively reveal classified relationships or capabilities;
* exposing programme involvement through calendars, emails, presentations or procurement documents.

</div>

This emphasis reflects a central feature of SAP security: protecting context can be as important as protecting technical details.

## Building secure working habits

A successful read-in teaches habits rather than isolated rules.

Personnel are expected to recognise programme markings before reading or distributing documents, verify another person's authorisation before discussing protected information, and challenge uncertain situations instead of making assumptions. Security officials stress that [uncertainty]({{ 'uncertainty/' | relative_url }}) should lead to verification rather than disclosure.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cdse.edu/Portals/124/Documents/student-guides/SA002-guide.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cdse.edu">[CDSE]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cdse.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Special Access Program Security Annual Refresher</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Special Access Program Security Annual RefresherFebruary 2, 2022 — Remember, by signing the SAPIA in both the indoctrination briefing...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 2, 2022</span></span></span>

Routine expectations usually include:

* checking that [workspaces]({{ 'workspaces/' | relative_url }}) are properly sanitised after meetings;

<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* using only authorised information systems and communication channels;
* following approved storage and destruction procedures;
* reporting suspected compromises promptly;
* protecting conversations from inadvertent exposure in public or mixed-access environments.

</div>

Annual refresher training reinforces these practices throughout an individual's period of SAP access.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cdse.edu/Portals/124/Documents/student-guides/SA002-guide.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cdse.edu">[CDSE]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cdse.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Special Access Program Security Annual Refresher</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Special Access Program Security Annual RefresherFebruary 2, 2022 — Remember, by signing the SAPIA in both the indoctrination briefing...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 2, 2022</span></span></span>

## Why the briefing focuses on behaviour rather than memorisation

A common misconception is that a SAP read-in primarily teaches secret technical facts. In reality, much of the briefing concerns behaviour.

Programme details inevitably change as projects evolve, but disciplined security practices remain essential throughout the programme's life. For that reason, [read-ins]({{ 'read-ins/' | relative_url }}) concentrate on decision-making: recognising when information belongs inside the compartment, understanding how need-to-know limits conversations, identifying authorised channels, and avoiding accidental disclosure through ordinary professional activities.

This behavioural emphasis also explains why personnel remain bound by their indoctrination agreement after leaving the programme.
