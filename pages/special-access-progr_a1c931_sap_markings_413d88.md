## Introduction

SAP markings are the visible warning system for special-access-program material. They tell an authorised reader that the ordinary classification label — Confidential, Secret or Top Secret — is not the whole handling instruction. A document may be “Top Secret”, but if its banner also says “SPECIAL ACCESS REQUIRED” or “SAR” with a programme name, codeword, or authorised identifier, the document belongs inside a narrower access regime: only people formally approved for that SAP, with a need to know, may handle the protected information. Executive Order 13526 permits SAPs only where the threat or vulnerability is exceptional and ordinary access rules are not enough; the markings are the practical mechanism that carries that extra control into documents, media and information systems.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CFR-2010-title3-vol1/pdf/CFR-2010-title3-vol1-eo13526.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: govinfo.gov">[GovInfo]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">govinfo.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on govinfo.gov.</span></span></span>



<img src="{{ "/assets/images/special-access-progr_a1c931_sap_markings_413d88-overview.webp" | relative_url }}" alt="Overview image for Markings" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">

The key point is simple but easily missed: SAP markings do not create a new classification level above Top Secret. They add a control channel on top of the classification level. That is why [banner lines]({{ 'banner-lines/' | relative_url }}), [portion markings]({{ 'portion-markings/' | relative_url }}), programme identifiers, “MULTIPLE PROGRAMS”, and sometimes “WAIVED” matter so much. They are not decorative labels; they are operational instructions meant to prevent a cleared but unauthorised person, office, contractor, courier, archive reviewer or automated system from treating SAP material as ordinary classified information. Federal marking rules require classified information to be marked uniformly and conspicuously so that there is no doubt about its protected status and level of protection; SAP markings are the specialised version of that principle for special-access material.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-32/subtitle-B/chapter-XX/part-2001" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ecfr.gov">[eCFR]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ecfr.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">32 CFR Part 2001 -- Classified National Security Information...</span></span></span>

## What “Special Access Required” adds to a banner

A normal classified document banner tells the reader the highest classification level of information in the document. A SAP banner adds the fact that access is controlled by a specific programme. In Department of Defense marking guidance, the banner line for SAP material begins with the classification level spelled out, followed by a double slash, then “SPECIAL ACCESS REQUIRED” or the authorised abbreviation “SAR”, followed by the SAP nickname or codeword. CDSE training gives the notional example “TOP SECRET//SPECIAL ACCESS REQUIRED-SWAGGER”, with all examples used for training rather than real programme disclosure.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cdse.edu/Portals/124/Documents/student-guides/shorts/SAS0030-guide.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cdse.edu">[CDSE]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cdse.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Speical Access Programs: Markings Short</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Speical Access Programs: Markings Short...</span></span></span>

That order is important. The classification level comes first because it establishes the baseline degree of national-security protection. The SAP caveat comes next because it narrows who may see the information within that classification level. Dissemination controls, if any, come after the programme marking. In other words, the banner is a compact routing instruction: first “how classified is this?”, then “which special-access channel controls it?”, then “are there additional release or reporting restrictions?”<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cdse.edu/Portals/124/Documents/student-guides/shorts/SAS0030-guide.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cdse.edu">[CDSE]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cdse.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Speical Access Programs: Markings Short</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Speical Access Programs: Markings Short...</span></span></span>

The word “conspicuous” is not casual. DoD SAP marking policy says the banner line must be obvious enough to alert anyone handling the document that it contains SAP material, for example by using larger type, a different font or a contrasting colour. The point is to make mishandling less likely at the moment of use: on the first page, on each page, on copied material, on attached charts, and on media that may move through storage, transport or review processes.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/DD/issuances/dodm/520507-V4p.PDF" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[WHS ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">520507 V4p</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">WHS ESDDoDM 5205.07, Volume 4, &quot;Special Access Program (SAP) Security Manual: Marking,&quot; October 10, 2013; Incorporating Change 2 on Septe...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 10, 2013</span></span></span>

This visible design is especially important because clearance alone is not enough. A person with a [Top Secret clearance]({{ 'clearance-gap/' | relative_url }}) may still be outside the authorised SAP access list. The banner is therefore a gatekeeping signal: it warns the reader not to assume that a normal classified channel, a normal distribution list or a general-purpose secure system is sufficient.

## How the banner line is built

A SAP banner line is easiest to understand as a sequence. For a single notional programme, the pattern is:

**TOP SECRET//SPECIAL ACCESS REQUIRED-PROGRAM NAME**<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/DD/issuances/dodm/520507m1.PDF" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[esd.whs.mil]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Source details in endnotes.</span></span></span>

or, using the authorised abbreviation:

**TOP SECRET//SAR-PROGRAM NAME**

CDSE guidance says SAP banner markings must use all uppercase letters, either fully spelled out in English or using authorised abbreviations. The programme nickname or codeword is written out in the banner line rather than replaced by the shorter programme identifier used in portion markings. If dissemination controls such as “WAIVED”, “NOFORN” or “REL TO” apply, they appear after the SAP programme marking.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cdse.edu/Portals/124/Documents/student-guides/shorts/SAS0030-guide.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cdse.edu">[CDSE]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cdse.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Speical Access Programs: Markings Short</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Speical Access Programs: Markings Short...</span></span></span>

The separators are part of the control language. Double slashes separate major marking categories, such as classification, [SCI controls]({{ 'sci-controls/' | relative_url }}), SAP controls and dissemination controls. A hyphen joins “SAR” or “SPECIAL ACCESS REQUIRED” to the programme indicator. The [Intelligence]({{ 'intelligence/' | relative_url }}) Community markings register emphasises that the hyphen after “SAR-” is not a general separator; it is treated as part of the SAP category indicator for syntax purposes.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cdse.edu/Portals/124/Documents/jobaids/information/IC-Markings-System-Register.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cdse.edu">[CDSE]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cdse.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">IC Markings System Register</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">IC Markings System Register</span></span></span>

That may sound technical, but it matters in practice. A banner such as **TOP SECRET//SAR-BLUE FROG//NOFORN** tells the reader three different things: the document is Top Secret, it belongs to the named SAP channel, and it is not releasable to foreign nationals. A wrongly ordered or ambiguously separated banner can cause errors in routing, copying, automated labelling, electronic access control or derivative marking.

## Programme identifiers and portion markings

Document banners identify the overall controls for the document. Portion markings do the same job at the paragraph, subject line, attachment, slide, table, chart or other portion level. This distinction matters because one document can contain differently controlled pieces of information. ISOO’s marking handbook explains that portion markings identify the information that must be protected and the level of protection required; examples of portions include titles, subjects, paragraphs, bullets, graphics, tables and charts.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/files/isoo/training/marking-booklet-revision.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Marking Classified National Security Information Handbook</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesMarking Classified National Security Information Handbook - Revised 01/04/2018...</span></span></span>

For SAP material, CDSE guidance says portion markings containing SAP information include the classification abbreviation, then “SAR”, then the programme identifier. In a notional example, a Top Secret portion in a programme with the programme identifier “SAM” would be marked in the form **(TS//SAR-SAM)**. Where multiple SAPs apply to a portion, the identifiers are listed alphabetically and separated by a single slash.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cdse.edu/Portals/124/Documents/student-guides/shorts/SAS0030-guide.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cdse.edu">[CDSE]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cdse.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Speical Access Programs: Markings Short</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Speical Access Programs: Markings Short...</span></span></span>

The banner and the portion marking therefore serve different audiences. The banner warns everyone handling the page or file that the whole document requires SAP-aware handling. The portion marking helps authorised users identify exactly which parts carry which restrictions, especially when copying excerpts, preparing derivative products, briefing slides, emails or attachments. The banner says “this document is in a SAP channel”; the portion mark says “this specific paragraph, title, chart or attachment is controlled this way”.

This is one reason SAP marking is closely tied to classification guides. DoD information-security rules state that SAP information is to be marked in accordance with the security classification guide developed for each programme. That guide supplies the programme-specific details: what is classified, at what level, for how long, under which access channel, and with which marking conventions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.dodig.mil/Portals/48/Documents/Policy/520001_vol2.pdf?ver=2017-04-25-160926-223" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dodig.mil">[dodig.mil]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dodig.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">520001 vol2</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">SAP control markings used in the banner line and at portions denote classified information that requires...Read more...</span></span></span>

## Multiple programmes and mixed controls

The marking problem becomes harder when a document combines information from more than one SAP. Current CDSE SAP marking guidance says that two programmes can be listed in the banner line with each programme’s “SAR” entry separated by a single slash, as in the notional form **TOP SECRET//SAR-BLUE FROG/SAR-MUDDY PATH**. When three or more SAPs are included in a single document, the banner uses **SAR-MULTIPLE PROGRAMS** rather than listing all programme names in the banner.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cdse.edu/Portals/124/Documents/student-guides/shorts/SAS0030-guide.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cdse.edu">[CDSE]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cdse.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Speical Access Programs: Markings Short</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Speical Access Programs: Markings Short...</span></span></span>

DoD SAP marking policy similarly says that when information from three or more SAPs is included in a single document, “MULTIPLE PROGRAMS” is placed after SAR in the banner line. The same policy connects that marking to derivative classification: “Multiple Sources” is placed as the derivative classification instruction on the first page, and the multiple sources are listed at the end of the document.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/DD/issuances/dodm/520507-V4p.PDF" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[WHS ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">520507 V4p</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">WHS ESDDoDM 5205.07, Volume 4, &quot;Special Access Program (SAP) Security Manual: Marking,&quot; October 10, 2013; Incorporating Change 2 on Septe...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 10, 2013</span></span></span>

The practical logic is balance. Listing every programme in a crowded banner can become unwieldy and can reveal more programme relationships than necessary to someone who only needs to recognise that the document is SAP-controlled. But hiding the programme basis completely would create accountability problems. The “MULTIPLE PROGRAMS” convention preserves the immediate handling warning while moving fuller source detail into the document’s controlled classification information.

Portion markings still matter in these mixed cases. CDSE guidance indicates that multiple programme identifiers in portion markings are listed alphabetically and separated by slashes. This helps a derivative classifier or authorised reader understand which exact programmes apply to a particular paragraph or attachment, rather than assuming every part of a combined document belongs to every programme named somewhere in the file.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cdse.edu/Portals/124/Documents/student-guides/shorts/SAS0030-guide.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cdse.edu">[CDSE]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cdse.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Speical Access Programs: Markings Short</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Speical Access Programs: Markings Short...</span></span></span>

## Waived SAPs and the “WAIVED” marking

The most sensitive marking readers are likely to notice in this area is “WAIVED”. In DoD usage, a waived SAP is a subset of [unacknowledged SAPs]({{ 'unacknowledged/' | relative_url }}) for which the Secretary of Defense has exempted the programme from normal congressional reporting requirements under the relevant statutory process. In marking terms, “WAIVED” appears as a dissemination control after the SAP programme marking. CDSE training describes “WAIVED” as signalling that the programme is exempted from normal congressional reporting requirements by the Secretary of Defense.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cdse.edu/Portals/124/Documents/student-guides/shorts/SAS0030-guide.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cdse.edu">[CDSE]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cdse.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Speical Access Programs: Markings Short</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Speical Access Programs: Markings Short...</span></span></span>

DoD SAP marking policy is explicit that SAPs specifically exempted from normal congressional reporting requirements by the Secretary of Defense must be marked “WAIVED” in the banner line and at applicable portions. DoD information-security guidance gives the notional form **TOP SECRET//SAR-BP//WAIVED**, treating “WAIVED” as a dissemination control marking rather than a classification level.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/DD/issuances/dodm/520507-V4p.PDF" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[WHS ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">520507 V4p</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">WHS ESDDoDM 5205.07, Volume 4, &quot;Special Access Program (SAP) Security Manual: Marking,&quot; October 10, 2013; Incorporating Change 2 on Septe...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 10, 2013</span></span></span><span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.dodig.mil/Portals/48/Documents/Policy/520001_vol2.pdf?ver=2017-04-25-160926-223" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dodig.mil">[dodig.mil]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dodig.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">520001 vol2</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">SAP control markings used in the banner line and at portions denote classified information that requires...Read more...</span></span></span>

This distinction is important for public understanding. “WAIVED” does not mean the document is beyond all law or oversight. It means the material belongs to a narrower reporting and handling channel. The marking warns authorised readers that even within SAP procedures, additional restrictions apply. In day-to-day handling, the visible “WAIVED” label helps prevent a document from being routed through ordinary SAP distribution, ordinary congressional reporting support workflows, or ordinary derivative products without checking the programme’s special instructions.

## Why markings prevent mishandling

SAP markings are a failure-prevention system. They help stop at least four kinds of error.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.cdse.edu/Portals/124/Documents/jobaids/information/IC-Markings-System-Register.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cdse.edu">[cdse.edu]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cdse.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">IC Markings System Register</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">IC Markings System Register</span></span></span>

First, they prevent **clearance confusion**. A person may be cleared to the document’s classification level but not read into the SAP. The SAR marking signals that a clearance check is not enough; programme access must also be verified.

Second, they prevent **channel confusion**. SAP material is not supposed to move through every system or office that can process collateral Secret or Top Secret information. DoD policy notes that SAP information must be processed only on an information system accredited for SAP processing and operating at a classification level that meets or exceeds the SAP data’s classification.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.dodig.mil/Portals/48/Documents/Policy/520001_vol2.pdf?ver=2017-04-25-160926-223" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dodig.mil">[dodig.mil]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dodig.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">520001 vol2</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">SAP control markings used in the banner line and at portions denote classified information that requires...Read more...</span></span></span>

Third, they prevent **copying and excerpting mistakes**. Portion markings identify which specific paragraphs, charts, titles or attachments carry SAP controls. Without portion markings, an authorised user might extract a paragraph into a new memo, briefing slide or email and accidentally strip away the special-access warning.

Fourth, they prevent **records and accountability errors**. DoD SAP marking examples refer to document-control information for SAP documents requiring formal accountability. That matters because SAP offices often need to know not only what information exists, but which copy, medium or attachment has been created, transferred, destroyed, reviewed or retained.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/DD/issuances/dodm/520507-V4p.PDF" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[WHS ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">520507 V4p</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">WHS ESDDoDM 5205.07, Volume 4, &quot;Special Access Program (SAP) Security Manual: Marking,&quot; October 10, 2013; Incorporating Change 2 on Septe...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 10, 2013</span></span></span>

The broader federal marking rule is that markings must leave no doubt about classified status, level of protection and duration of classification. In a SAP setting, the same principle becomes more demanding: the marking must also make the extra access channel unmistakable.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-32/subtitle-B/chapter-XX/part-2001" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ecfr.gov">[eCFR]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ecfr.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">32 CFR Part 2001 -- Classified National Security Information...</span></span></span>

## What banners do not tell you

A SAP banner is powerful, but it is not a complete explanation of the programme. It does not prove that the reader has access. It does not reveal the full purpose of the SAP. It does not replace the security classification guide. It does not show all access approvals, billet lists, system accreditations, courier rules, storage requirements or oversight channels attached to the programme.

Nor does a SAP marking necessarily mean the entire subject is exotic or operationally dramatic. SAPs can protect weapons work, intelligence-sensitive activity, technical systems, [acquisition]({{ 'acquisition/' | relative_url }}) details, operational methods or other information whose exposure would be unusually damaging. The marking’s job is not to explain why the programme exists; it is to make sure the information is handled under the programme’s authorised rules.

The Intelligence Community markings register also shows why public examples are necessarily limited. It states that SAP programmes and compartments are unpublished, even while the syntax rules for SAP markings can be described publicly. That creates a useful boundary for public understanding: the public can know the grammar of the label without knowing the real programme vocabulary behind many labels.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cdse.edu/Portals/124/Documents/jobaids/information/IC-Markings-System-Register.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cdse.edu">[CDSE]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cdse.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">IC Markings System Register</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">IC Markings System Register</span></span></span>

## The reader’s takeaway

The banner on a SAP document is a compact access-control instruction. A line such as **TOP SECRET//SAR-PROGRAM NAME//WAIVED** is not merely saying “very secret”. It is saying: protect at the Top Secret level, handle through the named special-access channel, and apply the additional dissemination restriction. A portion marking such as **(TS//SAR-ABC)** then applies that logic to a specific paragraph, table, chart or attachment.

That is why SAP markings are central to special-access-program implementation. The legal authority for SAPs sits in executive orders, statutes and agency policy, but the everyday control mechanism is the mark on the page, slide, file, media label or system display. It is the point where abstract secrecy rules become a readable instruction for the person holding the document.
