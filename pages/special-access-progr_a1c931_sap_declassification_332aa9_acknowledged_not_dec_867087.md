## Introduction

A special access programme (SAP) does not become fully public simply because the government acknowledges that it exists. In the United States, an **acknowledged SAP** is one whose existence—and sometimes its broad purpose—may be disclosed, while the sensitive information that justified special protection can remain classified for years or even decades. By contrast, a **fully declassified programme** is one in which the information itself has been formally released from classification controls, subject only to any remaining legal restrictions unrelated to national security. This distinction is central to understanding how SAPs transition from extraordinary secrecy towards greater transparency without automatically exposing operational capabilities, [intelligence]({{ 'intelligence/' | relative_url }}) methods or technical details.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cdse.edu/Portals/124/Documents/student-guides/SA001-guide.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cdse.edu">[cdse.edu]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cdse.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">An unacknowledged SAP is one whose existence and purpose are protected. ## What acknowledgement changes

Public acknowledgement changes the government's position on one specific question: whether it can officially confirm that the programme exists.

For an acknowledged SAP, officials may be authorised to confirm the programme's existence, identify its sponsoring organisation, or describe its general mission. Budget information may also become more visible than it would be for an unacknowledged programme. However, acknowledgement does not remove the programme from the national security classification system. Instead, it changes the level of secrecy surrounding the programme while leaving many underlying facts protected.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cdse.edu/Portals/124/Documents/student-guides/SA001-guide.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cdse.edu">[CDSE]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cdse.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">An unacknowledged SAP is one whose existence and purpose are protected. This distinction is reflected in Department of Defense training and policy. ## What can still remain protected

Acknowledgement removes secrecy about existence, not necessarily secrecy about content.

Even after a SAP becomes publicly known, agencies may continue to classify information whose disclosure could reasonably be expected to damage national security under the standards established by Executive Order 13526. The order requires information to be declassified when protection is no longer justified, but it does not require agencies to release information merely because the programme itself has been acknowledged.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://obamawhitehouse.archives.gov/the-press-office/executive-order-classified-national-security-information" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[whitehouse.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">executive order classified national security information</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Order 13526- Classified National Security Information29 Dec 2009 — This order prescribes a uniform system for classifying, safeguarding,...</span></span></span>

Information commonly remaining classified includes:

* detailed system performance;
* signatures and survivability characteristics;
* intelligence sources and collection methods;
* operational tactics and employment concepts;
* electronic warfare capabilities;
* vulnerabilities and countermeasures;
* design data, manufacturing processes and software;
* relationships with other classified [compartments]({{ 'compartments/' | relative_url }}) or intelligence activities.

Because classification decisions apply to individual pieces of information rather than to programme names alone, different elements of the same SAP can move through declassification at different speeds. An aircraft's existence may be public while its radar cross-section, mission software or sensor performance remain classified.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://obamawhitehouse.archives.gov/the-press-office/executive-order-classified-national-security-information" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[whitehouse.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">executive order classified national security information</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Order 13526- Classified National Security Information29 Dec 2009 — This order prescribes a uniform system for classifying, safeguarding,...</span></span></span>

## Why acknowledgement is not the same as declassification

The two processes serve different legal and administrative purposes.

Acknowledgement concerns whether the government officially recognises a programme's existence. Declassification concerns whether information continues to meet the legal standards for national security classification.

This means several combinations are possible:

This staged approach reflects how Executive Order 13526 treats classification as information-specific rather than programme-specific. ## Why former participants still need authorisation

One of the most common misconceptions is that personnel may freely discuss a programme once it becomes publicly acknowledged.

In reality, security obligations attach to the classified information itself rather than to the programme's public status. Individuals who worked within an acknowledged SAP remain bound by security agreements, non-disclosure obligations and classification guidance. They may discuss only information that has been specifically authorised for public release.

Department of Defense SAP training emphasises this distinction. Personnel may, in some circumstances, acknowledge that they worked on an acknowledged SAP, but they are not authorised to disclose classified technologies, operational methods or protected technical details without appropriate approval. Security officers remain responsible for advising personnel about what may and may not be disclosed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cdse.edu/Portals/124/Documents/student-guides/SA002-guide.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cdse.edu">[CDSE]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cdse.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Special Access Program Security Annual Refresher</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">As with acknowledged. The public acknowledgement of a programme does not automatically constitute declassification of the knowledge they acquired while holding authorised access.

## How programmes move towards full declassification

Acknowledgement often represents an intermediate stage rather than the final destination.

As programmes age, technologies become obsolete, or strategic circumstances change, agencies may gradually release additional information through formal declassification reviews, historical publications, museum transfers or mandatory declassification processes. Each release is evaluated independently against continuing national security risks rather than by reference to whether the programme has already been publicly acknowledged. Executive Order 13526 also requires periodic review of classified information and establishes mechanisms for mandatory declassification review and automatic declassification, subject to recognised exemptions for particularly sensitive information.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://obamawhitehouse.archives.gov/the-press-office/executive-order-classified-national-security-information" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[whitehouse.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">executive order classified national security information</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Order 13526- Classified National Security Information29 Dec 2009 — This order prescribes a uniform system for classifying, safeguarding,...</span></span></span>

2. Publicly acknowledged while remaining heavily classified.
3. Removed from SAP handling but still protected under ordinary classification rules.
4. Progressively declassified over time.
