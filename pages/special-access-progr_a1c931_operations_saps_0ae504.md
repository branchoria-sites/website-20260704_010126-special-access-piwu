## Introduction

Operations and Support special access programmes, often shortened to OS-SAPs, are the branch of the US special-access-programme system used when the sensitive thing is not mainly a new weapon design or an [intelligence]({{ 'intelligence/' | relative_url }}) collection source, but a military activity: its planning, execution, supporting arrangements, operational concepts, participating organisations or mission-related infrastructure. In plain terms, this is how a mission plan itself can become a compartment.



<img src="{{ "/assets/images/special-access-progr_a1c931_operations_saps_0ae504-overview.webp" | relative_url }}" alt="Overview image for Operations" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">

That matters because a future operation can be compromised long before it begins. An adversary does not need the whole plan if scattered clues reveal timing, force movements, logistics bottlenecks, communications nodes, target selection or third-country support. US defence doctrine calls these “OPSEC indicators”: friendly actions and open-source information that can be pieced together to infer intentions, capabilities or activities.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://media.defense.gov/2020/Oct/28/2002524944/-1/-1/0/JP%203-13.3-OPSEC.PDF" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: media.defense.gov">[U.S. Department of War]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">media.defense.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">U.S. Department of War JP 3-13.3, Operations Security</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">U.S. Department of War JP 3-13.3, Operations Security</span></span></span>(https://media.defense.gov/2020/Oct/28/2002524944/-1/-1/0/JP%203-13.3-OPSEC.PDF)

OS-SAPs therefore sit at the operational end of the SAP world. They are not simply “more classified files”. They are access regimes built around the practical problem of allowing planners, commanders, logisticians, contractors and support personnel to prepare an especially sensitive mission without exposing the pattern of action that would make the mission predictable.

## What makes an Operations SAP different

The most concise official definition is that an Operations and Support SAP is established primarily to protect “the planning for, execution of, and support to especially sensitive military operations”. A DoD security lexicon adds that such a programme may protect organisations, property, operational concepts, plans and activities.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/FOID/Reading%20Room/Administration_and_Management/16-F-0890_DoD_Security_Lexicon.pdf?ver=2017-05-15-140009-097" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[WHS ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on whs.mil.</span></span></span>

That definition is narrower than many popular uses of “black programme”. An [acquisition]({{ 'acquisition/' | relative_url }}) SAP may protect the development or procurement of a system. An intelligence SAP may protect sensitive intelligence or counter-intelligence operations. An OS-SAP protects the mission environment in which forces may actually prepare, move, use capabilities, co-ordinate with partners or execute a plan. CDSE training material puts the category under the Office of the Under Secretary of Defense for Policy, reflecting its connection to planning, contingency operations and sensitive military activity rather than ordinary procurement management.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cdse.edu/Portals/124/Documents/student-guides/SA001-guide.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cdse.edu">[CDSE]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cdse.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">SA001 Student Guide</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">SA001 Student Guide</span></span></span>

The category is also a reminder that secrecy has phases. A capability can begin life in an acquisition SAP, be discussed with planners only later, and then require operational [compartments]({{ 'compartments/' | relative_url }}) when it becomes relevant to combatant-command planning. The 2024 [DoD SAP policy]({{ 'do-d-policy/' | relative_url }}) directs the Under Secretary of Defense for Policy to ensure SAPs are integrated into national security strategy, defence plan development and contingency operations through the Integrated Joint Special Technical Operations process. It also refers to operational compartments and sub-compartments for SAP capabilities once they become relevant to combatant-command planners.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/portals/54/documents/dd/issuances/dodd/520507p.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[WHS ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on whs.mil.</span></span></span>

In practice, this means OS-SAPs are about controlled disclosure at the point where a protected capability, operational concept or support arrangement has to leave the laboratory or staff vault and enter real planning. The security problem changes from “who may know this exists?” to “who must know enough to move, sustain, authorise or employ it without revealing the mission?”

## Planning and execution secrecy

Mission planning creates sensitive information even when no single component is exotic. A route, date, aircraft package, communications plan, staging base, reserve force or refuelling pattern can become highly revealing when combined. Joint operations-security doctrine explains the logic: adversaries collect friendly detectable actions and open-source information, compare them with models of how a force prepares and operates, and gradually infer future capabilities or courses of action.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://media.defense.gov/2020/Oct/28/2002524944/-1/-1/0/JP%203-13.3-OPSEC.PDF" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: media.defense.gov">[U.S. Department of War]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">media.defense.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">U.S. Department of War JP 3-13.3, Operations Security</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">U.S. Department of War JP 3-13.3, Operations Security</span></span></span>(https://media.defense.gov/2020/Oct/28/2002524944/-1/-1/0/JP%203-13.3-OPSEC.PDF)

An OS-SAP is one way to reduce that exposure when ordinary classification channels are considered too broad. It can restrict who sees the operational concept, who knows the protected capability is available, which planners are read into the compartment, and how related material is stored, transmitted and discussed. The point is not to make planning mystical. It is to prevent a large staff process from turning a sensitive mission into an observable pattern.

This is especially important because military planning is collaborative by nature. A real plan may touch airspace, logistics, legal review, communications, [cyber]({{ 'cyber/' | relative_url }}) support, medical support, transport, host-nation access, contractor services and senior authorisation. Each function may need a different slice of the picture. SAP governance recognises this problem by using compartments, access approvals and central records of personnel and facilities. The 2024 DoD SAP policy says DoD SAPCO maintains authoritative repositories for SAP personnel access and SAP facility [accreditation]({{ 'accreditation/' | relative_url }}), and it requires co-ordination before exposing SAP information outside DoD.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/portals/54/documents/dd/issuances/dodd/520507p.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[WHS ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on whs.mil.</span></span></span>

The operational tension is clear: if too many people are excluded, the mission may be poorly integrated; if too many are included, the mission’s pattern may leak. OS-SAP mechanisms try to create a controlled middle ground, where support functions can be performed without broadcasting the whole operational story.

## Support networks and logistics

The “support” in Operations and Support SAPs is not an afterthought. Logistics is often the most visible part of a future operation. Moving personnel and materiel, maintaining equipment, arranging basing, securing communications and sustaining a deployment all produce detectable traces. RAND’s military-logistics work describes logistics as the transfer of personnel and materiel and the maintenance needed to support an ongoing deployment or respond to emergent threats.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.rand.org/topics/military-logistics.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: rand.org">[RAND Corporation]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">rand.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Corporation Military Logistics &#124; RAND</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Corporation Military Logistics &#124; RAND</span></span></span>

That support layer is exactly where sensitive mission planning can become exposed. A ship movement, airlift pattern, unusual fuel demand, contractor mobilisation, specialist maintenance visit or secure-facility build-out may reveal more than a document [title]({{ 'statute/' | relative_url }}). Joint OPSEC examples list logistic capabilities and constraints, lines of communication, host-nation support, third-nation support, critical communication nodes and exercise or operation plans as types of information adversaries may value.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://jfsc.ndu.edu/Portals/72/Documents/JC2IOS/Additional_Reading/1C2_JP_3-13-3_OPSEC_Process.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: jfsc.ndu.edu">[jfsc.ndu.edu]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">jfsc.ndu.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">J P 3-13.3, Operations Security</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">J P 3-13.3, Operations Security</span></span></span>

OS-SAP controls therefore often have to reach beyond the people writing the concept of operations. They can involve separate information systems, SAP-accredited facilities, cleared contractor channels, limited-access planning cells and special handling rules for support documentation. Defence policy also recognises that audits, technical security countermeasures, polygraph examinations and joint inspections may need SAP-specific processes so people can perform oversight or support duties while limiting the need for detailed briefings.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/portals/54/documents/dd/issuances/dodd/520507p.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[WHS ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on whs.mil.</span></span></span>

This is one reason Operations SAPs are hard to discuss publicly. Their value is often not in protecting a spectacular secret by itself, but in protecting the connection between many ordinary-looking facts. A logistics contract, a flight plan and a secure communications requirement may each look incomplete in isolation. Together, they may reveal where a force is going, when it will arrive, what it intends to do and what it depends on.

## Operational clues adversaries value

The open-source age has made the OS-SAP problem more vivid. Publicly available data can reveal patterns that older security systems did not anticipate. In 2018, the Strava heat-map incident showed how aggregated fitness-tracking data could expose the location and routines of personnel at military and intelligence-linked sites; the risk was not that a user posted a war plan, but that repeated personal movement created a pattern useful for operational security analysis.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theguardian.com/world/2018/jan/28/fitness-tracking-app-gives-away-location-of-secret-us-army-bases" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theguardian.com">[The Guardian]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theguardian.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on theguardian.com.</span></span></span>

Flight tracking creates a similar problem. National Defense reported in 2023 that sites aggregating ADS-B and related flight data can show aircraft identity, altitude, speed, heading and origin-destination information, and quoted an Air Force statement describing open-source flight tracking and aggregation of military aircraft as a direct threat to military air operations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nationaldefensemagazine.org/articles/2023/2/6/open-source-flight-tracking-called-threat-to-military-aircraft" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationaldefensemagazine.org">[National Defense Magazine]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationaldefensemagazine.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on nationaldefensemagazine.org.</span></span></span>

These examples help explain why mission planning may require special-access treatment even when the underlying clues are not all classified. An adversary may be looking for:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--decision" markdown="1">

* **Timing:** changes in alert posture, leave patterns, unusual training cycles or late-night activity.
* **Force composition:** which units, aircraft, ships, specialists or contractors are involved.
* **Routes and staging:** airfields, ports, refuelling points, transit corridors or temporary facilities.
* **Support dependencies:** logistics constraints, maintenance needs, host-nation support and communications nodes.
* **Intent:** target selection, reserve forces, rules for employment or signs that a contingency plan has shifted from paper to preparation.

</div>

Joint OPSEC doctrine is explicit that critical information may be classified or unclassified. What makes it dangerous is whether an adversary needs it to plan and act effectively against the mission.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://media.defense.gov/2020/Oct/28/2002524944/-1/-1/0/JP%203-13.3-OPSEC.PDF" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: media.defense.gov">[U.S. Department of War]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">media.defense.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">U.S. Department of War JP 3-13.3, Operations Security</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">U.S. Department of War JP 3-13.3, Operations Security</span></span></span>(https://media.defense.gov/2020/Oct/28/2002524944/-1/-1/0/JP%203-13.3-OPSEC.PDF)

## How compartmentation changes the planning process

An OS-SAP does not remove normal command responsibility, nor does it mean every participant sees everything. Its mechanism is compartmented need-to-know. A planner may know a protected capability is available for a contingency but not all technical details. A support officer may know a movement requirement without knowing the full target set. A contractor may support a secure facility or communications path without being read into the complete operation.

The 2024 DoD SAP policy points to this layered approach. It assigns DoD SAPCO responsibility for classification guides, personnel-access repositories and facility accreditation, while assigning the Under Secretary of Defense for Policy responsibility for integrating SAPs into plan development and contingency operations. It also directs that SAP-protected capabilities and information be approved for inclusion when relevant to combatant-command planners, and that deployment and execution authorities be developed for sensitive, scarce or contingency-specific capabilities.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/portals/54/documents/dd/issuances/dodd/520507p.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[WHS ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on whs.mil.</span></span></span>

This is where OS-SAPs differ from simple document secrecy. The programme has to manage operational readiness. A protected capability that cannot be planned for in time is strategically less useful; a protected capability planned too broadly may be compromised. OS-SAP governance tries to decide who gets [read in]({{ 'read-in/' | relative_url }}) early enough for the mission to work, while keeping the access list small enough that the mission remains hard to infer.

There is also an oversight dimension. Under 10 U.S.C. §119, the Secretary of Defense must report annually to the defence committees on DoD special access programmes, and a SAP generally may not be initiated until the defence committees are notified and 30 days have elapsed. The statute also allows case-by-case waivers for certain report contents if inclusion would adversely affect national security, with information and justification provided to the chair and ranking minority member of each defence committee.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/10/119" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: govinfo.gov">[GovInfo]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">govinfo.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on govinfo.gov.</span></span></span>

## Why waived and unacknowledged status matters for operations

Some OS-SAPs may be acknowledged, but the most sensitive mission-planning cases are naturally candidates for unacknowledged treatment, because acknowledging the programme may reveal that a particular operational capability, planning cell or contingency exists. Waived status is still narrower: it is tied to restricted congressional reporting rather than a general absence of oversight.

For operations, the reason is practical. If a future mission depends on surprise, access, deception, scarce capabilities or delicate partner arrangements, even the existence of a compartment may be revealing. An adversary does not need the access list; knowing that a named region, platform, unit or support arrangement has been placed in a special compartment might be enough to redirect collection.

At the same time, the public rules do not describe OS-SAPs as free-standing secret wars. They sit inside a formal DoD SAP structure involving component SAPCOs, OSD-level SAP central offices, the SAP Oversight Committee and congressional notification. CDSE training material says the Deputy Secretary of Defense approves every DoD SAP, that governance relies on the SAPOC, Senior Review Group and Senior SAP Working Group, and that Congress must be notified once a SAP is approved.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cdse.edu/Portals/124/Documents/student-guides/SA001-guide.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cdse.edu">[CDSE]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cdse.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">SA001 Student Guide</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">SA001 Student Guide</span></span></span>

The controversy is not that operational secrecy exists. Most readers accept that some mission plans cannot be public in real time. The harder question is how to preserve enough internal and congressional visibility to prevent duplication, waste, unlawful action or operational stovepipes while still protecting plans whose value depends on concealment.

## The central tradeoff

Operations SAPs exist because the act of preparing for a mission can expose the mission. They protect the bridge between secret capability and real-world execution: the plans, support networks, movements, facilities, access arrangements and operational concepts that allow a sensitive activity to happen.

Their strongest justification is also their greatest risk. Compartmentation can preserve surprise, protect personnel and deny adversaries the clues they need. But excessive compartmentation can make co-ordination harder, limit challenge inside government and isolate planners from people who need enough context to spot operational, legal or logistical problems. OPSEC doctrine itself recognises the need for practical, timely and effective programmes rather than secrecy for its own sake.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://media.defense.gov/2020/Oct/28/2002524944/-1/-1/0/JP%203-13.3-OPSEC.PDF" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: media.defense.gov">[U.S. Department of War]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">media.defense.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">U.S. Department of War JP 3-13.3, Operations Security</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">U.S. Department of War JP 3-13.3, Operations Security</span></span></span>(https://media.defense.gov/2020/Oct/28/2002524944/-1/-1/0/JP%203-13.3-OPSEC.PDF)

The most accurate way to understand OS-SAPs is therefore not as a mysterious label, but as a mission-protection mechanism. They are designed for the point where future action, support arrangements and observable preparation become the sensitive thing.

    var template = String(cfg.url_template || 'https://www.ebay.com/sch/i.html?_nkw={query}');
    var url = template.replace('{query}', encodeQuery(query || '')).replace('{query_plain}', String(query || ''));
    var joiner = url.indexOf('?') >= 0 ? '&' : '?';
    var params = [];
    if (cfg.mkrid) params.push('mkrid=' + encodeURIComponent(cfg.mkrid));
    if (cfg.campaign_id) params.push('campid=' + encodeURIComponent(cfg.campaign_id));
    params.push('mkevt=1');
    params.push('mkcid=1');
    params.push('toolid=' + encodeURIComponent(cfg.tool_id || '10001'));
    if (reference) params.push('customid=' + encodeURIComponent(reference).slice(0, 256));
    return url + joiner + params.join('&');
  }
  function domainLabel(marketId) {
    var cfg = markets[marketId] || markets[defaultMarket] || {};
    return cfg.domain_label || 'eBay';
  }
  function flagClass(marketId) {
    return 'fr-ebay-market-flag fr-ebay-market-flag--' + String(marketId || '').toLowerCase().replace(/_/g, '-').replace(/[^a-z0-9-]+/g, '');
  }
  function closeMenu(section) {
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var picker = section.querySelector('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
    if (menu) menu.hidden = true;
    if (trigger) trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', 'false');
    if (picker) picker.classList.remove('fr-ebay-market-picker--open');
  }
  function availableMarkets(section) {
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      return Array.prototype.slice.call(select.options)
        .map(function (option) { return option.value; })
        .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
    }
    return Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]'))
      .map(function (button) { return button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'); })
      .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
  }
  function applyMarket(section, marketId, persist) {
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    if (available.indexOf(marketId)< 0) marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-localized-link]')).forEach(function (link) {
      var query = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-query') || '';
      var reference = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-reference') || '';
      link.setAttribute('href', buildUrl(query, reference, marketId));
    });
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select && available.indexOf(marketId) >= 0) select.value = marketId;
    var selectedText = marketId;
    if (select && select.selectedIndex >= 0) selectedText = select.options[select.selectedIndex].text;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-flag], [data-ebay-trigger-market-flag]')).forEach(function (flag) {
      flag.className = flagClass(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-trigger-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-domain-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = domainLabel(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.setAttribute('aria-selected', button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option') === marketId ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;
      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
})();
