## Introduction

The “eight-member oversight model” is the shorthand often used for the narrowest public version of congressional oversight for waived Department of Defense special-access programmes. It does not mean a programme has no legal reporting route. It means the Secretary of Defense may, case by case, withhold certain normally required SAP report information from the wider defence committees when including it would harm national security, while providing the information and waiver justification to the chair and ranking minority member of each relevant congressional defence committee. In common descriptions, that becomes eight senior lawmakers: the majority and minority leaders of the key House and Senate defence oversight channels.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/10/119" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: law.cornell.edu">[Legal Information Institute]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">law.cornell.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Legal Information Institute10 U.S. Code § 119</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Legal Information Institute10 U.S. Code § 119</span></span></span>



<img src="{{ "/assets/images/special-access-progr_a1c931_eight_member_model_2fdfba-overview.webp" | relative_url }}" alt="Overview image for Eight Members" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">

The model matters because it shows the central tension in waived and [unacknowledged SAP]({{ 'unacknowledged-7fdfef/' | relative_url }}) oversight. Congress is not formally bypassed, but the number of people expected to know enough to challenge cost, legality, duplication or misuse can become very small. That makes the system defensible as a secrecy tool, yet vulnerable as an [accountability]({{ 'accountability/' | relative_url }}) design.

## What the eight-member model actually does

The legal core is 10 U.S.C. § 119, the [statute]({{ 'statute/' | relative_url }}) governing congressional oversight of Department of Defense special-access programmes. The normal rule requires annual reporting on DoD SAPs, including budget totals and programme-level information; new SAPs must also be reported with notice, justification, cost estimate and identification of similar programmes or technologies. The waived-SAP mechanism sits inside that reporting system, rather than outside it.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/10/119" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: law.cornell.edu">[Legal Information Institute]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">law.cornell.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Legal Information Institute10 U.S. Code § 119</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Legal Information Institute10 U.S. Code § 119</span></span></span>

The key waiver provision allows the Secretary of Defense to omit certain information from the wider report if the Secretary determines that including it would adversely affect national security. The statute then requires the omitted information, plus the justification for the waiver, to be provided jointly to the chair and ranking minority member of each defence committee.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/10/119" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: law.cornell.edu">[Legal Information Institute]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">law.cornell.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Legal Information Institute10 U.S. Code § 119</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Legal Information Institute10 U.S. Code § 119</span></span></span>

That is why the model is often described as “eight-member” oversight. The Federation of American Scientists has summarised waived SAPs as the most sensitive SAP category, exempt from normal congressional notification requirements, where “only eight senior members of the congressional defense committees may be advised of the program”.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://fas.org/publication/dod_saps/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: fas.org">[Federation of American Scientists]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">fas.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">dod saps</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">dod saps</span></span></span> The older Moynihan Commission report used similar architecture language, distinguishing acknowledged programmes from unacknowledged “black” programmes and then describing waived programmes as especially sensitive black programmes exempt from standard congressional reporting.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://sgp.fas.org/library/moynihan/chap2.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sgp.fas.org">[sgp.fas.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sgp.fas.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Report of the Commission on Protecting and Reducing Government Secrecy</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Report of the Commission on Protecting and Reducing Government Secrecy</span></span></span>

The practical result is not a magical exemption from [Congress]({{ 'congress/' | relative_url }}). It is a funnel. Ordinary SAP reporting spreads information across the defence committee system; waived reporting can concentrate the most sensitive facts at the very top of that system.

## Which leaders are usually described

The “eight” usually refers to the chair and ranking minority member on the main House and Senate defence oversight channels. In plain terms, these are the senior majority and minority lawmakers who lead the committees responsible for authorising and funding defence activity. Public shorthand often names the House Armed Services Committee, Senate Armed Services Committee, House Appropriations defence channel and Senate Appropriations defence channel, although exact statutory phrasing and committee practice can be more technical than the shorthand suggests.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/10/119" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: law.cornell.edu">[Legal Information Institute]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">law.cornell.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Legal Information Institute10 U.S. Code § 119</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Legal Information Institute10 U.S. Code § 119</span></span></span>

The point is less the arithmetic than the design principle. The executive branch is still reporting to both parties and both chambers, but it is not necessarily distributing the same information to every member who would normally help scrutinise defence programmes. In the best case, this puts enough constitutional friction into the system while protecting an extremely fragile secret. In the worst case, it leaves oversight dependent on a handful of busy senior legislators who may not have the time, [staff access]({{ 'staff-access/' | relative_url }}) or technical depth to test what they are told.

The model also differs from ordinary committee oversight because it can suppress the usual ecology of congressional scrutiny. Large defence programmes are normally examined through hearings, staff review, budget marks, member questions, competing jurisdictional interests and comparison with other programmes. [Waived SAP reporting]({{ 'waived-oversight/' | relative_url }}) narrows those channels. The oversight does not disappear, but many of the routine ways Congress detects problems become harder to use.

## Why staff access may differ

A common misunderstanding is that “eight members” always means exactly eight human beings know everything and no staff can ever assist. The statute itself speaks about providing the information and waiver justification to the chairs and ranking minority members; it does not, in that subsection, spell out a full staff-access architecture.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/10/119" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: law.cornell.edu">[Legal Information Institute]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">law.cornell.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Legal Information Institute10 U.S. Code § 119</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Legal Information Institute10 U.S. Code § 119</span></span></span>

In practice, staff access appears to depend on committee rules, executive-branch approval and programme-specific access decisions. Publicly available security guidance makes a clear distinction between personal office staff and committee staff: the Project On Government Oversight notes that personal office staff are not eligible for SCI or SAP access merely because they work for a member, while committee staff may receive SCI eligibility and SAP access as determined by the committee chair and ranking member in coordination with the Intelligence Community.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.pogo.org/reports/a-primer-on-congressional-staff-clearances" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pogo.org">[pogo.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pogo.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">A Primer on Congressional Staff Clearances &#124; Project On Government Oversight</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">A Primer on Congressional Staff Clearances &#124; Project On Government Oversight</span></span></span>

DoD policy also centralises congressional SAP contact. The 2024 [DoD SAP policy]({{ 'do-d-policy/' | relative_url }}) says personnel with DoD SAP access must notify the Director of the DoD Special Access Program Central Office when briefing or providing SAP material to members of Congress or staff, and it bars contractors from providing SAP material to members or staff without that Director’s approval.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/portals/54/documents/dd/issuances/dodd/520507p.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[WHS ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on whs.mil.</span></span></span> Older Air Force policy stated that, unless approved by the Secretary or Deputy Secretary of Defense, access to waived SAPs was limited to the chair, ranking minority member and staff directors of the relevant defence and intelligence committees.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://static.e-publishing.af.mil/production/1/saf_aa/publication/afpd16-7/afpd16-7.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: static.e-publishing.af.mil">[E-Publishing]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">static.e-publishing.af.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">pd16 7</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">pd16 7</span></span></span>

This is the crucial nuance: the “eight-member” model describes the required senior legislative notification channel, not necessarily every person who may ever be read into a related compartment. Staff directors or selected committee staff may be involved, but their access is not the same as broad committee access. That matters because staff are often the people with the time and technical continuity to compare budgets, track schedule slips, notice duplication and preserve institutional memory across election cycles.

## Why the model can be legal and still risky

The strongest argument for the eight-member model is that some secrets are genuinely fragile. The whole SAP framework exists because ordinary classification may not be enough for especially sensitive capabilities, sources, methods or acquisition efforts. Current DoD policy says access to SAPs must be strictly limited to the minimum number of personnel necessary and based on valid need to know, clearance, personnel-security prerequisites and contribution to execution or oversight.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/portals/54/documents/dd/issuances/dodd/520507p.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[WHS ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on whs.mil.</span></span></span>

The same 2024 DoD instruction says the Secretary of Defense may designate selected unacknowledged SAPs as waived when standard reporting to Congress would adversely affect national security, but also states that waived SAPs remain subject to congressional oversight.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/DD/issuances/dodi/520511p.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[WHS ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on whs.mil.</span></span></span> This is the official answer to the claim that waived SAPs are inherently lawless: the waiver changes the reporting route, not the existence of legal accountability.

The critique is that accountability becomes highly concentrated. GAO’s historical work on SAPs shows why that is not a theoretical concern. A 1992 GAO report found that DoD had properly established the sampled SAPs but had provided less oversight over them than similarly sized non-SAP acquisition programmes; it also noted that waivers and exemptions had limited review, documentation and reporting requirements, and that previous audits had found weak internal controls, poor internal communication and inadequate record-keeping.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gao.gov/assets/nsiad-93-78.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.gov">[GAO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on gao.gov.</span></span></span>

GAO also found that congressional oversight had increased after earlier concerns, including formal reporting requirements and pressure for DoD to provide information on all SAPs to the defence committees.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gao.gov/assets/nsiad-93-78.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.gov">[GAO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on gao.gov.</span></span></span> That history is important because it shows the eight-member model as a retreat from broad committee visibility only in a narrow class of cases, not as the baseline for SAP oversight. The risk is that the exception, once normalised, can weaken the very reforms that were meant to prevent black programmes from escaping budgetary and institutional scrutiny.

## The accountability tradeoff

The eight-member model is best understood as a controlled bottleneck. Its benefit is obvious: fewer people briefed means fewer opportunities for accidental disclosure, political signalling, press leakage or adversary inference. For a genuinely sensitive unacknowledged programme, even confirming that a line item, platform, operation or technology exists may reveal too much.

The cost is just as clear. Concentrated oversight can make Congress more dependent on executive-branch framing. A small group of senior legislators may receive the legally required information but still struggle to test it without trusted staff, comparative programme data, independent audit access or the ability to discuss concerns with other cleared colleagues. Oversight becomes more personal and less institutional.

The model therefore creates three accountability pressures:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--comparison" markdown="1">

* **Capacity pressure:** senior members have many responsibilities, while SAP review can require sustained technical, legal and budgetary attention.
* **Continuity pressure:** committee staff often preserve knowledge across Congresses; limited staff access can reduce the memory needed to spot recurring problems.
* **Challenge pressure:** secrecy can make it harder to bring in rival expertise, compare similar programmes or test whether the same mission is being funded elsewhere.

</div>

None of these pressures proves abuse. They show why waived SAPs can be lawful yet democratically uncomfortable. The model protects secrets by shrinking the circle of knowledge, but the smaller the circle becomes, the more the system depends on the judgement, independence and persistence of a very small number of overseers.

## What the model does not prove

The eight-member model should not be read as evidence that waived SAPs are automatically illegal, privately controlled or outside government. Public statutes and DoD policy point the other way: waived SAPs are recognised within a formal framework, and the waiver must be justified case by case.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/10/119" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: law.cornell.edu">[Legal Information Institute]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">law.cornell.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Legal Information Institute10 U.S. Code § 119</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Legal Information Institute10 U.S. Code § 119</span></span></span>

It also should not be treated as proof that all details are shared with only eight people forever. Access can vary by programme, committee role, staff authorisation and internal DoD governance. DoD’s own SAP governance structure is broader inside the executive branch, with the SAP Oversight Committee, Senior Review Group and Senior SAP Working Group involved in management and oversight of DoD SAPs.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/portals/54/documents/dd/issuances/dodd/520507p.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[WHS ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on whs.mil.</span></span></span>

The narrower claim is stronger and more useful: for waived SAPs, the law allows the executive branch to compress normal congressional reporting into a very small senior-member channel when broader reporting would harm national security. That is the mechanism that makes waived SAP oversight distinctive. It is also the reason the model remains controversial. It preserves a legal line to Congress, but it does so by weakening many of the ordinary institutional habits through which Congress turns information into real scrutiny.

    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
})();
</script>
