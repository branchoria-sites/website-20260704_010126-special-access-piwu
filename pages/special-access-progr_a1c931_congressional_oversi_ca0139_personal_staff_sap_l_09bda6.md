## Introduction

A Member of [Congress]({{ 'congress/' | relative_url }}) who is not part of the formal defence oversight structure cannot simply ask a personal aide to review Department of Defense (DoD) Special Access Program (SAP) files. The barrier is not merely a security clearance. Access to DoD SAPs depends on a combination of statutory oversight rules, formal committee jurisdiction, programme-specific access approval, and the long-standing "need-to-know" principle. Even staff who already hold high-level clearances are not automatically entitled to enter a SAP compartment.

This distinction is central to congressional oversight. Congress has legal authority to oversee DoD SAPs, including highly sensitive programmes, but that authority is channelled primarily through the House and Senate Armed Services and Appropriations Committees and a limited number of designated professional [committee staff]({{ 'staff-access/' | relative_url }}). Personal office staff serve individual members rather than committees, so they generally fall outside the access framework established by DoD policy and congressional security procedures.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&amp;num=0&amp;req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title10-section119" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uscode.house.gov">[house.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uscode.house.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Code10 USC 119: Special access programs: congressional...(a)(1) Not later than March 1 of each year, the Secretary of Defense shall subm...</span></span></span>

## What DoD policy says about personal office access

The clearest rule appears in DoD policy governing congressional access to SAPs. DoD Directive 5205.07 states unambiguously: **"Personal staff shall not be granted access to DoD SAPs."** The directive distinguishes between personal office employees and professional committee staff, who may receive access under tightly controlled conditions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.dami.army.pentagon.mil/site/sci/docs/DoD%205205_07.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dami.army.pentagon.mil">[dami.army.pentagon.mil]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dami.army.pentagon.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">DoD Directive 5205.07, July 1, 2010by DD SAPCO · 2010 — Only those professional staff members who meet SAP access eligibility requirement...</span></span></span>

The restriction reflects several layers of security:

* **A security clearance is necessary but not sufficient.** A person must also be formally nominated, vetted for the specific SAP, and determined to have a need to know for that particular compartment.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/DD/issuances/dodm/520507m1.PDF" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[WHS ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">b.Read more</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">WHS ESDDoDM 5205.07, &quot;Special Access Program Security Manual...January 21, 2025 — 17 Jan 2025 — Establishes, develops, and coordinates...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 21, 2025</span></span></span>
* **SAP access is programme-specific.** Being read into one SAP does not authorise access to another, even at the same classification level. DoD security manuals require separate access determinations and continuing oversight for each compartment.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/DD/issuances/dodm/520507m1.PDF" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[WHS ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">b.Read more</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">WHS ESDDoDM 5205.07, &quot;Special Access Program Security Manual...January 21, 2025 — 17 Jan 2025 — Establishes, develops, and coordinates...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 21, 2025</span></span></span>
* **Congressional handling requirements are specialised.** The DoD SAP Central Office (SAPCO) verifies that congressional offices authorised to receive SAP information maintain the required physical security, handling procedures and storage arrangements before SAP information is processed there.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/DD/issuances/dodm/520507m1.PDF" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[WHS ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">b.Read more</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">WHS ESDDoDM 5205.07, &quot;Special Access Program Security Manual...January 21, 2025 — 17 Jan 2025 — Establishes, develops, and coordinates...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 21, 2025</span></span></span>

These rules mean that an individual member's office cannot create its own SAP review capability simply by employing cleared staff.

## Why committee staff matter more than personal aides

Congressional oversight of SAPs is organised around committees rather than individual offices because the committees exercise the formal legislative powers to authorise, appropriate and oversee defence programmes.

DoD policy therefore provides a separate pathway for **professional committee staff**, not personal aides. For non-waived SAPs, committee staff directors determine which professional staff require access on behalf of committee leadership. Access is generally limited to a small number of majority and minority professional staff members, with additional personnel requiring specific justification. Every nominee must still satisfy SAP eligibility requirements, and each access request is coordinated through DoD SAPCO.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.dami.army.pentagon.mil/site/sci/docs/DoD%205205_07.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dami.army.pentagon.mil">[dami.army.pentagon.mil]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dami.army.pentagon.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">DoD Directive 5205.07, July 1, 2010by DD SAPCO · 2010 — Only those professional staff members who meet SAP access eligibility requirement...</span></span></span>

This structure serves several purposes:

* **Institutional continuity.** Professional committee staff often remain in place across multiple Congresses and develop expertise in classified defence programmes, whereas personal office staff may change frequently.
* **Jurisdictional discipline.** Committee staff support statutory oversight responsibilities that belong to the committee rather than to an individual member's political office.
* **Controlled dissemination.** Restricting access to a small number of designated staff reduces the number of people inside exceptionally sensitive [compartments]({{ 'compartments/' | relative_url }}) while still allowing Congress to perform its oversight role.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&amp;num=0&amp;req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title10-section119" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uscode.house.gov">[house.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uscode.house.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Code10 USC 119: Special access programs: congressional...(a)(1) Not later than March 1 of each year, the Secretary of Defense shall subm...</span></span></span>

For waived SAPs, the access rules become even narrower, with DoD policy limiting authorised congressional recipients principally to specified committee leaders and staff directors unless the Secretary or Deputy Secretary of Defense approves otherwise.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.dami.army.pentagon.mil/site/sci/docs/DoD%205205_07.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dami.army.pentagon.mil">[dami.army.pentagon.mil]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dami.army.pentagon.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">DoD Directive 5205.07, July 1, 2010by DD SAPCO · 2010 — Only those professional staff members who meet SAP access eligibility requirement...</span></span></span>

## Why a personal office cannot substitute for committee oversight

A common misconception is that a member with a sufficiently high clearance should be able to designate trusted personal employees to help analyse classified programmes. In the SAP system, that assumption does not hold.

Personal office staff work directly for an individual legislator. They do not exercise committee oversight authority, do not normally participate in committee-controlled classified review processes, and are not part of the limited cadre of professional staff identified by committee leadership for SAP access. Consequently, even if they possess a [Top Secret clearance]({{ 'clearance-gap/' | relative_url }}), they generally lack both the programme-specific authorisation and the institutional role required for SAP admission.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.dami.army.pentagon.mil/site/sci/docs/DoD%205205_07.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dami.army.pentagon.mil">[pentagon.mil]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dami.army.pentagon.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">DoD Directive 5205.07, July 1, 2010by DD SAPCO · 2010 — Only those professional staff members who meet SAP access eligibility requirement...</span></span></span>

The distinction mirrors the broader security principle used throughout the national security system: access follows an official function, not simply a person's clearance level.

## How this shapes oversight by non-committee members

The practical consequence is that members who sit outside the defence oversight committees face significant limits in independently examining DoD SAPs.

Rather than assigning personal aides to inspect programme files, non-committee members typically must rely on institutional channels such as:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--decision" markdown="1">

* classified briefings authorised under existing access rules;
* information provided through the appropriate defence committees;
* committee reports and classified annexes where applicable; and
* legislative engagement with committee leadership responsible for SAP oversight.

</div>

For the most sensitive programmes, particularly waived SAPs, these limitations are even more pronounced because Congress itself has accepted a statutory model in which detailed oversight is concentrated within a very small group of authorised committee leaders and staff. ## The practical effect of the staff limits

The prohibition on personal office access is not simply an administrative preference. It is one of the mechanisms that keeps SAP oversight compartmented while preserving a defined congressional review process.

In practice, this means that oversight depends less on whether an individual member wishes to investigate a programme and more on whether the member operates within the committee structure that Congress and the DoD have designated for SAP review.
