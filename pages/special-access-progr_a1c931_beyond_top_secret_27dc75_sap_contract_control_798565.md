## Introduction

A contract that involves a United States Special Access Program (SAP) does more than require the contractor to protect classified information at the appropriate classification level. It can impose programme-specific security obligations that go beyond the baseline rules of the National Industrial Security Program (NISP), including additional approvals, dedicated security procedures, tighter handling requirements, specialised oversight and stricter rules for retaining or disposing of programme material after the contract ends. These extra obligations exist because SAPs are intended for information requiring safeguards beyond ordinary classified handling, even when the underlying information is already classified as Secret or Top Secret.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.dcsa.mil/Industrial-Security/Special-Access-Programs-Templates/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dcsa.mil">[DCSA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dcsa.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Special Access Programs &amp; TemplatesThis web page has been established as a repository for SAP-specific processes, procedures, documen...</span></span></span>

For contractors, the practical effect is that holding the right facility clearance and employing cleared personnel is only the starting point. Performance on a SAP contract depends on complying with programme-specific instructions issued by the sponsoring government office, often through contract clauses, security guidance and formal programme documentation that supplements the ordinary classified-contract framework.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cdse.edu/Portals/124/Documents/student-guides/IS128-guide.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cdse.edu">[CDSE]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cdse.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Preparing the DD Form 254October 5, 2021 — Evan: To ensure adequate protection of this procurement, Special Access Program or SAP req...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 5, 2021</span></span></span>

## How SAP clauses shape contractor duties

Most classified Department of Defense contracts use the [DD Form 254]({{ 'dd-form-254/' | relative_url }}), the Contract Security Classification Specification, to communicate security requirements. For ordinary classified work, the form primarily identifies classification guidance and applicable safeguarding rules. Where SAP information is involved, however, the form explicitly signals that additional security requirements exceeding the normal National Industrial Security Program Operating Manual (NISPOM) may apply. Those requirements are incorporated into the contract itself, referenced in the DD Form 254, or provided separately by the responsible SAP security office.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/portals/54/documents/dd/forms/dd/dd0254-Inst.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[whs.mil]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">WHS ESDDD Form 254-Inst, &quot;Instructions for DoD Contract Security...The purpose of this document is to levy uniform requirements for appl...</span></span></span>

Rather than creating a completely separate contracting system, SAP clauses typically add controls such as:

* programme-specific security manuals and classification guides;<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.socom.mil/FOIA/FOIA%20Contracts/H92222-11-D-0001-P00010%20%28Conformed%29/H92222-11-D-0001-P00010%20Attch%206%20-%20DD254.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: socom.mil">[socom.mil]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">socom.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">H92222 11 D 0001 P00010 Attch 6 DD254</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">DDForm 254, Contract Security Classification Specification5 Nov 2010 — Ref 10f: The following regulations, guides, manuals, instructions...</span></span></span>
* mandatory government approval before particular subcontracting arrangements or personnel access;
* specialised reporting and operational security (OPSEC) obligations;
* additional inspection or oversight arrangements;
* restrictions on where work may be performed or where information may be stored;
* enhanced document marking, transmission and [accountability]({{ 'accountability/' | relative_url }}) procedures beyond ordinary classified handling.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/portals/54/documents/dd/forms/dd/dd0254-Inst.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[whs.mil]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">WHS ESDDD Form 254-Inst, &quot;Instructions for DoD Contract Security...The purpose of this document is to levy uniform requirements for appl...</span></span></span>

These obligations are tied to the programme itself rather than simply to the contract's classification level. Two Top Secret contracts may therefore impose very different security burdens if only one supports a SAP.

### Programme security instructions become contractual obligations

A notable feature of SAP contracting is that security guidance is often incorporated by reference to programme-specific documents rather than relying solely on government-wide security manuals. The DD Form 254 instructions direct contracting authorities to identify applicable programme security guides, security classification guides and other supporting documents when SAP requirements exist. Contractors are therefore expected to follow both the baseline classified-information rules and the additional programme instructions governing that particular SAP.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/portals/54/documents/dd/forms/dd/dd0254-Inst.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[WHS ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">WHS ESDDD Form 254-Inst, &quot;Instructions for DoD Contract Security...The purpose of this document is to levy uniform requirements for appl...</span></span></span>

Because these programme documents may evolve during a long-running [acquisition]({{ 'acquisition/' | relative_url }}), revised security guidance can be issued through updated DD Forms 254 or formal contract modifications when classification guidance or security requirements change.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/portals/54/documents/dd/forms/dd/dd0254-Inst.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[WHS ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">WHS ESDDD Form 254-Inst, &quot;Instructions for DoD Contract Security...The purpose of this document is to levy uniform requirements for appl...</span></span></span>

## Closeout and retention limits

SAP obligations frequently continue after technical performance has ended. Ending work on a contract does not automatically permit a contractor to retain programme material indefinitely or dispose of it at will.

The DD Form 254 process establishes specific closeout procedures for classified contracts. Under current instructions, contractors generally receive an automatic two-year retention period for classified material unless the government directs otherwise. If continued retention is needed beyond that period, the contractor must submit a formal request, and the Government Contracting Activity must issue a final DD Form 254 or other written authorisation permitting continued retention. Without that approval, the contractor must return, transfer or properly dispose of the classified material according to government instructions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/portals/54/documents/dd/forms/dd/dd0254-Inst.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[WHS ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">WHS ESDDD Form 254-Inst, &quot;Instructions for DoD Contract Security...The purpose of this document is to levy uniform requirements for appl...</span></span></span>

For SAP material, programme offices may impose even more detailed closeout procedures covering inventories, accountability records, destruction certificates, transfer documentation or continuing storage arrangements. These measures reflect the fact that SAP information remains under programme control even after contractual work has ceased.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.dcsa.mil/Industrial-Security/Special-Access-Programs-Templates/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dcsa.mil">[DCSA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dcsa.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Special Access Programs &amp; TemplatesThis web page has been established as a repository for SAP-specific processes, procedures, documen...</span></span></span>

## Why programme approval matters after the award

Winning a classified contract does not automatically authorise a company to expand work, add subcontractors or move personnel into a SAP.

SAP contracts commonly require additional government approval before:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* granting programme access to new employees;
* bringing subcontractors into the programme;
* transferring programme information between contracts;
* relocating classified work to another facility; or
* imposing SAP-related security obligations further down the supply chain.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/portals/54/documents/dd/forms/dd/dd0254-Inst.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[WHS ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">WHS ESDDD Form 254-Inst, &quot;Instructions for DoD Contract Security...The purpose of this document is to levy uniform requirements for appl...</span></span></span>

</div>

The DD Form 254 instructions specifically state that prime contractors require Government Contracting Activity approval before imposing additional security requirements on subcontractors, and guidance for SAP work notes that subcontracting authority and access approval must come from the responsible SAP security office. This keeps programme managers—not only contracting officers—involved in decisions affecting access to especially sensitive information.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/portals/54/documents/dd/forms/dd/dd0254-Inst.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[WHS ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">WHS ESDDD Form 254-Inst, &quot;Instructions for DoD Contract Security...The purpose of this document is to levy uniform requirements for appl...</span></span></span>

Some SAPs also assign inspection responsibilities differently from ordinary classified contracts. ## What contractors should expect beyond an ordinary classified contract

From a contractor's perspective, the distinctive feature of a SAP contract is not simply higher classification but additional governance. The programme office can require written approvals, dedicated security documentation, specialised handling procedures and continuing accountability throughout the contract's life cycle.

In practice, this means contractors must manage two overlapping sets of obligations. One consists of the government-wide classified contracting rules that apply across the National Industrial Security Program. The second consists of programme-specific requirements established because the sponsoring agency determined that ordinary classified safeguards were insufficient for that particular capability, technology or operation. Compliance therefore depends not only on protecting classified information generally, but also on following the unique rules established for that individual Special Access Program.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.dcsa.mil/Industrial-Security/Special-Access-Programs-Templates/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dcsa.mil">[dcsa.mil]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dcsa.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Special Access Programs &amp; TemplatesThis web page has been established as a repository for SAP-specific processes, procedures, documen...</span></span></span>
