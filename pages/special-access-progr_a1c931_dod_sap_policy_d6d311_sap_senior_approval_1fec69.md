## Introduction

Within the Department of Defense (DoD), creating a Special Access Program (SAP) is deliberately treated as an exceptional governance decision rather than an administrative security measure. A SAP imposes access restrictions beyond ordinary classified-information controls, affecting [acquisition]({{ 'acquisition/' | relative_url }}), operations, congressional reporting, personnel access and long-term oversight. Because those extraordinary restrictions can also reduce transparency and complicate [accountability]({{ 'accountability/' | relative_url }}), DoD policy requires approval at the Department's highest civilian leadership levels rather than allowing individual programme managers or military components to establish SAPs independently. This approval requirement is intended to ensure that enhanced secrecy is reserved for cases where ordinary classification and need-to-know controls are genuinely inadequate.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/portals/54/documents/dd/issuances/dodd/520507p.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[WHS ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">WHS ESDdod directive 5205.07 special access program policySeptember 11, 2024 — 12 Sept 2024 — The Director, DoD SAPCO has jurisdiction ov...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 11, 2024</span></span></span>

## Who Gets to Create a SAP?

Current DoD policy places responsibility for approving SAP actions with the Department's senior civilian leadership, centred on the Secretary of Defense and the Deputy Secretary of Defense. While military departments and other DoD Components may identify programmes that appear to warrant SAP protection, they do not simply declare a programme to be a SAP on their own authority. Instead, proposals are developed through established governance channels and coordinated by the DoD Special Access Program Central Office (SAPCO) before being forwarded for senior decision.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/portals/54/documents/dd/issuances/dodd/520507p.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[WHS ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">WHS ESDdod directive 5205.07 special access program policySeptember 11, 2024 — 12 Sept 2024 — The Director, DoD SAPCO has jurisdiction ov...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 11, 2024</span></span></span>

This structure reflects an important policy principle: the official authorising exceptional secrecy should not be the same official managing the programme seeking that protection. Separating programme execution from approval reduces the risk that security restrictions become self-justifying or are expanded merely for administrative convenience.

## Why Senior Approval Is Required

The requirement for Deputy Secretary or Secretary-level approval serves several governance objectives simultaneously.

**Exceptional secrecy requires exceptional justification.** DoD policy states that SAP protections exist only when information faces an exceptional vulnerability or threat and ordinary classified-information controls are insufficient, or when a [statute]({{ 'statute/' | relative_url }}) specifically requires a SAP. Senior approval forces the Department to test that claim before granting additional restrictions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/portals/54/documents/dd/issuances/dodd/520507p.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[WHS ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">WHS ESDdod directive 5205.07 special access program policySeptember 11, 2024 — 12 Sept 2024 — The Director, DoD SAPCO has jurisdiction ov...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 11, 2024</span></span></span>

**A SAP changes more than [access lists]({{ 'access-lists/' | relative_url }}).** Establishing a SAP creates continuing obligations involving specialised security procedures, reporting, oversight, inspection and lifecycle management. Since those obligations affect multiple organisations across the Department, approval is treated as an enterprise-level decision rather than a programme-level preference.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/portals/54/documents/dd/issuances/dodd/520507p.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[WHS ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">WHS ESDdod directive 5205.07 special access program policySeptember 11, 2024 — 12 Sept 2024 — The Director, DoD SAPCO has jurisdiction ov...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 11, 2024</span></span></span>

**Civilian leadership retains control over extraordinary secrecy.** The Secretary and Deputy Secretary are ultimately responsible for balancing military necessity, legal compliance and democratic accountability. Requiring their approval ensures that compartmentation remains a policy decision made by officials accountable for the Department as a whole rather than by individual acquisition or operational organisations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/portals/54/documents/dd/issuances/dodd/520507p.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[WHS ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">WHS ESDdod directive 5205.07 special access program policySeptember 11, 2024 — 12 Sept 2024 — The Director, DoD SAPCO has jurisdiction ov...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 11, 2024</span></span></span>

## The Role of SAP Oversight Bodies

Senior approval does not occur in isolation. DoD policy establishes governance bodies that evaluate proposed SAP actions before they reach the Deputy Secretary or Secretary.

The DoD SAP Central Office functions as the Department's coordinating authority. It reviews proposed actions, maintains authoritative records, develops security guidance, coordinates annual SAP reviews and serves as the principal liaison for congressional reporting concerning SAPs. Before forwarding actions to the Deputy Secretary, SAPCO coordinates with the relevant Cognizant Authorities and ensures proposals have been examined within the established governance process.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/portals/54/documents/dd/issuances/dodd/520507p.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[WHS ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">WHS ESDdod directive 5205.07 special access program policySeptember 11, 2024 — 12 Sept 2024 — The Director, DoD SAPCO has jurisdiction ov...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 11, 2024</span></span></span>

This layered review is designed to prevent unilateral decisions. Technical experts, security officials, acquisition organisations and senior departmental leadership each examine whether the proposed compartmentation remains justified, legally supportable and operationally necessary.

## Why Programme Managers Cannot Simply Add SAP Protection

Many classified defence activities are already protected by Confidential, Secret or Top Secret classification combined with established need-to-know rules. Those mechanisms are considered the normal method of safeguarding national-security information.

Creating a SAP instead introduces additional administrative burdens and operational consequences. Personnel require separate nomination and approval processes, facilities may need specialised [accreditation]({{ 'accreditation/' | relative_url }}), information handling becomes more restrictive, and coordination across the wider Department can become more difficult. Excessive compartmentation may also hinder legitimate military planning or technology integration if applied too broadly.

For those reasons, DoD policy deliberately prevents programme managers from adding SAP status merely because a project is technologically advanced, politically sensitive or highly valued. The burden rests on demonstrating that ordinary classified-information protections cannot adequately mitigate the specific security risk.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/portals/54/documents/dd/issuances/dodd/520507p.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[WHS ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">WHS ESDdod directive 5205.07 special access program policySeptember 11, 2024 — 12 Sept 2024 — The Director, DoD SAPCO has jurisdiction ov...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 11, 2024</span></span></span>

## Distinguishing Routine Classified Programmes from Novel Cases

The approval threshold also helps distinguish programmes that simply contain highly classified information from those requiring genuinely extraordinary protection.

Most Top Secret programmes remain outside the SAP system because existing classification procedures are considered sufficient. Only where the information's sensitivity, vulnerability or operational consequences exceed what collateral security can reasonably protect does DoD policy contemplate SAP establishment. In other words, "important" or "highly classified" is not, by itself, enough.

This distinction is particularly significant when emerging technologies or operational concepts are involved. Novel capabilities may appear exceptionally sensitive during early development, but senior review requires decision-makers to determine whether enhanced compartmentation is truly necessary or whether conventional classified-information controls already provide adequate protection. That assessment helps keep the SAP system focused on its intended purpose rather than allowing it to expand with every advanced defence programme.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/portals/54/documents/dd/issuances/dodd/520507p.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[WHS ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">WHS ESDdod directive 5205.07 special access program policySeptember 11, 2024 — 12 Sept 2024 — The Director, DoD SAPCO has jurisdiction ov...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 11, 2024</span></span></span>

## Senior Approval Supports Accountability as Well as Secrecy

The approval requirement also strengthens oversight after a SAP is established. Because senior leadership authorised the programme's exceptional protections, the same governance framework supports periodic reviews, reporting obligations and decisions about whether those protections remain justified.

Current DoD policy assigns SAPCO responsibility for coordinating annual SAP reviews and congressional reporting, maintaining central records and supporting continuing oversight. Rather than treating approval as a one-time event, the Department embeds SAPs within an ongoing governance process intended to ensure that extraordinary secrecy continues only while its original justification remains valid.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/portals/54/documents/dd/issuances/dodd/520507p.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[WHS ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">WHS ESDdod directive 5205.07 special access program policySeptember 11, 2024 — 12 Sept 2024 — The Director, DoD SAPCO has jurisdiction ov...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 11, 2024</span></span></span>
