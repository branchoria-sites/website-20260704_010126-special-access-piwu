## Introduction

The Information Security Oversight Office, or ISOO, is the small public-facing oversight office that helps keep the United States secrecy system from becoming entirely self-policing. For special-access-programs, including waived and unacknowledged programmes, its role is not to publish secrets or decide defence policy. Its role is to make sure agencies account for these [compartments]({{ 'compartments/' | relative_url }}), review whether they still meet the legal test for special protection, and submit to government-wide classification oversight. Executive Order 13526 gives [ISOO access]({{ 'isoo-access/' | relative_url }}) to special access programmes “in accordance with the security requirements of each programme”, while also allowing access to be narrowed for exceptionally sensitive programmes. That combination is the central tension: ISOO is a genuine oversight mechanism, but it operates inside the classified system it oversees.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/isoo/policy-documents/cnsi-eo.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Creation must be justified.">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Creation must be justified.</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on archives.gov.</span></span></span>

## Why ISOO matters for SAP accountability

Special access programmes are designed to restrict knowledge more tightly than ordinary classified information. That makes them useful for protecting especially vulnerable military, [intelligence]({{ 'intelligence/' | relative_url }}) or technological information, but it also creates an obvious governance problem: the people best placed to know whether a programme is over-classified, duplicated, obsolete, or misused may be the very people least likely to be allowed into it.

ISOO exists to reduce that risk at the level of the secrecy system. The office is housed within the National Archives and Records Administration and is responsible to the President for policy and oversight of the government-wide security classification system and the National Industrial Security Program, while receiving policy and programme guidance from the National Security Council.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/isoo" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on archives.gov.</span></span></span> Under Executive Order 13526, ISOO can develop implementing directives, oversee agency compliance, review agency classification regulations, conduct on-site reviews, require reports and information, act on complaints, standardise procedures, and report annually to the President.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/isoo/policy-documents/cnsi-eo.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Creation must be justified.">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Creation must be justified.</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on archives.gov.</span></span></span>

For SAPs, the key point is that the executive order does not treat compartmented secrecy as a zone outside the classification system. It says SAPs are subject to ISOO’s oversight programme, and that the ISOO Director must be afforded access to those programmes so ISOO can perform its assigned functions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/isoo/policy-documents/cnsi-eo.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Creation must be justified.">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Creation must be justified.</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on archives.gov.</span></span></span> That matters because it rebuts a common misconception: an unacknowledged or highly compartmented programme may be hidden from the public, but it is not supposed to be hidden from every oversight channel.

## Accounting for SAPs

A SAP is not meant to be created casually. Executive Order 13526 says such programmes should be kept to an “absolute minimum” and established only when ordinary access rules for information at the same classification level are not enough to protect exceptionally vulnerable information. It also limits SAPs to situations where the number of people with ordinary access is reasonably small and proportionate to the protection objective.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/isoo/policy-documents/cnsi-eo.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Creation must be justified.">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Creation must be justified.</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on archives.gov.</span></span></span>

The [accountability]({{ 'accountability/' | relative_url }}) mechanism begins with [accounting]({{ 'accounting/' | relative_url }}). Each agency head must establish and maintain a system of accounting for special access programmes, and the agency head or principal deputy must review each programme annually to decide whether it still meets the executive order’s requirements.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/isoo/policy-documents/cnsi-eo.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Creation must be justified.">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Creation must be justified.</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on archives.gov.</span></span></span> ISOO’s implementing directive adds a practical inspection layer: agencies with SAPs must evaluate those programmes at least annually as part of their self-inspection responsibilities, and senior agency officials must report annually to ISOO on their self-inspection programme.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/isoo/policy-documents/isoo-implementing-directive.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">isoo implementing directive</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National Archives32 CFR Parts 2001 and 2003 Classified National Security Information; Final Rule &#124; National Archives...</span></span></span>

That sounds administrative, but it is a serious control. A living accounting system forces agencies to answer questions that secrecy otherwise tends to blur: What SAPs exist? Who is responsible for them? Why do they still require enhanced protection? Are the compartments still justified by threat, vulnerability and value? The e-CFR version of ISOO’s rules says enhanced SAP controls must be based on an assessment of the value, critical nature and vulnerability of the information, rather than on habit or institutional preference.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/32/2001.49" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: law.cornell.edu">[Legal Information Institute]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">law.cornell.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cornell.edu.</span></span></span>

ISOO has also publicly flagged that SAP accounting is not merely theoretical. In its FY 2021 annual report, ISOO said that, after a National Security Council request for information on active SAPs and Controlled Access Programs, it identified inconsistencies and challenges in agency compliance with the requirements of Executive Order 13526 and 32 CFR Part 2001. It recommended reevaluating the criteria for creating and maintaining such programmes and said additional oversight was needed to ensure agencies were appropriately establishing and administering them.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/files/isoo/reports/isoo-2021-annual-report-to-the-president-final.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">isoo 2021 annual report to the president final</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">isoo 2021 annual report to the president final</span></span></span>

## Oversight boundaries

ISOO’s authority is broad, but it is not unlimited. The office oversees the classification system; it does not replace agency heads, the Secretary of Defense, the Director of National Intelligence, inspectors general, or congressional committees. This is especially important for SAPs because different accountability channels answer different questions.

ISOO’s channel asks whether the programme fits the government-wide classification rules: whether special access is justified, accounted for, reviewed, and compliant with Executive Order 13526. Congressional oversight, by contrast, includes budgetary and programme reporting duties for Department of Defense SAPs under 10 U.S.C. § 119. That [statute]({{ 'statute/' | relative_url }}) requires annual reports to the defence committees, information on new SAPs, cost estimates, justification for SAP designation, and notice before certain classification changes or public announcements.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/10/119" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cornell.edu">[Legal Information Institute]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cornell.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Legal Information Institute10 U.S. Code § 119</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Legal Information Institute10 U.S. Code § 119</span></span></span>

Defence policy adds another layer. The 2024 DoD SAP directive establishes the governance structure for oversight and management of all DoD SAPs in accordance with Executive Order 13526.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/portals/54/documents/dd/issuances/dodd/520507p.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on whs.mil.</span></span></span> The related DoD instruction assigns SAP oversight authorities, cognisant authorities and SAP Central Offices, and requires lifecycle attention from establishment through management, sharing and disestablishment.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/DD/issuances/dodi/520511p.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on whs.mil.</span></span></span> In plain terms, ISOO is the system-wide classification overseer, while DoD’s internal SAP machinery manages the defence enterprise’s programme-specific controls.

The boundary can become contested when agency documents describe internal SAP oversight but omit ISOO. In 2017, the Federation of American Scientists noted that an updated Air Force SAP policy made provision for internal oversight and limited congressional access, but did not acknowledge ISOO’s authority to review and oversee SAPs. ISOO Director Mark Bradley called that an error, pointing back to Executive Order 13526’s requirement that ISOO be afforded access.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://fas.org/publication/usaf-saps/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: fas.org">[Federation of American Scientists]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">fas.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on fas.org.</span></span></span> The episode is useful because it shows the difference between written authority and bureaucratic reflex: even where the executive order is clear, compartmented communities may need reminding that SAP oversight is not purely internal.

## Extra limits for sensitive programmes

The most sensitive SAPs create a sharper version of the same problem. Executive Order 13526 gives ISOO access, but it also allows an agency head to limit SAP access to the ISOO Director and no more than one other ISOO employee. For programmes that are “extraordinarily sensitive and vulnerable”, access may be limited to the Director only.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/isoo/policy-documents/cnsi-eo.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Creation must be justified.">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Creation must be justified.</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on archives.gov.</span></span></span>

That provision is best understood as a compromise. It preserves the principle that ISOO must not be locked out of SAP oversight, while recognising that some compartments may be so sensitive that even oversight access is minimised. The oversight boundary therefore shifts from “public accountability” to “controlled internal accountability”: the public will not see the programme, most officials will not see it, and even ISOO access may be restricted to one person, but the programme is still supposed to remain inside a lawful oversight chain.

Waived Department of Defense SAPs add a separate limit on congressional reporting. Under 10 U.S.C. § 119, the Secretary of Defense may waive certain reporting requirements case by case if including the information would adversely affect national security, but must provide the information and justification for the waiver to the chair and ranking minority member of each defence committee.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/10/119" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cornell.edu">[Legal Information Institute]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cornell.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Legal Information Institute10 U.S. Code § 119</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Legal Information Institute10 U.S. Code § 119</span></span></span> Current DoD policy similarly says the Secretary may designate select [unacknowledged SAPs]({{ 'unacknowledged/' | relative_url }}) as waived when standard reporting would adversely affect national security, while adding that waived SAPs remain subject to congressional oversight.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/DD/issuances/dodi/520511p.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on whs.mil.</span></span></span>

The practical takeaway is that “waived” does not mean “unaccountable”. It means the reporting channel is narrower, the audience is smaller, and the public trace is thinner. For ISOO, the parallel issue is similar: the executive order may allow access to be narrowed for extraordinarily sensitive programmes, but it does not convert those programmes into a class outside ISOO’s oversight framework.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/isoo/policy-documents/cnsi-eo.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Creation must be justified.">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Creation must be justified.</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on archives.gov.</span></span></span>

## What ISOO can see that the public cannot

ISOO’s annual reports are one of the few public windows into whether the secrecy system is functioning, but they necessarily describe SAP oversight at a high level. The office reports annually to the President and gathers classification data, self-assessment reporting and targeted oversight findings from agencies. Its FY 2024 report says it provides statistics, analysis and evaluation of the Classified National Security Information and Controlled Unclassified Information programmes based on agency self-assessment reporting and ISOO targeted oversight [inspections]({{ 'inspections/' | relative_url }}).<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/files/isoo/reports/isoo-fy-2024-annual-report-final-508.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">isoo fy 2024 annual report final 508</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">isoo fy 2024 annual report final 508</span></span></span>

That public reporting is valuable precisely because SAP details are not public. ISOO can say, for example, that it found compliance challenges in how agencies accounted for SAPs and CAPs, or that reform processes are underway, without disclosing the programmes themselves. In FY 2022, ISOO reported that the National Security Council staff memorandum had addressed the need to tighten how SAPs and CAPs are created and overseen.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/files/isoo/reports/isoo-2022-annual-report-to-the-president.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">isoo 2022 annual report to the president</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">isoo 2022 annual report to the president</span></span></span> In FY 2024, ISOO said it continued to support NSC-led interagency policy processes to update and reform classified and controlled information programmes, including SAPs, although those processes had not yet produced new published policies.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/files/isoo/reports/isoo-fy-2024-annual-report-final-508.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">isoo fy 2024 annual report final 508</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">isoo fy 2024 annual report final 508</span></span></span>

The limitation is just as important as the value. ISOO’s public reports can reveal systemic concerns, reform priorities and oversight posture, but they cannot let readers audit individual unacknowledged or waived programmes. That creates a recurring trust gap: the public can see that an oversight framework exists, and can see when ISOO flags weaknesses, but cannot independently inspect the most sensitive compartments.

## The accountability test that matters

The strongest way to judge ISOO’s role is not to ask whether it makes SAPs transparent. It does not, and is not designed to. The better test is whether it prevents the SAP system from becoming a one-way ratchet in which programmes are created, compartmented, retained and shielded without periodic justification.

## Why this narrow office carries unusual weight

ISOO’s importance is easy to miss because it does not look like a dramatic watchdog. It does not hold public hearings about unacknowledged programmes. It does not publish lists of waived SAPs. It does not declassify secrets by itself simply because the public is curious. Its power lies in something quieter: it is the office named in the executive order as the classification-system overseer, with authority to demand information, conduct reviews and report to the President. [National Archives](https://www.archives.gov/isoo/policy-documents/cnsi-eo.html)

For special-access-programs, including waived and unacknowledged programmes, that quiet role is central. Without ISOO, SAP accountability would depend even more heavily on the agencies that create and run the compartments, plus the limited congressional channels available for defence SAPs. With ISOO, there is at least a government-wide classification overseer charged with asking whether the secrecy architecture itself is being followed.

The result is neither full transparency nor unchecked secrecy. It is a constrained oversight model: agencies may protect the most sensitive information through special access controls, but they must account for those controls, review them, and remain answerable to an oversight office whose authority reaches into the compartmented system itself.

      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
})();
