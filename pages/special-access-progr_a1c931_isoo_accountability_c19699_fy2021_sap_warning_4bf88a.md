## Introduction

The Information Security Oversight Office ([ISOO]({{ 'isoo/' | relative_url }})) used its Fiscal Year (FY) 2021 Annual Report to highlight an unusual governance problem rather than a security breach: the federal government could not consistently account for all active Special Access Programs (SAPs) and Controlled Access Programs (CAPs) using the standards required by the national classification system. The warning did not suggest that secret programmes had been publicly exposed or that their operational details were compromised. Instead, it revealed weaknesses in how agencies documented, justified and administered highly restricted programmes, including acknowledged and [unacknowledged SAPs]({{ 'unacknowledged/' | relative_url }}), when responding to a government-wide information request.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/files/isoo/reports/isoo-2021-annual-report-to-the-president-final.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">We recommend reevaluating the criteria for creating and maintaining SAPs and CAPs to ensure they align with...</span></span></span>

This episode matters because oversight of SAPs depends on accurate [accounting]({{ 'accounting/' | relative_url }}) rather than public disclosure. If agencies cannot consistently identify which programmes exist, who is responsible for them, or whether they continue to satisfy the legal criteria for enhanced protection, external oversight becomes significantly more difficult even while operational secrecy remains intact.

## What triggered the SAP information request?

The catalyst was a July 2021 request from the National Security Council (NSC). According to ISOO, the NSC directed the office to contact all executive branch agencies administering active SAPs and CAPs in order to compile what it described as the first comprehensive government-wide list of such programmes. The exercise covered both acknowledged and unacknowledged programmes and sought to verify that agencies were complying with the requirements established by Executive Order 13526.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/files/isoo/reports/isoo-2021-annual-report-to-the-president-final.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">We recommend reevaluating the criteria for creating and maintaining SAPs and CAPs to ensure they align with...</span></span></span>

The request served two related purposes:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--checklist" markdown="1">

* determine which agencies were operating SAPs and CAPs;
* verify that programmes had been properly established, reviewed and monitored under the governing classification rules;
* assess whether agencies were maintaining reliable records rather than simply relying on internal compartmented practices.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/files/isoo/reports/isoo-2021-annual-report-to-the-president-final.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">We recommend reevaluating the criteria for creating and maintaining SAPs and CAPs to ensure they align with...</span></span></span>

</div>

Although the public report does not disclose programme names or numbers, it makes clear that the exercise was administrative rather than investigative. The concern was whether agencies were meeting the [accountability]({{ 'accountability/' | relative_url }}) requirements that accompany exceptional secrecy.

## What inconsistencies did ISOO report?

ISOO's findings were notable because they focused on systemic administration rather than isolated mistakes.

The report states that the information collected revealed "inconsistencies and challenges" in agency compliance with both Executive Order 13526 and the implementing regulations in 32 CFR Part 2001. These rules govern how SAPs are created, maintained and periodically reviewed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/files/isoo/reports/isoo-2021-annual-report-to-the-president-final.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">We recommend reevaluating the criteria for creating and maintaining SAPs and CAPs to ensure they align with...</span></span></span>

The report does not publish a detailed breakdown of each inconsistency, but its recommendations indicate the kinds of weaknesses ISOO believed existed. Specifically, it called for:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--checklist" markdown="1">

* re-evaluating the criteria used to create and maintain SAPs and CAPs;
* ensuring that programmes continue to align with current national security needs rather than historical practice;
* developing and implementing clearer requirements supporting national security objectives;
* increasing oversight to ensure agencies establish and administer these programmes appropriately.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/files/isoo/reports/isoo-2021-annual-report-to-the-president-final.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">We recommend reevaluating the criteria for creating and maintaining SAPs and CAPs to ensure they align with...</span></span></span>

</div>

The language is significant because it does not imply that every SAP lacked justification. Instead, it suggests that the government's overall management framework contained enough inconsistencies that stronger oversight was warranted.

## Why the warning stood out

Annual ISOO reports commonly discuss classification statistics, declassification activity and policy developments. The FY 2021 report was unusual because it publicly acknowledged deficiencies involving one of the most sensitive categories of classified information.

Rather than treating SAP administration as a closed issue handled entirely inside defence and [intelligence]({{ 'intelligence/' | relative_url }}) organisations, ISOO stated that the review uncovered compliance problems serious enough to warrant formal recommendations to the President. The report therefore demonstrated that even programmes protected by extraordinary security measures remain subject to government-wide oversight expectations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/files/isoo/reports/isoo-2021-annual-report-to-the-president-final.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">We recommend reevaluating the criteria for creating and maintaining SAPs and CAPs to ensure they align with...</span></span></span>

Equally important is what the report did **not** claim. It did not allege widespread abuse of SAP authorities, identify unlawful programmes or disclose operational details. The warning concerned governance: whether agencies were consistently applying the legal framework governing enhanced access restrictions.

## Why accounting matters more than counting

The issue raised by ISOO was broader than simply producing a numerical inventory.

Effective accounting for SAPs requires agencies to demonstrate that each programme:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--checklist" markdown="1">

* continues to satisfy the legal threshold for special access;
* has identifiable management responsibility;
* is periodically reviewed;
* remains necessary despite the executive order's instruction that SAPs be kept to an absolute minimum;
* is administered under the requirements established in Executive Order 13526 and 32 CFR Part 2001.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/files/isoo/reports/isoo-2021-annual-report-to-the-president-final.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">We recommend reevaluating the criteria for creating and maintaining SAPs and CAPs to ensure they align with...</span></span></span>

</div>

Without reliable accounting, oversight bodies face a practical problem. They may be unable to determine whether programmes have become obsolete, duplicative or unnecessarily compartmented because the administrative record itself is incomplete or inconsistent.

The FY 2021 findings therefore highlighted a distinction that is often overlooked: protecting information through compartmentation is only one part of SAP governance. Maintaining accurate records explaining why those [compartments]({{ 'compartments/' | relative_url }}) exist is an equally important accountability function.

## Why the warning matters for future oversight

The FY 2021 report illustrates that oversight of highly classified programmes depends on institutional processes rather than public transparency.

ISOO's recommendations pointed towards stronger life-cycle management of SAPs and CAPs instead of simply preserving existing structures. By recommending renewed evaluation criteria and additional oversight, the office signalled that programmes should be actively justified against current security needs rather than automatically continued because they have long existed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/files/isoo/reports/isoo-2021-annual-report-to-the-president-final.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">We recommend reevaluating the criteria for creating and maintaining SAPs and CAPs to ensure they align with...</span></span></span>

The warning also demonstrated that oversight can reveal meaningful weaknesses without revealing classified information. Public readers learned that accounting deficiencies existed, yet no operational details of any special access programme were disclosed.

Within the broader history of Information Security Oversight Office involvement in SAP accountability, the FY 2021 report therefore stands as an important public acknowledgement that effective oversight requires more than protecting secrets.
