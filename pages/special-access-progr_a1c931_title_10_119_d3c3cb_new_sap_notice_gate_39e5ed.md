## Introduction

The annual reporting system in 10 U.S.C. §119 contains a lesser-known but important safeguard for programmes that are about to enter the Department of Defense's Special Access Program (SAP) system. Rather than allowing a newly designated SAP to appear for the first time in the ordinary annual report due on 1 March, [Congress]({{ 'congress/' | relative_url }}) requires an earlier notification pathway. By 1 February, new or proposed SAPs are reported separately, giving the congressional defence committees advance notice of why extraordinary security controls are being applied before the programme is fully established. This earlier reporting requirement is intended to ensure that the decision to create a SAP receives oversight at the point of designation rather than only after it has become an established classified programme.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&amp;num=0&amp;req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title10-section119" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uscode.house.gov">[U.S. Code]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uscode.house.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Code10 USC 119: Special access programs: congressional...(a)(1) Not later than March 1 of each year, the Secretary of Defense shall subm...</span></span></span>

## The oversight brake before a SAP starts

The February reporting requirement serves a different purpose from the standard annual SAP report. The March report focuses on programmes that already exist, their [budgets]({{ 'budgets/' | relative_url }}), [milestones]({{ 'milestones/' | relative_url }}) and projected costs. The February submission, by contrast, is aimed at programmes that are newly designated or proposed for SAP status.

This distinction reflects an important policy choice. Designating a programme as a SAP imposes security measures beyond those used for ordinary classified information. Those additional controls can affect funding visibility, contracting, personnel access and congressional oversight. Because those consequences arise immediately upon designation, Congress requires notice before the programme simply becomes another entry in the following year's oversight cycle.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&amp;num=0&amp;req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title10-section119" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uscode.house.gov">[U.S. Code]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uscode.house.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Code10 USC 119: Special access programs: congressional...(a)(1) Not later than March 1 of each year, the Secretary of Defense shall subm...</span></span></span>

In practical terms, the February submission functions as an early review point. It provides Congress with an opportunity to evaluate whether the proposed use of special access protection appears justified while the programme is still at the beginning of its SAP lifecycle.

## What counts as a new SAP

The reporting pathway applies when a programme is newly designated as a Special Access Program or is proposed to receive SAP status. The trigger is not merely the existence of a new defence activity or [acquisition]({{ 'acquisition/' | relative_url }}) effort; it is the decision that the programme requires the enhanced safeguarding and access restrictions associated with the SAP system.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&amp;num=0&amp;req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title10-section119" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uscode.house.gov">[U.S. Code]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uscode.house.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Code10 USC 119: Special access programs: congressional...(a)(1) Not later than March 1 of each year, the Secretary of Defense shall subm...</span></span></span>

Although many defence projects begin as ordinary classified programmes, only a comparatively small number receive SAP designation. The decision is generally reserved for circumstances where normal classification measures are considered insufficient to protect exceptionally sensitive technology, [intelligence]({{ 'intelligence/' | relative_url }}) activities or operational capabilities. The February notification therefore centres on the designation decision itself rather than on the programme's technical details.

## Why justification and similar-programme data matter

The information required for a newly designated SAP differs noticeably from the information required for an established programme.

According to Department of Defense training material summarising the statutory requirements, the February SAP listing includes:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--metric" markdown="1">

* notice that the programme has been designated as a SAP together with the justification for that designation;
* the current estimate of the programme's total cost; and
* identification of existing programmes or technologies with similar missions or technological objectives.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cdse.edu/Portals/124/Documents/student-guides/SA001-guide.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cdse.edu">[CDSE]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cdse.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Student Guide Course: Special Access Program (SAP)...Section 119 of Title 10 specifies the types of reports that are submitted to Co...</span></span></span>

</div>

Each element addresses a different oversight concern.

The justification requirement asks the Department to explain why ordinary classified handling is inadequate. Congress is therefore reviewing not simply whether the programme is important, but whether it genuinely warrants the exceptional secrecy of a SAP.

The cost estimate allows congressional committees to understand the likely financial scale before significant spending has accumulated. Early cost visibility is particularly important because later annual reports may describe programmes that are already well underway.

The requirement to identify similar programmes is especially significant from an oversight perspective. It encourages reviewers to consider whether the proposed SAP duplicates existing work or whether comparable capabilities are already protected elsewhere. That comparison helps reduce unnecessary proliferation of SAPs and gives Congress additional context for evaluating whether a separate protected programme is warranted.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cdse.edu/Portals/124/Documents/student-guides/SA001-guide.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cdse.edu">[CDSE]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cdse.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Student Guide Course: Special Access Program (SAP)...Section 119 of Title 10 specifies the types of reports that are submitted to Co...</span></span></span>

## How the 30-day waiting period works

The February reporting requirement is closely linked to a statutory waiting period intended to prevent immediate implementation after notification.

Under 10 U.S.C. §119, a newly designated SAP generally may not be carried out until 30 days have elapsed after the required congressional notification has been submitted. The waiting period gives the defence committees time to review the justification and raise questions before the programme proceeds under its new special-access authorities.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&amp;num=0&amp;req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title10-section119" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uscode.house.gov">[U.S. Code]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uscode.house.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Code10 USC 119: Special access programs: congressional...(a)(1) Not later than March 1 of each year, the Secretary of Defense shall subm...</span></span></span>

The waiting period is not designed as a congressional approval process in the sense of requiring an affirmative vote. Instead, it creates a structured pause between executive designation and programme implementation. During that interval, congressional oversight can occur while the decision remains relatively easy to revisit, rather than after organisational structures, [contracts]({{ 'contracts/' | relative_url }}) and security arrangements have become entrenched.

For programmes regarded as exceptionally sensitive, separate statutory provisions governing waived SAPs modify the ordinary reporting process. Even then, the basic principle remains that heightened secrecy is paired with tailored congressional notification rather than complete exemption from legislative oversight.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&amp;num=0&amp;req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title10-section119" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uscode.house.gov">[U.S. Code]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uscode.house.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Code10 USC 119: Special access programs: congressional...(a)(1) Not later than March 1 of each year, the Secretary of Defense shall subm...</span></span></span>

## Why the February gate matters

The February reporting mechanism illustrates that congressional oversight under 10 U.S.C. §119 begins before a programme settles into the annual reporting cycle. Instead of treating SAP designation as an internal administrative decision, the [statute]({{ 'statute/' | relative_url }}) requires the Department of Defense to explain why enhanced secrecy is necessary, estimate the programme's anticipated costs and identify comparable efforts before the programme moves forward.

Viewed alongside the annual 1 March report, the February gate creates two distinct stages of oversight. The first examines whether a programme should receive special-access protection at all; the second monitors programmes that have already entered the SAP system.
