## Introduction

A Special Access Program (SAP) banner line is not simply a document [title]({{ 'statute/' | relative_url }}). It is a compact handling instruction that tells every authorised reader, at a glance, how the information must be protected. The sequence of markings communicates three separate things: the overall classification level, the fact that special access controls apply, and exactly which SAP channel governs the material. If additional dissemination restrictions exist, those appear in a prescribed position as well. The result is a standardised syntax that allows security officers, document custodians and cleared readers to recognise the correct handling rules immediately without describing the programme itself. ## How the banner line becomes an instruction

The order of the elements is deliberate because each part answers a different handling question.

A typical SAP banner follows this pattern:

**Classification level → SAP caveat ("SPECIAL ACCESS REQUIRED" or "SAR") → programme nickname or codeword → dissemination controls (if any).**

For example:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **SECRET//SAR-RAZOR DUST**
* **TOP SECRET//SAR-DIGITAL AXIS//WAIVED**
* **TOP SECRET//SI-GAMMA//SAR-PRIOR TALLY**

</div>

Reading from left to right, a cleared handler learns:

* **How highly classified the information is.** The classification (such as Secret or Top Secret) establishes the minimum clearance required.
* **Whether ordinary classified handling is sufficient.** The appearance of **SPECIAL ACCESS REQUIRED** or **SAR** signals that clearance alone is not enough; the reader must also be formally indoctrinated into the relevant SAP.
* **Which protected access channel applies.** The nickname or codeword identifies the specific programme governing access.
* **Whether extra distribution restrictions exist.** Controls such as **WAIVED** or other authorised dissemination markings add further limitations on sharing or release.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/DD/issuances/dodm/520507-V4p.PDF" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[whs.mil]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Banner Line. The banner therefore functions less like a heading and more like a routing instruction for everyone who stores, transmits, reproduces or reads the document.

## Why the order matters

The sequence is fixed because different categories of markings perform different security functions.

The classification level always comes first because it establishes the baseline protection required for every recipient. Any Sensitive Compartmented Information (SCI) controls, where applicable, precede [SAP markings]({{ 'markings/' | relative_url }}) according to the prescribed hierarchy. Only after those system-wide controls does the SAP caveat appear, followed by the programme identifier. Dissemination controls are placed last.

This hierarchy prevents ambiguity. A security professional can determine, in order:

## What examples such as SAR-BP are actually showing

A common point of confusion is the difference between banner lines and [portion markings]({{ 'portion-markings/' | relative_url }}).

The **banner line** uses the programme's full nickname or codeword:

* **TOP SECRET//SAR-TIN BAKER**

Within individual paragraphs, however, the document normally switches to the programme's assigned **Programme Identifier (PID)**—typically a two- or three-letter abbreviation.

A paragraph might therefore be marked:

* **(TS//SAR-TB)**

or, for another programme:

* **(TS//SAR-BP)**

The short identifier is not intended to rename the programme. It simply provides a compact way of repeating the applicable SAP control throughout the document without reproducing the full nickname every time.

DoD guidance specifically states that assigned PIDs are **not** to replace the full programme nickname or codeword in the banner line itself. Their purpose is paragraph-level handling, where each portion must independently indicate the SAP restrictions applying to that specific text.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/DD/issuances/dodm/520507-V4p.PDF" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[WHS ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Banner Line. ## From page markings to paragraph markings

The banner tells a handler how to protect the entire page, but classified documents also require more granular markings.

Each paragraph containing SAP information carries its own portion marking that combines:<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://tjaglcs.army.mil/Portals/0/G2/Marking_Classified_Information.pdf?ver=37ujphdQLYKwrx3DD7zNcw%3D%3D" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: tjaglcs.army.mil">[tjaglcs.army.mil]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">tjaglcs.army.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Marking Classified Information</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Classified Information Job Aid18 Dec 2014 — For more information on marking SAP documents, consult DoD Manual 5205.07, Vol. 4. Special A...</span></span></span>


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--phrase-pills" markdown="1">

* the paragraph's classification level;
* the **SAR** caveat; and
* the applicable PID.

</div>

If [multiple SAPs]({{ 'multiple-saps/' | relative_url }}) are discussed within a paragraph, every relevant PID must appear, normally listed alphabetically and separated by slashes. This allows individual passages to be extracted, quoted or reproduced without losing the handling instructions attached to that specific information.

The distinction is practical:

* **Banner line:** instructions for handling the page or medium.
* **Portion marking:** instructions for handling an individual paragraph or section.

Together they ensure that security controls remain attached to the information even when only part of a document is copied or referenced.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/DD/issuances/dodm/520507-V4p.PDF" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[WHS ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Banner Line. ## How multiple programmes are signalled

Banner syntax also tells handlers whether more than one SAP governs the document.

When information from two SAPs appears together, both programme names are included:

* **TOP SECRET//SAR-BLUE FOG/SAR-MUDDY PATH**

When three or more SAPs are involved, the banner normally changes to:

* **TOP SECRET//SAR-MULTIPLE PROGRAMS**

The detailed list of applicable programmes is then maintained elsewhere in the document rather than crowding the banner itself.

This convention keeps the banner immediately readable while still warning every authorised handler that several independent SAP channels must be considered before information is shared or extracted. ## Where dissemination controls such as WAIVED fit

Dissemination controls modify what a cleared, SAP-authorised reader may do with the information after access has been established.

These controls appear after the programme name in the banner line, separated by double slashes.

One example is:

* **TOP SECRET//SAR-DIGITAL AXIS//WAIVED**

In [DoD SAP policy]({{ 'do-d-policy/' | relative_url }}), **WAIVED** identifies a programme that has been exempted from the normal congressional reporting requirements by the Secretary of Defense. Because it functions as a dissemination control, the marking appears last in the banner hierarchy and is also repeated where applicable in paragraph markings and on media containing the information.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/DD/issuances/dodm/520507-V4p.PDF" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[WHS ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Banner Line. ## Why the banner is a handling tool rather than a programme description

Perhaps the most important point is that the banner is designed for people who already possess authorised access—not for explaining what the programme does.

A phrase such as **SAR-PRIOR TALLY** or **SAR-TIN BAKER** communicates almost nothing about the underlying activity. Instead, it tells authorised personnel which security channel governs the material, which markings must accompany copies and extracts, and whether additional dissemination restrictions apply.

In practice, the banner acts as a compact set of operating instructions. Every authorised handler can immediately determine whether they are permitted to access the material, how each page and paragraph must remain marked, and what additional distribution limits continue to follow the information throughout its lifecycle.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/DD/issuances/dodm/520507-V4p.PDF" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[WHS ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Banner Line.
