## Introduction

Historical reviews by the US Government [Accountability]({{ 'accountability/' | relative_url }}) Office (GAO) reached a consistent conclusion about highly classified defence [acquisition]({{ 'acquisition/' | relative_url }}) programmes: secrecy by itself does not improve programme management. Although special access programmes (SAPs) receive extraordinary security protections, the GAO found that they remained vulnerable to many of the same weaknesses that affected conventional defence acquisitions, including cost growth, schedule delays, weak management controls, and performance shortfalls. Those findings are especially relevant to waived SAPs, where congressional reporting is intentionally narrowed. They suggest that while limiting access may reduce security risks, it does not eliminate the need for rigorous financial management, independent oversight, or disciplined acquisition practices.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gao.gov/assets/nsiad-93-78.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.gov">[GAO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Oversight of Special Access Programs Has IncreasedDecember 21, 1992 — 21 Dec 1992 — Sampled programs experienced cost increases, sched...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 21, 1992</span></span></span>

## What GAO found in SAP management

A key GAO review published in late 1992 examined Department of Defense (DoD) oversight of special access programmes after [Congress]({{ 'congress/' | relative_url }}) had expanded statutory reporting requirements. Rather than finding that SAPs operated outside normal acquisition systems, the GAO concluded that they generally followed the same budgeting, resource justification, execution, and financial review processes used for other defence acquisition programmes. Classified funding lines obscured the programmes from public view, but internally they still relied on familiar management structures.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gao.gov/assets/nsiad-93-78.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.gov">[GAO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Oversight of Special Access Programs Has IncreasedDecember 21, 1992 — 21 Dec 1992 — Sampled programs experienced cost increases, sched...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 21, 1992</span></span></span>

The most striking finding was that the sampled SAPs displayed management outcomes similar to ordinary acquisition programmes. According to the GAO, programmes in its sample experienced:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Cost increases.
* Schedule delays.
* Performance problems comparable to those found elsewhere in the Department of Defense.

</div>

This observation directly challenged any assumption that exceptional secrecy produced exceptional acquisition performance. Instead, it suggested that technical complexity, ambitious requirements, programme management, and contracting practices continued to drive outcomes regardless of classification level.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gao.gov/assets/nsiad-93-78.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.gov">[GAO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Oversight of Special Access Programs Has IncreasedDecember 21, 1992 — 21 Dec 1992 — Sampled programs experienced cost increases, sched...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 21, 1992</span></span></span>

The review also concluded that congressional oversight had become stronger following legislative reforms requiring more structured reporting on SAPs. However, the GAO did not portray increased reporting as a substitute for continuous management discipline. Oversight could identify risks, but it could not automatically prevent familiar acquisition failures.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gao.gov/assets/nsiad-93-78.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.gov">[GAO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Oversight of Special Access Programs Has IncreasedDecember 21, 1992 — 21 Dec 1992 — Sampled programs experienced cost increases, sched...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 21, 1992</span></span></span>

## Why normal acquisition problems still appeared

The GAO's findings reflected a broader reality of defence procurement: classification changes who can examine a programme, but it does not change the underlying engineering, contracting, or organisational challenges.

Several factors explain why secret programmes remain exposed to ordinary acquisition risks.

**Complex technology remains difficult to develop.** Many SAPs involve advanced aircraft, sensors, communications systems, or other cutting-edge capabilities. Technical [uncertainty]({{ 'uncertainty/' | relative_url }}), integration problems, and changing requirements continue to affect schedules and costs regardless of security classification.

**Management incentives do not disappear.** Programme offices still face pressures to meet [milestones]({{ 'milestones/' | relative_url }}), defend [budgets]({{ 'budgets/' | relative_url }}), and satisfy operational requirements. Excessive optimism, incomplete cost estimates, and evolving mission needs can therefore affect SAPs just as they affect conventional programmes.

**Reduced visibility can weaken external challenge.** Narrower access limits the number of reviewers capable of questioning assumptions or identifying emerging problems. Although highly cleared officials may receive detailed information, fewer independent perspectives may increase the importance of strong internal controls.

**Security can complicate oversight.** Classification restrictions can make it more difficult to compare programmes, share lessons across organisations, or involve outside experts. The security measures are intended to protect sensitive capabilities, but they may also reduce opportunities for routine scrutiny if not balanced by robust internal review mechanisms.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gao.gov/assets/nsiad-93-78.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.gov">[GAO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Oversight of Special Access Programs Has IncreasedDecember 21, 1992 — 21 Dec 1992 — Sampled programs experienced cost increases, sched...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 21, 1992</span></span></span>

Importantly, the GAO did not argue that secrecy itself caused programme failure. Rather, it concluded that secrecy did not prevent the well-known acquisition problems that repeatedly affected defence procurement more broadly.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gao.gov/assets/nsiad-93-78.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.gov">[GAO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Oversight of Special Access Programs Has IncreasedDecember 21, 1992 — 21 Dec 1992 — Sampled programs experienced cost increases, sched...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 21, 1992</span></span></span>

## How the findings support stronger oversight

Within the framework of waived SAPs, these conclusions have important implications.

A waived SAP narrows the statutory flow of information to Congress by allowing certain programme details to be provided only to the chair and ranking minority member of each defence committee when broader reporting would harm national security. That legal mechanism protects exceptionally sensitive information, but it also concentrates oversight among a much smaller group of officials.

The GAO's evidence reinforces the importance of ensuring that this narrower oversight channel remains effective. If secret programmes experience the same acquisition risks as ordinary programmes, then oversight becomes more—not less—important. Cost growth, schedule slippage, and performance deficiencies cannot be assumed away simply because a programme is highly classified.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gao.gov/assets/nsiad-93-78.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.gov">[GAO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Oversight of Special Access Programs Has IncreasedDecember 21, 1992 — 21 Dec 1992 — Sampled programs experienced cost increases, sched...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 21, 1992</span></span></span>

The practical implication is that waived reporting should be understood as a security accommodation rather than a management exemption. Robust internal auditing, disciplined acquisition controls, independent review, and informed congressional oversight remain essential because the underlying programme risks persist.

## The enduring lesson

The GAO's early 1990s reviews remain influential because they separated two issues that are often conflated: protecting sensitive information and managing complex acquisition programmes.

The evidence showed that SAP secrecy successfully concealed programme details from wider public disclosure, but it did not insulate programmes from the familiar realities of defence acquisition. Cost overruns, delays, and performance problems still emerged under classified conditions, demonstrating that sound governance depends on effective management and meaningful oversight rather than secrecy alone.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gao.gov/assets/nsiad-93-78.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.gov">[GAO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Oversight of Special Access Programs Has IncreasedDecember 21, 1992 — 21 Dec 1992 — Sampled programs experienced cost increases, sched...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 21, 1992</span></span></span>
