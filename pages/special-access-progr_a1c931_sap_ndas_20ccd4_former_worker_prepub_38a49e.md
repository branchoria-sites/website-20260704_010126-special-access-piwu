## Introduction

Former personnel who have been granted access to United States Special Access Program (SAP) information do not automatically gain the freedom to discuss that experience once they leave government service or a defence contractor. Instead, the obligation to protect SAP information generally continues indefinitely, and one of its most significant practical consequences is the requirement to submit certain public communications for official prepublication review before they are released.

For people who worked on highly sensitive, including waived or unacknowledged, SAPs, this duty extends well beyond books and memoirs. Depending on the agreement they signed and the nature of the material, articles, conference papers, podcasts, interviews, lawsuits, fictional works and other public-facing material may require review if they contain, appear to contain, or could reasonably be derived from protected programme knowledge. The purpose is not to approve opinions or prevent criticism as such, but to prevent the unauthorised disclosure of classified or SAP-derived information while allowing lawful public speech where possible.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/DD/forms/dd/dd2836.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[whs.mil]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">WHS ESDspecial access program indoctrination agreementApril 16, 2007 — I understand and agree that my obligation to submit such preparati...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 16, 2007</span></span></span>

## When public speech can trigger review

The [SAP Indoctrination]({{ 'sapia/' | relative_url }}) Agreement (DD Form 2836) contains one of the clearest publicly available descriptions of these obligations. It requires a person granted SAP access to submit for security review any writing or other preparation—including works of fiction—that contains, purports to contain, describes, or is believed to derive from SAP information before it is disclosed publicly or shown to unauthorised persons. Importantly, the agreement expressly states that this obligation applies both during access and afterwards.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/DD/forms/dd/dd2836.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[WHS ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">WHS ESDspecial access program indoctrination agreementApril 16, 2007 — I understand and agree that my obligation to submit such preparati...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 16, 2007</span></span></span>

In practice, this means a former SAP participant should consider review before releasing material such as:

* Memoirs describing work on classified programmes.
* Academic papers discussing projects, technologies or [operations]({{ 'operations/' | relative_url }}) encountered during SAP duties.

<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Conference presentations or lectures drawing on personal experience.
* Podcast or documentary interviews about former assignments.
* Opinion articles or blog posts relying on insider knowledge.
* Fiction that closely reflects protected operational details.
* Court filings or declarations that discuss classified programme information.

</div>

The requirement is tied to the content rather than the format. A podcast interview may require review just as much as a printed autobiography if it discusses protected material.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/DD/forms/dd/dd2836.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[WHS ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">WHS ESDspecial access program indoctrination agreementApril 16, 2007 — I understand and agree that my obligation to submit such preparati...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 16, 2007</span></span></span>

## How the review process generally works

Although procedures differ between agencies and programme sponsors, the basic mechanism follows a common pattern.

A former employee prepares the intended material and submits it to the government office responsible for reviewing publications associated with the organisation that granted access. For Department of Defense personnel, this frequently involves the prepublication review system administered through the Department's security review structure, while [intelligence]({{ 'intelligence/' | relative_url }}) agencies maintain their own review authorities.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://columbialawreview.org/wp-content/uploads/2016/04/Casey-Kevin.pdf?inline=1" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: columbialawreview.org">[Columbia Law Review]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">columbialawreview.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Columbia Law ReviewTILL DEATH DO US PART: PREPUBLICATION REVIEW IN...by K Casey · Cited by 11 — In essence, these agreements require emp...</span></span></span>

Reviewers do not ordinarily evaluate whether the publication is favourable or critical. Their stated role is to determine whether the submission contains classified information, SAP information, or information derived from such material that remains protected.

The SAP Indoctrination Agreement also establishes an expectation that the reviewing agency will respond within a reasonable period, normally no more than 30 working days after receiving the submission, although particularly complex or multi-agency reviews may take longer in practice if coordination is required.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/DD/forms/dd/dd2836.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[WHS ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">WHS ESDspecial access program indoctrination agreementApril 16, 2007 — I understand and agree that my obligation to submit such preparati...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 16, 2007</span></span></span>

## How derived knowledge creates risk

One of the hardest issues for former SAP personnel is that protected information is not limited to reproducing classified documents.

Someone may never quote a classified report yet still disclose protected information by combining personal memories, operational experience and publicly available material. This is sometimes described as "derived" information: details that reveal classified knowledge even though every individual sentence may appear unclassified.

Examples can include:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--caution" markdown="1">

* Explaining how an acknowledged capability actually operates.
* Describing operational procedures learned only through SAP access.
* Confirming or denying speculation about an unacknowledged programme.
* Connecting separate public facts in a way that exposes classified relationships.
* Revealing technical limitations or vulnerabilities known through programme participation.

</div>

Because former participants possess context unavailable to ordinary readers, they may unintentionally disclose information that appears innocuous to them but remains classified from the government's perspective. This is why many prepublication agreements require review whenever the author has reason to believe the material could derive from protected programme knowledge.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/DD/forms/dd/dd2836.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[WHS ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">WHS ESDspecial access program indoctrination agreementApril 16, 2007 — I understand and agree that my obligation to submit such preparati...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 16, 2007</span></span></span>

## Why former workers often submit material even when unsure

Prepublication review serves as a form of risk management for former employees.

A person may sincerely believe that everything in a manuscript comes from public sources. However, if years of work inside SAPs influenced how those facts were selected, interpreted or connected, the government may view the resulting publication differently.

Recent Intelligence Community guidance has attempted to clarify this problem by explaining that former personnel should judge whether an informed intelligence professional might reasonably conclude that material contains or derives from protected intelligence. Where there is reasonable doubt, submission for review is expected. At the same time, the guidance recognises that material clearly based only on officially acknowledged public information may not always require review.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.justsecurity.org/107368/prepublication-review-step-right-direction/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: justsecurity.org">[Just Security]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">justsecurity.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">prepublication review step right direction</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Community Directive concerning the obligations of former government employees who are bound by “prepublication review” requirements. ## What silence means after debriefing

Leaving a SAP normally involves a formal [debriefing]({{ 'debriefing/' | relative_url }}), but debriefing is not the same as release from secrecy obligations.

The publicly available SAP Indoctrination Agreement states that, unless an authorised government representative provides a written release, the agreement's obligations continue indefinitely. The debriefing serves primarily as a reminder that the individual remains bound by those commitments after employment, retirement or reassignment ends.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/DD/forms/dd/dd2836.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[WHS ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">WHS ESDspecial access program indoctrination agreementApril 16, 2007 — I understand and agree that my obligation to submit such preparati...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 16, 2007</span></span></span>

As a result, many former personnel adopt conservative communication practices. Rather than attempting to determine independently whether sensitive memories remain protected, they seek official review before discussing experiences that touch on programme activities.

This continuing silence should not automatically be interpreted as confirmation that extraordinary claims are true or false. In many cases, it simply reflects the enduring legal obligations attached to access to highly restricted programmes.

## Disputes over the review system

Prepublication review has long been controversial.

Former intelligence and defence personnel have argued that reviews can be inconsistent, slow or overly cautious, potentially delaying legitimate public discussion or memoirs that contain no classified information.
