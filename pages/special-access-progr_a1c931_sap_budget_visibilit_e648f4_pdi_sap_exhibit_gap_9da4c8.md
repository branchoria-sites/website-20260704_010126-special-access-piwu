## Introduction

The Pacific Deterrence Initiative (PDI) was created to give [Congress]({{ 'congress/' | relative_url }}) a clearer picture of how the United States funds military posture, infrastructure and capability improvements intended to deter conflict in the Indo-Pacific. Yet a notable example identified by the Government [Accountability]({{ 'accountability/' | relative_url }}) Office (GAO) shows that the public budget display did not tell the whole story. A fiscal year 2025 research and development programme worth roughly **$1.3 billion**, identified as a special access programme (SAP), was omitted from the publicly released PDI budget exhibit because Department of Defense guidance indicated that SAPs should not be included. The case illustrates how a strategically significant programme can remain funded and visible to authorised oversight while disappearing from the public presentation that many observers rely upon to understand defence priorities.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://files.gao.gov/reports/GAO-26-107698/index.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: files.gao.gov">[files.gao.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">files.gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Clearer Guidance Is Needed to Improve Visibility into...Nov 25, 2025 — The Department of Defense&#x27;s (DOD) annual Pacific Deterrence Initi...</span></span></span>

## What the Pacific Deterrence exhibit was meant to show

Congress established the PDI as more than a funding label. It also required an annual budget exhibit intended to provide a coherent account of how the Department of Defense was investing in deterrence across the Indo-Pacific. The exhibit is designed to help legislators compare requested funding with strategic objectives, assess whether major capability gaps are being addressed, and track changes from year to year.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-26-107698" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.gov">[GAO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Defense Budget: Clearer Guidance Is Needed to Improve...... Budget: Clearer Guidance Is Needed to Improve Visibility into Resourcing...</span></span></span>

In principle, the public exhibit serves several audiences simultaneously:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--comparison" markdown="1">

* Congress, when reviewing annual appropriations.
* Analysts and researchers comparing defence priorities over time.
* Allies seeking reassurance about long-term US commitments.
* The public, which can observe broad investment trends without requiring access to classified information.

</div>

The expectation is not that every sensitive capability will be described in detail, but that the exhibit provides a meaningful representation of where resources are being directed.

## Why the SAP was excluded

The GAO found that one significant exception involved a next-generation national defence space architecture research and development programme designated as a special access programme. According to the report, approximately **$1.3 billion** associated with this effort appeared in the Indo-Pacific Command's independent assessment but was absent from the public PDI budget exhibit because existing Department of Defense guidance treated SAPs as programmes that should be excluded from the exhibit.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://files.gao.gov/reports/GAO-26-107698/index.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: files.gao.gov">[files.gao.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">files.gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Clearer Guidance Is Needed to Improve Visibility into...Nov 25, 2025 — The Department of Defense&#x27;s (DOD) annual Pacific Deterrence Initi...</span></span></span>

Importantly, the omission did **not** mean the programme lacked funding or congressional oversight. Rather, it reflected a distinction between:

* classified budget information available through restricted oversight channels; and
* publicly released budget exhibits intended to explain defence priorities.

The programme therefore remained part of actual defence planning while disappearing from one of the principal public documents used to understand PDI spending.

The GAO did not argue that the classified programme itself should be publicly disclosed. Instead, it highlighted that the exclusion affected the usefulness of the budget exhibit as a representation of actual Indo-Pacific resourcing.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://files.gao.gov/reports/GAO-26-107698/index.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: files.gao.gov">[files.gao.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">files.gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Clearer Guidance Is Needed to Improve Visibility into...Nov 25, 2025 — The Department of Defense&#x27;s (DOD) annual Pacific Deterrence Initi...</span></span></span>

## Why this mattered beyond one programme

The missing SAP became significant because of its scale. A billion-dollar programme is not a rounding error within a regional deterrence initiative. When spending of that magnitude is absent from the public presentation, readers comparing PDI totals with strategic requirements may reach incomplete conclusions about the balance of investment.

The omission also demonstrated that differences between the PDI budget exhibit and the Indo-Pacific Command's independent assessment were not explained solely by differing planning assumptions. GAO concluded that they also reflected differences in programme selection, including the treatment of classified activities. As a result, the published exhibit and the command's assessment portrayed different pictures of the resources supporting Indo-Pacific deterrence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://files.gao.gov/reports/GAO-26-107698/index.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: files.gao.gov">[files.gao.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">files.gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Clearer Guidance Is Needed to Improve Visibility into...Nov 25, 2025 — The Department of Defense&#x27;s (DOD) annual Pacific Deterrence Initi...</span></span></span>

This distinction matters because Congress established both reporting mechanisms to improve visibility into regional defence priorities. If major funded programmes are systematically excluded from one of them, the public-facing document inevitably provides only a partial account.

## What the omission reveals about budget transparency

The PDI case illustrates a broader feature of special access programme budgeting: transparency is often selective rather than absolute.

Several lessons emerge from the GAO's findings.

* **Public visibility is not equivalent to congressional visibility.** Sensitive programmes may be reported through classified mechanisms while remaining absent from publicly released budget exhibits.
* **Budget documents are shaped by reporting guidance.** The omission resulted from Department of Defense guidance on what should appear in the exhibit rather than from an absence of funding.
* **Large classified investments can alter the apparent distribution of priorities.** Even when aggregate spending is accurate, excluding individual programmes changes how readers interpret strategic emphasis.
* **Comparisons between public exhibits and operational planning documents require caution.** Apparent discrepancies may reflect reporting rules as much as changes in defence strategy.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://files.gao.gov/reports/GAO-26-107698/index.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: files.gao.gov">[files.gao.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">files.gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Clearer Guidance Is Needed to Improve Visibility into...Nov 25, 2025 — The Department of Defense&#x27;s (DOD) annual Pacific Deterrence Initi...</span></span></span>

## GAO's broader assessment

The missing SAP was one example within a wider review that found inconsistent programme selection across the PDI budget exhibits for fiscal years 2023 through 2025. GAO identified other inconsistencies involving facilities sustainment programmes, geographic scope and research projects included by different military departments. These variations, combined with exclusions such as the classified space programme, led GAO to conclude that the existing guidance did not provide Congress with the intended level of visibility into Indo-Pacific resourcing. The agency recommended that the Department of Defense revise its guidance to clarify programme selection criteria and better align the budget exhibit with funded priorities. The Department agreed with those recommendations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://files.gao.gov/reports/GAO-26-107698/index.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: files.gao.gov">[files.gao.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">files.gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Clearer Guidance Is Needed to Improve Visibility into...Nov 25, 2025 — The Department of Defense&#x27;s (DOD) annual Pacific Deterrence Initi...</span></span></span>

The Pacific Deterrence SAP therefore stands as a concrete illustration of a recurring challenge in defence budgeting. A programme can be strategically important, fully funded and subject to classified oversight while remaining invisible in the principal public exhibit that observers use to understand regional defence priorities.
