## Introduction

Cyber capability SAPs sit at the sharpest edge of special-access secrecy because digital tools can lose value as soon as enough is known about them. A classified aircraft, sensor or satellite may remain useful after its broad existence becomes public. A cyber capability often depends on a narrower secret: a software flaw not yet patched, a command-and-control method not yet detected, a target network path not yet closed, or a way of blending into normal system traffic. Once that detail is exposed, defenders, vendors and adversaries can all react quickly.

That is why cyber-related special-access-programmes are best understood not only as secret projects, but as time-sensitive stores of operational advantage. The secrecy question is not simply “would disclosure embarrass the government?” It is “would disclosure cause the capability to expire, help others copy it, burn a source of [intelligence]({{ 'intelligence/' | relative_url }}), or leave ordinary users less safe?” US policy acknowledges this tension directly: the government’s Vulnerabilities Equities Process says the public interest normally favours disclosure of software vulnerabilities, unless there is a demonstrable, overriding intelligence, law-enforcement or national-security reason to retain them.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://trumpwhitehouse.archives.gov/sites/whitehouse.gov/files/images/External%20-%20Unclassified%20VEP%20Charter%20FINAL.PDF" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: trumpwhitehouse.archives.gov">[Trump White House Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">trumpwhitehouse.archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Trump White House ArchivesVulnerabilities Equities Policy and ProcessNovember 14, 2017 — 15 Nov 2017 — The primary focus of this policy i...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 14, 2017</span></span></span>

## Why cyber secrets decay faster than many other SAP secrets

The special-access framework is built for information whose exposure could cause unusual damage. In the cyber domain, that damage can be immediate because the protected information may be inseparable from the technical conditions that make the operation possible. [DoD SAP policy]({{ 'do-d-policy/' | relative_url }}) treats SAPs as programmes requiring enhanced governance and oversight under Executive Order 13526, while separate SAP cybersecurity guidance applies to the networks and systems used by the SAP community.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/portals/54/documents/dd/issuances/dodd/520507p.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">dod directive 5205.07 special access program policySeptember 11, 2024 — 12 Sept 2024 — Maintains a single, authoritative repository fo...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 11, 2024</span></span></span>

The distinct feature of cyber capability SAPs is that secrecy can protect several fragile layers at once. The tool itself may matter, but so may the vulnerability it exploits, the environment where it works, the target’s software version, the operator’s infrastructure, and the intelligence picture that showed where to use it. Revealing any one of those layers may allow a target to patch, reconfigure, monitor, deceive or attribute future activity.

This makes exposure risk different from ordinary classification risk. A leaked document about a past diplomatic judgement may be damaging, but it does not necessarily destroy the underlying capacity to conduct diplomacy. A leaked cyber exploit can turn into a patch notice, a malware signature, a criminal tool, a foreign-intelligence opportunity, or a reason for the target to rebuild its network. The operational shelf life of the secret may collapse from years to days.

## Tools and target knowledge

A cyber capability is rarely just a piece of code. It is usually a bundle of tool, target knowledge and operational procedure. The tool may exploit a vulnerability; the target knowledge explains where that vulnerability exists; and the method explains how to reach the system without being noticed. For a SAP, the most sensitive fact may be any one of those elements.

This is why public policy debates around zero-day vulnerabilities are so relevant to cyber SAPs. A zero-day is a software flaw unknown to the vendor or public, giving an attacker a temporary advantage because normal patching and detection may not yet exist. RAND’s study of more than 200 zero-day vulnerabilities and exploits was designed to inform precisely this kind of policy problem: whether to retain or disclose vulnerabilities, how long they may remain useful, and what risk is created by stockpiling them.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.rand.org/pubs/research_reports/RR1751.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: rand.org">[RAND Corporation]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">rand.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">RAND CorporationZero Days, Thousands of Nights: The Life and Times...9 Mar 2017 — This report provides findings from real-world zero-day...</span></span></span>

The White House Vulnerabilities Equities Process places this dilemma in plain terms. It says US government-discovered vulnerabilities should generally be disclosed to improve cybersecurity, but allows retention when an overriding mission need justifies continued use. That is the policy version of the SAP exposure problem: the same vulnerability that enables a lawful intelligence or military mission may also endanger civilian systems if it remains unpatched or escapes control.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://trumpwhitehouse.archives.gov/sites/whitehouse.gov/files/images/External%20-%20Unclassified%20VEP%20Charter%20FINAL.PDF" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: trumpwhitehouse.archives.gov">[Trump White House Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">trumpwhitehouse.archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Trump White House ArchivesVulnerabilities Equities Policy and ProcessNovember 14, 2017 — 15 Nov 2017 — The primary focus of this policy i...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 14, 2017</span></span></span>

Target knowledge can be just as sensitive as the tool. If an adversary learns that a particular ministry, weapons system, router family, cloud service, industrial controller or contractor network is being used as an access point, it can close the route without ever seeing the exploit. In that sense, a cyber SAP may be protecting a map as much as a weapon.

## Exposure can turn a capability into a public hazard

The strongest public example of exposure risk is the Shadow Brokers leak of NSA-linked tools, especially EternalBlue. EternalBlue exploited a Microsoft Windows Server Message Block vulnerability and was publicly leaked in 2017. Microsoft had issued a patch before the most destructive attacks, but many systems remained unpatched; the exploit was then used in WannaCry, NotPetya and later criminal and state-linked activity.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.wired.com/story/eternalblue-leaked-nsa-spy-tool-hacked-world" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: wired.com">[WIRED]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">wired.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Leaked NSA Spy Tool That Hacked the World</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Leaked NSA Spy Tool That Hacked the World</span></span></span>

The case matters for cyber SAP analysis because it shows three separate harms from exposure. First, the originating capability was burned: once the exploit became public, vendors and defenders could detect and patch against it. Second, the capability diffused: other actors could study, adapt or reuse it. Third, the public risk persisted: patch availability did not mean patch deployment, especially across hospitals, local governments, industrial environments and legacy systems.

Security researchers and journalists have since treated EternalBlue as a cautionary example of how government-held exploits can become general-purpose attack infrastructure after disclosure or loss of control. Check Point’s timeline of Shadow Brokers-related attacks noted the rapid appearance of follow-on activity after the leaked tools became available, including WannaCry and later exploit suites using related techniques.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://research.checkpoint.com/2017/brokers-shadows-analyzing-vulnerabilities-attacks-spawned-leaked-nsa-hacking-tools/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: research.checkpoint.com">[Check Point Research]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">research.checkpoint.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Check Point Research BROKERS IN THE SHADOWS: Analyzing vulnerabilities</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Check Point Research BROKERS IN THE SHADOWS: Analyzing vulnerabilities</span></span></span>

The lesson is not that governments can never hold cyber tools. It is that cyber capability secrecy carries a dual-use burden. A retained vulnerability may serve intelligence or defence objectives, but it may also leave everyone using the affected technology exposed until the flaw is fixed. The public debate is therefore not only about secrecy from adversaries; it is also about risk transferred to ordinary networks.

## Operational vulnerability

Cyber [operations]({{ 'operations/' | relative_url }}) are unusually vulnerable to feedback. Once a tool is used, it may leave traces: logs, malware samples, network indicators, error messages, unusual authentication patterns, or forensic artefacts. Even without a leak, the operation itself can teach the target how it was reached. Exposure can therefore come from disclosure, capture, reverse engineering, defensive monitoring, vendor analysis, or another state silently observing the operation.

That is why US Cyber Command’s public “defend forward” and “[hunt forward]({{ 'hunt-forward/' | relative_url }})” language is useful context. USCYBERCOM describes hunt-forward operations as deployments, at a partner country’s invitation, in which operators search for malicious cyber activity and vulnerabilities on host-nation networks; it also says findings from such operations may be shared publicly.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cybercom.mil/Media/News/Article/3198878/cyber-101-defend-forward-and-persistent-engagement/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cybercom.mil">[cybercom.mil]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cybercom.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">CYBE R 101</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">CYBE R 101</span></span></span> The 2023 DoD Cyber Strategy similarly describes using outward-facing capabilities to identify and mitigate threats before they harm the United States.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://media.defense.gov/2023/Sep/12/2003299076/-1/-1/1/2023_DOD_Cyber_Strategy_Summary.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: media.defense.gov">[U.S. Department of War]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">media.defense.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on defense.gov.</span></span></span>

This creates a revealing contrast. Some cyber knowledge is deliberately exposed to improve defence: malware indicators, adversary tactics, vulnerable infrastructure and mitigation advice. CISA’s cybersecurity advisories are built around this model, publishing threat actor tactics, techniques, procedures and indicators so defenders can act.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/cybersecurity-advisories" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cisa.gov">[CISA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cisa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cisa.gov.</span></span></span> But SAP-protected offensive or intelligence capabilities may depend on withholding comparable detail because public release would enable targets to close the same openings.

The tension is not hypocrisy; it is the operating logic of the domain. Cyber defence benefits from fast sharing. Cyber exploitation often benefits from silence. A cyber SAP may therefore sit inside a constant policy conflict between protecting a sensitive access route and warning the wider ecosystem about a weakness.

## The oversight problem when the same secret is also a public risk

For waived and [unacknowledged SAPs]({{ 'unacknowledged/' | relative_url }}), the public may not know a programme exists at all. In the cyber setting, that lack of visibility is especially hard to evaluate because the external effects may be invisible until something goes wrong. A retained exploit may produce valuable intelligence for years, or it may be independently discovered by another actor tomorrow. A tool may be tightly controlled, or it may be copied, captured or leaked. Outside the compartment, those probabilities are difficult to judge.

The VEP is one public attempt to put structure around this problem. Its core question is whether to disclose a vulnerability to the vendor or retain it for authorised government use. The 2017 charter says disclosure should serve cybersecurity and protect core internet infrastructure, critical infrastructure and the US economy, absent an overriding mission reason for retention.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://trumpwhitehouse.archives.gov/sites/whitehouse.gov/files/images/External%20-%20Unclassified%20VEP%20Charter%20FINAL.PDF" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: trumpwhitehouse.archives.gov">[Trump White House Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">trumpwhitehouse.archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Trump White House ArchivesVulnerabilities Equities Policy and ProcessNovember 14, 2017 — 15 Nov 2017 — The primary focus of this policy i...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 14, 2017</span></span></span> Lawfare’s analysis of vulnerability equities processes describes the broader international trend: governments have begun formalising procedures because vulnerability retention affects both offensive capability and public defence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.lawfaremedia.org/article/future-vulnerabilities-equities-processes-around-world" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Lawfare">[Lawfaremedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Lawfare</span><span class="citation-popover-title">future vulnerabilities equities processes around world</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">future vulnerabilities equities processes around world</span></span></span>

For cyber SAPs, this means oversight should not ask only whether access is properly restricted. It must also ask whether the programme has a credible answer to four risk questions:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--caution" markdown="1">

* **What happens if the tool is captured?** A digital exploit can be copied perfectly, reverse-engineered and redeployed by others.
* **Who else is exposed while the flaw remains secret?** The affected systems may include allies, companies, hospitals or critical infrastructure, not just an adversary target.
* **How quickly would the capability expire if used or revealed?** Some accesses survive contact; others are one-shot opportunities.
* **Who can force reconsideration?** A SAP compartment can make it harder for outside cybersecurity, legal, civil-liberties or infrastructure-risk officials to challenge retention decisions.

</div>

The hardest cases are not the obviously reckless ones. They are the cases where the intelligence gain is real, the public risk is diffuse, and the people who bear the risk do not know the vulnerability exists.

## Limits of compartmenting digital work

Compartmenting is essential for cyber SAPs, but it cannot fully solve cyber exposure risk. Traditional SAP controls restrict who can know the programme, enter the facility, access the system or handle the material. DoD guidance for SAP systems applies risk-management, assessment and authorisation practices to the information systems used by the SAP community.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.dcsa.mil/Portals/91/Documents/CTP/tools/DCSA%20Assessment%20and%20Authorization%20Process%20Manual%20Version%202.2.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dcsa.mil">[DCSA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dcsa.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Assessment and Authorization Process Manual</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Assessment and Authorization Process Manual</span></span></span> These controls reduce insider, contractor, network and handling risk, but they do not change the basic nature of digital artefacts.

A cyber tool can be copied at negligible cost. It can be discovered from forensic residue. It can be reconstructed from behaviour. It can be neutralised by a vendor patch. It can also be overtaken by normal technology change: a software update, cloud migration, endpoint-security product or network redesign may render a carefully guarded capability obsolete without any public leak.

Compartmenting also creates coordination costs. Cyber operations depend on engineers, operators, lawyers, intelligence analysts, infrastructure experts, vendors, military commanders and sometimes foreign partners. Over-compartmenting can protect the secret while narrowing the pool of people able to spot collateral risk, [duplication]({{ 'duplication/' | relative_url }}), technical fragility or a safer defensive alternative. Under-compartmenting can expose the tool too widely. The governance challenge is to make the compartment large enough for competent risk judgement and small enough to preserve the capability.

This is where cyber SAPs differ from many hardware-focused SAPs. In a traditional weapons programme, security may be designed around preventing adversaries from learning performance characteristics or design details. In a cyber capability SAP, security must also anticipate that the act of operation may itself disclose the method. The programme is not merely protecting a finished asset; it is managing a perishable relationship between secrecy, access and target behaviour.

## What a balanced cyber SAP posture looks like

A balanced approach does not treat cyber SAP secrecy as either inherently illegitimate or automatically justified. It begins from a narrower claim: some cyber capabilities genuinely require SAP-level protection because revealing a tool, target or method can immediately destroy the capability and help adversaries. At the same time, cyber secrecy can externalise risk onto the wider digital ecosystem in ways that ordinary classification debates often miss.

The most defensible posture has three features. First, it uses SAP controls to protect truly fragile operational knowledge, not to avoid embarrassment or ordinary [accountability]({{ 'accountability/' | relative_url }}). Second, it builds in recurring review, because a vulnerability that was worth retaining six months ago may no longer justify the same risk after a vendor update, adversary discovery, partner exposure or change in mission value. Third, it connects SAP oversight to vulnerability-disclosure and defensive-warning processes, so the secrecy system does not become isolated from the people responsible for protecting the public networked world.

Cyber capability SAPs therefore illustrate the central paradox of waived and unacknowledged secrecy more sharply than almost any other category. The case for secrecy can be very strong, because exposure may burn a capability instantly. The case for oversight is just as strong, because the same secret may be a latent risk to everyone who depends on the vulnerable technology.

    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var picker = section.querySelector('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
    if (menu) menu.hidden = true;
    if (trigger) trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', 'false');
    if (picker) picker.classList.remove('fr-ebay-market-picker--open');
  }
  function availableMarkets(section) {
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      return Array.prototype.slice.call(select.options)
        .map(function (option) { return option.value; })
        .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
    }
    return Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]'))
      .map(function (button) { return button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'); })
      .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
  }
  function applyMarket(section, marketId, persist) {
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    if (available.indexOf(marketId)< 0) marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-localized-link]')).forEach(function (link) {
      var query = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-query') || '';
      var reference = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-reference') || '';
      link.setAttribute('href', buildUrl(query, reference, marketId));
    });
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select && available.indexOf(marketId) >= 0) select.value = marketId;
    var selectedText = marketId;
    if (select && select.selectedIndex >= 0) selectedText = select.options[select.selectedIndex].text;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-flag], [data-ebay-trigger-market-flag]')).forEach(function (flag) {
      flag.className = flagClass(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-trigger-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-domain-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = domainLabel(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.setAttribute('aria-selected', button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option') === marketId ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;
      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
})();
