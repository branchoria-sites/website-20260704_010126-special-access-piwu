## Introduction

Many people speak about “TS/SCI” as though it were a security clearance above Top Secret. That shorthand is widespread in job advertisements, news reports and popular culture, but it is technically incorrect. In the United States classification system, **Top Secret is the highest formal classification level**. “TS/SCI” instead describes a combination of two different concepts: eligibility to access **Top Secret** information and separate approval to access **Sensitive Compartmented Information (SCI)**, which is protected through specialised access-control systems rather than a higher classification level.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.odni.gov/files/documents/ICD/ICD-703.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: odni.gov">[ODNI]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">odni.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">25, 2023 — 21 Jun 2013 — EO 13526; The DNI is to establish uniform standards and procedures for granting of access to sensitive compartme...</span></span></span>

This distinction matters when comparing [intelligence]({{ 'intelligence/' | relative_url }}) [compartments]({{ 'compartments/' | relative_url }}) with Special Access Programs (SAPs). Both systems impose additional access controls beyond an individual's clearance, but neither creates a fourth classification level. They are mechanisms for restricting who may see particularly sensitive information, not for inventing a new rank above Top Secret.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.odni.gov/files/documents/ICD/ICD-703.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: odni.gov">[ODNI]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">odni.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">25, 2023 — 21 Jun 2013 — EO 13526; The DNI is to establish uniform standards and procedures for granting of access to sensitive compartme...</span></span></span>

## What classification level actually means

Classification levels measure the potential damage to national security if information is disclosed without authorisation. Under the U.S. classification system established by executive order, there are only three primary national security classification levels:

* **Confidential**
* **Secret**
* **Top Secret**

Top Secret is reserved for information whose unauthorised disclosure could reasonably be expected to cause exceptionally grave damage to national security. Executive Order 13526 does not establish a higher level above Top Secret. Instead, it allows agencies to apply additional access controls where ordinary classification alone is considered insufficient.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.odni.gov/files/documents/ICD/ICD-703.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: odni.gov">[ODNI]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">odni.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">25, 2023 — 21 Jun 2013 — EO 13526; The DNI is to establish uniform standards and procedures for granting of access to sensitive compartme...</span></span></span>

That means a document can be Top Secret without belonging to any compartment, while another document may be Top Secret and also require SCI access or SAP approval before it can be viewed. The classification indicates the sensitivity of the information; the compartment determines who is authorised to receive it.

## How SCI eligibility and approval differ

SCI is frequently misunderstood because it is discussed alongside [Top Secret clearances]({{ 'clearance-gap/' | relative_url }}). Intelligence Community Directive 703 defines Sensitive Compartmented Information as classified national intelligence concerning or derived from intelligence sources, methods or analytical processes that must be protected within formal access-control systems established by the Director of National Intelligence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.odni.gov/files/documents/ICD/ICD-703.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: odni.gov">[ODNI]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">odni.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">25, 2023 — 21 Jun 2013 — EO 13526; The DNI is to establish uniform standards and procedures for granting of access to sensitive compartme...</span></span></span>

The process therefore has several distinct steps:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--decision" markdown="1">

* A person is found eligible for a Top Secret clearance through the personnel security process.
* If their duties require intelligence protected within SCI control systems, they must also be determined eligible for SCI access under Intelligence Community rules.
* They must then be formally granted access to the particular SCI control system or compartment they need for their work.
* Even after that approval, access remains limited by the "need to know" principle.

</div>

Importantly, SCI eligibility does **not** automatically grant access to every intelligence compartment. Individuals are typically "read into" only those compartments necessary for their assigned duties, and one SCI compartment does not automatically confer access to another.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.dni.gov/files/documents/ICD/ICD-704-Personnel-Security-Standards-and-Procedures-for-Access-to-SCI-2018-06-20.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dni.gov">[Director of National Intelligence]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dni.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ICD 704 Personnel Security Standards and Procedures for Access to SCI</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The DNI establishes eligibility standards for access to SCI. temporary access shall not exceed one...</span></span></span>

This layered approach is similar to SAP administration. In both cases, possessing the necessary clearance is only the starting point; specific programme or compartment approval remains essential.

## Why "TS/SCI" creates confusion

The expression "TS/SCI" appears so often that many people assume it is the name of a single clearance level. Several practical factors reinforce that misunderstanding.

First, employers often advertise positions requiring "active TS/SCI" because applicants need both Top Secret eligibility and existing SCI access to begin work quickly. From a hiring perspective, combining the terms is convenient even though they refer to different elements of the security system.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.intelligencecareers.gov/dia/security-clearance-process" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: intelligencecareers.gov">[Intelligence Careers]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">intelligencecareers.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Intelligence CareersSecurity Clearance ProcessAll potential employees must: Obtain a Top Secret/Sensitive Compartmented Information (TS/S...</span></span></span>

Second, many intelligence organisations conduct most of their work inside Sensitive Compartmented Information Facilities (SCIFs), where SCI is routinely handled. Employees therefore encounter "TS/SCI" far more often than plain collateral Top Secret information, making the combined label seem like a distinct status rather than a combination of clearance and access.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://clearedjobs.net/guides/security-clearance-levels" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: clearedjobs.net">[ClearedJobs.Net]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">clearedjobs.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Security Clearance Levels Explained: Confidential, Secret...TS/SCI is the highest standard clearance level, required by most intelligenc...</span></span></span>

Finally, media reporting frequently describes especially sensitive intelligence as being "above Top Secret". Although this phrase is memorable, it obscures how the classification system actually works. ## Why this distinction matters when comparing SCI and SAPs

Confusing TS/SCI with a higher clearance rank can also distort comparisons between SCI and Special Access Programs.

Both SCI and SAPs add restrictions beyond ordinary classified handling:

* They require explicit approval in addition to an individual's clearance.
* They rely on formal [access lists]({{ 'access-lists/' | relative_url }}) and need-to-know determinations.

<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--comparison" markdown="1">

* They often involve additional security briefings and handling procedures.
* Access is specific to particular compartments or programmes rather than universal.

</div>

Neither system changes the underlying classification level. A Top Secret SAP remains Top Secret information protected by additional programme controls. Likewise, Top Secret SCI remains Top Secret information managed within intelligence compartment systems established by the Director of National Intelligence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.odni.gov/files/documents/ICD/ICD-703.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: odni.gov">[ODNI]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">odni.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">25, 2023 — 21 Jun 2013 — EO 13526; The DNI is to establish uniform standards and procedures for granting of access to sensitive compartme...</span></span></span>

Recognising this distinction helps explain why someone may legitimately possess a Top Secret clearance yet still be unable to view particular intelligence reporting or participate in a specific SAP. The deciding factor is not whether they have a mythical clearance "above Top Secret", but whether they have been authorised for that particular access channel.

## The practical takeaway

The simplest way to avoid the common misconception is to separate three different ideas:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **Classification level** answers: *How damaging would unauthorised disclosure be?*
* **SCI or SAP approval** answers: *Has this person been authorised for this protected information channel?*
* **Need to know** answers: *Does this person require this specific information to perform their duties?*

</div>
