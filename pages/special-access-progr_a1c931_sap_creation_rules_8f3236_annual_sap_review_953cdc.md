## Introduction

Special access programmes (SAPs) are not intended to remain in force indefinitely simply because they were once justified. Under the framework established by Executive Order 13526, a SAP exists only while enhanced access restrictions remain necessary to protect exceptionally vulnerable classified information. Annual review therefore serves as the programme's principal revalidation checkpoint: an opportunity to test whether the original reasons for imposing extraordinary controls still exist, whether the access restrictions remain proportionate, and whether the programme should continue, be modified, or be terminated.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/isoo/policy-documents/cnsi-eo.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesThe President Executive Order 13526August 15, 2016 — 29 Dec 2009 — This order prescribes a uniform system for classifyin...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 15, 2016</span></span></span>

This recurring review is an important governance safeguard. Technologies mature, military [operations]({{ 'operations/' | relative_url }}) conclude, [intelligence]({{ 'intelligence/' | relative_url }}) sources change, and adversaries develop new capabilities. A compartment that was essential during one stage of a programme may become unnecessary later. Annual revalidation is designed to ensure that special access remains an exception rather than becoming a permanent administrative habit.

## What annual revalidation asks

Executive Order 13526 requires agencies that establish SAPs to review them annually and determine whether each programme continues to satisfy the standards that justified its creation. Rather than assuming that enhanced controls should continue automatically, the review revisits the core legal tests that authorised the SAP in the first place.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/isoo/policy-documents/cnsi-eo.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesThe President Executive Order 13526August 15, 2016 — 29 Dec 2009 — This order prescribes a uniform system for classifyin...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 15, 2016</span></span></span>

The central questions include:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Does the information still face an exceptional vulnerability or threat?
* Are ordinary classification controls still insufficient?
* Is the enhanced access regime still proportionate?
* Does the programme continue to involve a reasonably limited access population?
* Have changes in mission, technology or operational circumstances reduced the need for extraordinary compartmentation?<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/isoo/policy-documents/cnsi-eo.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesThe President Executive Order 13526August 15, 2016 — 29 Dec 2009 — This order prescribes a uniform system for classifyin...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 15, 2016</span></span></span>

</div>

These questions are intended to force a fresh judgement rather than a routine administrative renewal. The existence of a SAP in previous years is not, by itself, evidence that it remains justified.

## Why SAP justifications can expire

The rationale for creating a SAP is tied to specific risks, not to the programme's prestige or budget. Those risks frequently evolve.

For example, a capability under development may require unusually strict compartmentation because disclosure would reveal a technological advantage before deployment. Once that capability becomes operational, publicly observable, or integrated into wider military planning, the original vulnerability may change significantly. Likewise, an intelligence operation may require extraordinary protection while active but not after the operation has concluded or its methods have changed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/isoo/policy-documents/cnsi-eo.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesThe President Executive Order 13526August 15, 2016 — 29 Dec 2009 — This order prescribes a uniform system for classifyin...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 15, 2016</span></span></span>

Annual review therefore asks whether the original protection problem still exists in substantially the same form. If not, agencies should consider reducing or eliminating the additional access controls rather than preserving them simply because they have become familiar.

This approach reflects a broader principle within Executive Order 13526: classification and special access are both intended to match current national security needs, not historical circumstances.

## How review separates classification from special access

One of the most important functions of annual revalidation is distinguishing between information that should remain classified and information that still requires special access controls.

These are separate decisions.

Information may continue to warrant classification at the Secret or Top Secret level because disclosure would still damage national security. That alone does not necessarily justify maintaining a SAP. A SAP imposes restrictions beyond ordinary classification, so the review examines whether those additional restrictions continue to address an exceptional protection problem rather than merely protecting sensitive information.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cdse.edu/Portals/124/Documents/student-guides/SA001-guide.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cdse.edu">[CDSE]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cdse.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">SA001 guide</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Student Guide Course: Special Access Program (SAP)...Executive Order 13526, “Classified National Security Information,” defines a SA...</span></span></span>

As a result, several outcomes are possible during review:

* **Classification and SAP both remain justified.** The exceptional vulnerability continues, so enhanced compartmentation stays in place.
* **Classification remains, but SAP ends.** Information continues to require protection, but standard classified-information controls are now considered sufficient.
* **[SAP structure]({{ 'sap-structure/' | relative_url }}) changes.** Access boundaries, [compartments]({{ 'compartments/' | relative_url }}) or participating organisations may be adjusted to better match current operational needs.
* **Programme concludes.** If the underlying mission has ended, the SAP itself may no longer have a continuing purpose even though some associated records remain classified.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/isoo/policy-documents/cnsi-eo.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesThe President Executive Order 13526August 15, 2016 — 29 Dec 2009 — This order prescribes a uniform system for classifyin...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 15, 2016</span></span></span>

The review therefore protects against treating "special access" as though it were simply a higher classification level, which Executive Order 13526 explicitly does not create.

## Oversight and accountability

Annual review also supports oversight beyond the programme itself. Executive Order 13526 requires agencies to maintain [accountability]({{ 'accountability/' | relative_url }}) for their SAPs, and the Information Security Oversight Office ([ISOO]({{ 'isoo/' | relative_url }})) is granted authority to access SAPs in carrying out its oversight responsibilities. This helps ensure that agencies periodically justify continued compartmentation instead of allowing programmes to persist without scrutiny.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/isoo/policy-documents/cnsi-eo.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesThe President Executive Order 13526August 15, 2016 — 29 Dec 2009 — This order prescribes a uniform system for classifyin...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 15, 2016</span></span></span>

Within the Department of Defense, current policy assigns the DoD Special Access Program Central Office (SAPCO) responsibility for coordinating guidance on annual SAP reviews and associated reporting under Executive Order 13526 and applicable congressional requirements. This institutionalises the annual review process within broader governance and reporting systems rather than treating it as an isolated security exercise.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/portals/54/documents/dd/issuances/dodd/520507p.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">dod directive 5205.07 special access program policy12 Sept 2024 — Coordinates and provides guidance for annual SAP reviews and reports...</span></span></span>

Although detailed review documentation for individual SAPs is generally classified, the governing policy demonstrates that annual review is intended to be a recurring management requirement with defined organisational responsibilities.

## Why annual review matters

Without periodic revalidation, special access programmes could gradually become permanent by default. Personnel turnover, organisational inertia and the understandable tendency to avoid reducing security controls all create pressure for compartments to continue long after their original justification has weakened.

Annual review counters that tendency by requiring decision-makers to reconnect present-day security measures with present-day risks. It reinforces that a SAP is justified only when ordinary classification protections are demonstrably inadequate, not simply because the programme has historically operated within a compartmented environment.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/isoo/policy-documents/cnsi-eo.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[archives.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesThe President Executive Order 13526August 15, 2016 — 29 Dec 2009 — This order prescribes a uniform system for classifyin...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 15, 2016</span></span></span>

In that sense, the annual review is more than an administrative requirement. It is the principal mechanism that keeps special access tied to current evidence, changing operational realities and the continuing legal standards established by Executive Order 13526.
