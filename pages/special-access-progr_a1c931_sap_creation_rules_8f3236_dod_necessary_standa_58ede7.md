## Introduction

Within the executive-order framework for creating Special Access Programs (SAPs), the Department of Defense (DoD) applies an even narrower policy test. Rather than treating SAPs as an enhanced form of ordinary classification, DoD policy states that they should be established and maintained only when **absolutely necessary** to protect the Department's most sensitive capabilities, technologies, information and [operations]({{ 'operations/' | relative_url }}), or when required by law. This higher threshold reflects the practical reality that every SAP imposes significant administrative, operational and oversight costs, making compartmentation a last-resort security measure rather than a routine management tool.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/portals/54/documents/dd/issuances/dodd/520507p.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">dod directive 5205.07 special access program policySeptember 11, 2024 — 12 Sept 2024 — Purpose: This issuance: • Establishes policy, a...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 11, 2024</span></span></span>

The result is a two-stage standard. Executive Order 13526 provides the government-wide legal basis by requiring exceptional vulnerability and the inadequacy of normal classified controls. DoD policy then tightens that principle by directing programme sponsors to justify why only the most restrictive protection regime will adequately safeguard a military capability or operation. The emphasis is not on creating more secrecy, but on reserving SAP protections for situations where conventional classified information procedures cannot realistically manage the risk.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/portals/54/documents/dd/issuances/dodd/520507p.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">dod directive 5205.07 special access program policySeptember 11, 2024 — 12 Sept 2024 — Purpose: This issuance: • Establishes policy, a...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 11, 2024</span></span></span>

## How DoD Narrows the Executive-Order Test

Executive Order 13526 permits SAPs only when ordinary classification rules are insufficient to protect exceptionally vulnerable information. DoD policy deliberately narrows that discretion. The current Special Access Program Policy states that DoD SAPs are to be established and maintained **"when absolutely necessary"** to protect the Department's most sensitive capabilities, information, technologies and operations, or where [statute]({{ 'statute/' | relative_url }}) specifically requires special treatment.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/portals/54/documents/dd/issuances/dodd/520507p.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">dod directive 5205.07 special access program policySeptember 11, 2024 — 12 Sept 2024 — Purpose: This issuance: • Establishes policy, a...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 11, 2024</span></span></span>

This wording matters because it changes the practical question facing decision-makers. Instead of asking whether additional security might be useful, programme sponsors must demonstrate that enhanced compartmentation is indispensable. A programme cannot justify SAP status merely because it is expensive, politically significant or classified at the Top Secret level. The burden is to show that ordinary security controls leave an unacceptable residual risk.

The policy also reflects reforms intended to reduce unnecessary compartmentation across the Department. Modern DoD governance places SAP approval within a formal oversight structure involving the Secretary or Deputy Secretary of Defense, the DoD Special Access Program Central Office (SAPCO), and senior review bodies. The objective is to ensure that SAP designation remains an exceptional management decision rather than an organisational preference.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/portals/54/documents/dd/issuances/dodd/520507p.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">dod directive 5205.07 special access program policySeptember 11, 2024 — 12 Sept 2024 — Purpose: This issuance: • Establishes policy, a...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 11, 2024</span></span></span>

## When Collateral Classified Controls Are Judged Insufficient

The phrase "absolutely necessary" has practical meaning only when compared with the security measures already available through the ordinary classified information system.

Collateral classified information already benefits from multiple layers of protection, including personnel security clearances, need-to-know restrictions, physical security standards, document [accountability]({{ 'accountability/' | relative_url }}), classified networks, insider-threat programmes and counter-[intelligence]({{ 'intelligence/' | relative_url }}) measures. SAP protections are intended only after these mechanisms are judged inadequate for a particular class of information.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/DD/issuances/dodm/520507m1.PDF" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">DoDM 5205.07, &quot;Special Access Program Security Manual...17 Jan 2025 — Administers the National Industrial Security Program (NISP) fo...</span></span></span>

Examples of situations where ordinary controls may fail include:

* disclosure of developmental military technologies whose existence alone would reveal a decisive operational advantage;
* operational plans where even limited knowledge outside a tightly controlled circle could compromise missions or personnel;
* integrated programmes in which seemingly harmless fragments of information could allow an adversary to reconstruct an entire capability;
* programmes facing unusually sophisticated foreign intelligence targeting that exceeds the protection expected from standard collateral controls.

The key point is that DoD policy focuses on the failure of existing safeguards, not simply on the sensitivity of the information itself. SAP status is therefore intended to solve a specific protection problem rather than to elevate the prestige of a programme.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://securityawareness.dcsa.mil/cdse/multimedia/shorts/sap-sec-incident/common/scenarios/industry_day/common/files/resources/5205.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: securityawareness.dcsa.mil">[securityawareness.dcsa.mil]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">securityawareness.dcsa.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">DoDM 5205.07, Volume 1, &quot;DoD Special Access Program (...18 Jun 2015 — It is DoD policy according to Reference (b) that DoD SAPs be estab...</span></span></span>

## Why Advanced Capabilities and Operations Dominate SAP Use

The wording of DoD policy explains why SAPs are concentrated around advanced military capabilities rather than across the wider defence establishment.

Modern military advantage increasingly depends on capabilities whose value derives from remaining unknown until employed. Examples include advanced weapons, specialised intelligence collection systems, unique operational methods and sensitive research programmes. If an adversary gains early knowledge of these capabilities, the strategic advantage they provide may be reduced long before they enter operational service.

For that reason, DoD policy specifically identifies the protection of sensitive **capabilities, technologies and operations**, rather than simply classified documents. The focus is on preserving military effectiveness by limiting knowledge to those individuals whose duties require access.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/portals/54/documents/dd/issuances/dodd/520507p.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">dod directive 5205.07 special access program policySeptember 11, 2024 — 12 Sept 2024 — Purpose: This issuance: • Establishes policy, a...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 11, 2024</span></span></span>

This also explains why many [acquisition]({{ 'acquisition/' | relative_url }}) programmes transition through different security arrangements over time. Early research and development may require SAP protection because disclosure would reveal unique technological breakthroughs. As systems mature, enter wider production or become publicly acknowledged, some information may migrate into ordinary classified channels while only the most sensitive elements remain compartmented. The policy therefore treats SAP status as responsive to operational necessity rather than as a permanent characteristic.

## Last-Resort Protection Rather Than a Higher Classification

A common misunderstanding is that SAPs create a classification level above Top Secret. DoD policy does not support that interpretation.

Information protected within a SAP still carries one of the standard national security classification levels—Confidential, Secret or Top Secret. The distinguishing feature is the addition of narrowly tailored access controls, enhanced administrative procedures and compartmentation beyond the normal classified information system. Training and policy guidance consistently emphasise that SAPs supplement, rather than replace, the existing classification structure.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cdse.edu/Training/Toolkits/Special-Access-Program-Toolkit/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cdse.edu">[CDSE]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cdse.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Special Access Program (SAP) ToolkitThis toolkit will point to the resources you need to perform your role in a Special Access Progra...</span></span></span>

The "absolutely necessary" standard reinforces this distinction. Since SAPs are not intended as a superior classification level, they cannot be justified merely because information is highly sensitive. Instead, officials must establish that conventional classification protections—even when properly implemented—cannot adequately mitigate the identified threat.

## Why the Standard Matters for Oversight

Restricting SAPs to situations of absolute necessity serves both security and governance.

From a security perspective, limiting compartmentation concentrates protective resources on programmes facing the greatest risk of espionage or operational compromise. Excessive compartmentation can dilute attention, complicate information sharing and create unnecessary barriers to mission execution.

From an oversight perspective, the standard discourages the proliferation of programmes hidden behind specialised administrative structures. Because SAPs receive distinctive governance, reporting and review procedures, limiting their use helps preserve accountability while still allowing the Department to protect genuinely exceptional military capabilities. Successive DoD directives and manuals therefore frame SAP protection as an extraordinary tool to be used only when ordinary classified information controls have demonstrably reached their limits.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/portals/54/documents/dd/issuances/dodd/520507p.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">dod directive 5205.07 special access program policySeptember 11, 2024 — 12 Sept 2024 — Purpose: This issuance: • Establishes policy, a...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 11, 2024</span></span></span>
