## Introduction

A new Department of Defense (DoD) Special Access Program (SAP) cannot simply be activated as soon as officials decide that extraordinary security controls are needed. Under federal law, the Department must first provide written notice to the congressional defence committees, and a statutory waiting period must expire before the programme may be initiated. This requirement is one of the most important governance mechanisms in the SAP system because it ensures that even highly classified programmes enter a formal oversight process before they begin. Although [Congress]({{ 'congress/' | relative_url }}) is not asked to approve every new SAP, the law gives the relevant committees time to review the proposal, ask questions, and, if necessary, use their legislative or appropriations powers before the programme becomes operational.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&amp;num=0&amp;req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title10-section119" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uscode.house.gov">[U.S. Code]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uscode.house.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Code10 USC 119: Special access programs: congressional...A special access program may not be initiated until- (1) the defense committees...</span></span></span>

## What notice is required before a new SAP starts?

The governing [statute]({{ 'statute/' | relative_url }}), **10 U.S.C. § 119**, establishes a straightforward rule: a DoD special access programme may not be initiated until two conditions have been met:

* the congressional defence committees have been notified of the programme; and
* **30 days** have elapsed after delivery of that notification.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&amp;num=0&amp;req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title10-section119" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uscode.house.gov">[U.S. Code]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uscode.house.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Code10 USC 119: Special access programs: congressional...A special access program may not be initiated until- (1) the defense committees...</span></span></span>

Current DoD implementation guidance mirrors this statutory requirement. The 2024 edition of DoD Instruction 5205.11 specifies that a **new SAP umbrella**—the top-level organisational structure under which [compartments]({{ 'compartments/' | relative_url }}) and sub-compartments may later be created—cannot be established until the Director of the DoD Special Access Program Central Office (SAPCO) has notified the congressional defence committees **in writing** and 30 calendar days have passed after the notice is delivered.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/DD/issuances/dodi/520511p.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[WHS ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">WHS ESDDoD Instruction 5205.11, &quot;Management, Administration...12 Sept 2024 — A new SAP umbrella may not be created until the Director...</span></span></span>

The distinction between a new SAP umbrella and later internal organisational changes is important. Once an umbrella exists, DoD policy allows additional capabilities or information to be placed within the existing SAP architecture through internal governance processes where appropriate. The statutory waiting period is specifically tied to establishing a new SAP rather than every subsequent administrative adjustment.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/DD/issuances/dodi/520511p.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[WHS ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">WHS ESDDoD Instruction 5205.11, &quot;Management, Administration...12 Sept 2024 — A new SAP umbrella may not be created until the Director...</span></span></span>

## Why the 30-day waiting period matters

The waiting period does not amount to a congressional veto. Instead, it creates a mandatory pause between executive decision-making and programme initiation.

During those 30 days, the Armed Services and Appropriations Committees of both the House and Senate—the statutory "congressional defence committees"—have an opportunity to:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--checklist" markdown="1">

* examine the justification for creating a new SAP;
* assess whether the proposed level of compartmentation appears necessary;
* review funding implications through classified budget materials;
* request additional classified briefings; and
* consider whether legislative or appropriations action is warranted.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&amp;num=0&amp;req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title10-section119" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uscode.house.gov">[U.S. Code]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uscode.house.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Code10 USC 119: Special access programs: congressional...A special access program may not be initiated until- (1) the defense committees...</span></span></span>

</div>

This waiting period reflects a compromise between operational secrecy and constitutional oversight. Congress generally does not participate in designing or managing SAPs, but the Executive Branch also cannot create entirely new protected programmes without first informing the committees responsible for defence authorisation and appropriations.

The requirement also helps prevent exceptionally sensitive activities from developing entirely outside the established oversight framework. Even when programme details remain classified from the public, the notification requirement creates a documented point at which the programme formally enters congressional review.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&amp;num=0&amp;req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title10-section119" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uscode.house.gov">[U.S. Code]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uscode.house.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Code10 USC 119: Special access programs: congressional...A special access program may not be initiated until- (1) the defense committees...</span></span></span>

## Why written notification is significant

The emphasis on **written** notification is more than an administrative detail.

Written notice establishes:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* when the statutory 30-day clock begins;
* what programme is being proposed;
* which congressional committees received notice; and
* an official record demonstrating compliance with the reporting requirements.

</div>

Because SAP oversight often occurs almost entirely through classified channels, these procedural requirements become especially important. They provide an auditable mechanism showing that the Department followed the legal process before initiating a new programme, even though the substance of the notification remains classified.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/DD/issuances/dodi/520511p.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[WHS ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">WHS ESDDoD Instruction 5205.11, &quot;Management, Administration...12 Sept 2024 — A new SAP umbrella may not be created until the Director...</span></span></span>

## How this fits into the SAP approval process

The congressional waiting period occurs relatively late in the internal SAP establishment process.

Before written notice is sent, DoD policy requires a proposed capability to move through internal review, including assessment of whether SAP protection is justified, consideration by the Special Access Security Working Group (SSWG), and, where a genuinely new SAP umbrella is recommended, approval by senior DoD leadership. Only after these internal decisions does the Director of DoD SAPCO provide written notification to Congress, beginning the statutory waiting period.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/DD/issuances/dodi/520511p.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[WHS ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">WHS ESDDoD Instruction 5205.11, &quot;Management, Administration...12 Sept 2024 — A new SAP umbrella may not be created until the Director...</span></span></span>

This sequencing means that Congress is reviewing a mature proposal rather than a preliminary concept. The Executive Branch determines whether a SAP should be proposed, while Congress receives notice before implementation rather than participating in the programme's initial design.

## What emergency or waiver questions remain?

The statutory notice-and-wait requirement is clear, but readers often ask whether exceptionally sensitive programmes can bypass it.

For ordinary DoD SAPs, neither **10 U.S.C. § 119** nor current DoD Instruction 5205.11 establishes a general emergency exception allowing a completely new SAP to begin immediately without first completing congressional notification and the 30-day waiting period. The implementing instruction repeats the requirement in mandatory language without creating an emergency bypass for establishing a new SAP umbrella.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&amp;num=0&amp;req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title10-section119" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uscode.house.gov">[U.S. Code]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uscode.house.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Code10 USC 119: Special access programs: congressional...A special access program may not be initiated until- (1) the defense committees...</span></span></span>

The more complex question concerns **waived SAPs**. Waived programmes occupy a separate legal category with modified congressional reporting rules because of exceptional national security sensitivity. Those provisions affect *who* receives information and *how much* is reported, rather than eliminating congressional oversight altogether. They do not repeal the broader statutory framework governing SAP oversight, but they do permit narrower notification practices for the most sensitive programmes under specific legal authorities.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://fas.org/publication/dod_saps/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: fas.org">[Federation of American Scientists]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">fas.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">dod saps</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Federation of American ScientistsSpotlight on DoD Special Access Programs7 Feb 2013 — “Waived SAPs” are the most sensitive of special acc...</span></span></span>

As a result, the central rule remains straightforward: creating a new DoD SAP is not solely an executive decision implemented overnight. Even within one of the government's most secretive security systems, Congress is entitled to receive formal notice and a defined opportunity for review before the programme officially begins.
