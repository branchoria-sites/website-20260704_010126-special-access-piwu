## Introduction

Annual reports on Department of Defense (DoD) Special Access Programs (SAPs) emphasise **milestones rather than operational secrets** because [Congress]({{ 'congress/' | relative_url }}) needs evidence that highly classified programmes are progressing, remaining justified, and spending public money appropriately without exposing information that could undermine national security. Under 10 U.S.C. §119, the statutory reporting requirement is designed as an oversight mechanism, not a technical disclosure process. Rather than requiring descriptions of classified capabilities, [intelligence]({{ 'intelligence/' | relative_url }}) sources, engineering solutions, or operational plans, the law requires programme summaries, major milestones, historical and projected costs, and lifecycle information. This allows congressional defence committees to judge whether programmes are meeting expectations while preserving the compartmentation that makes SAPs effective.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cdse.edu/Portals/124/Documents/student-guides/SA001-guide.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cdse.edu">[CDSE]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cdse.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Student Guide Course: Special Access Program (SAP</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Student Guide Course: Special Access Program (SAP)...February 4, 2020 — SAP oversight is outlined in Section 119, Title 10 United St...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 4, 2020</span></span></span>

## Tracking Progress Without Revealing the Secret

The reporting framework in 10 U.S.C. §119 reflects a deliberate distinction between **programme management** and **operational knowledge**. Congress does not need every technical detail to determine whether a programme is being managed responsibly. Instead, the annual reports focus on measurable indicators that can be compared over successive years.

The [statute]({{ 'statute/' | relative_url }}) requires information such as:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* a brief description of the programme;
* discussion of major milestones;
* actual costs from previous years;
* estimated total programme cost; and
* projected funding over the current budget year and several future years.

</div>

These elements create a management record without requiring disclosure of the classified technology, intelligence methods, military plans, or vulnerabilities that justified SAP protection in the first place. The Centre for Development of Security Excellence (CDSE), which trains DoD security personnel, summarises the same reporting requirements and describes milestone reporting as covering current issues and significant programme changes rather than operational details.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cdse.edu/Portals/124/Documents/student-guides/SA001-guide.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cdse.edu">[CDSE]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cdse.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Student Guide Course: Special Access Program (SAP</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Student Guide Course: Special Access Program (SAP)...February 4, 2020 — SAP oversight is outlined in Section 119, Title 10 United St...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 4, 2020</span></span></span>

Milestones therefore function as observable management checkpoints. Depending on the programme, they might include approval of a development phase, completion of testing, transition into production, fielding of a capability, or another significant lifecycle event. Whether the underlying technology concerns advanced aircraft, [cyber]({{ 'cyber/' | relative_url }}) capabilities, or sensitive intelligence systems remains compartmented, but Congress can still determine whether the programme is advancing as planned.

## How Congress Can Compare Promises with Progress

A milestone has value because it can be tested against previous commitments. Annual reporting creates a chronological record that enables congressional committees to compare what programme managers previously expected with what actually occurred.

This longitudinal approach supports several forms of oversight:

* **Schedule [accountability]({{ 'accountability/' | relative_url }}).** If expected milestones repeatedly slip, committees can ask why delays occurred even without discussing classified technical details.

<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--comparison" markdown="1">

* **Budget discipline.** Cost increases become more meaningful when linked to missed or delayed milestones rather than viewed in isolation.
* **Lifecycle monitoring.** Reports reveal whether programmes remain in development longer than anticipated, transition into production, or approach completion.
* **Programme continuity.** Successive annual reports allow committees to identify whether promised advances are materialising or whether programmes are effectively stagnating despite continued funding.

</div>

Because Congress receives reports every year, milestone reporting becomes more informative over time than a single technical briefing. A classified capability may remain hidden from public view for decades, but repeated reporting allows authorised oversight bodies to build an evidence-based picture of programme health through changes in schedule, funding, and status.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cdse.edu/Portals/124/Documents/student-guides/SA001-guide.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cdse.edu">[CDSE]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cdse.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Student Guide Course: Special Access Program (SAP</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Student Guide Course: Special Access Program (SAP)...February 4, 2020 — SAP oversight is outlined in Section 119, Title 10 United St...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 4, 2020</span></span></span>

This approach also reflects familiar [acquisition]({{ 'acquisition/' | relative_url }}) oversight principles used elsewhere within the Department of Defense. Major defence programmes are commonly evaluated through progress against planned baselines rather than through disclosure of every engineering detail. SAP reporting applies that logic within a far more restrictive security environment.

## Why Operational Secrets Are Deliberately Excluded

The omission of detailed operational information is not an oversight gap but a security feature.

SAPs exist because ordinary classification measures are considered insufficient to protect especially sensitive capabilities, technologies, intelligence sources, or military [operations]({{ 'operations/' | relative_url }}). Revealing precisely how a programme works, who participates, or what capability it delivers could compromise the very advantage that special access protection is intended to preserve.

Operational details can expose:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* intelligence collection methods;
* technical vulnerabilities;
* defence capabilities still under development;
* operational plans;
* identities of particularly sensitive organisations or personnel; and
* relationships between otherwise compartmented programmes.

</div>

By focusing instead on milestones, Congress can verify that work is occurring without expanding the number of people who must know the classified substance. This follows the broader SAP principle of limiting knowledge to those with both the appropriate clearance and a demonstrated need to know.

Even within Congress, access to detailed information varies according to the sensitivity of the programme. ## Milestones Also Support Decisions About Continued Protection

Milestone reporting serves another purpose beyond budget oversight: it helps determine whether extraordinary security measures remain justified.

Current DoD policy requires annual reviews of every SAP to assess whether continued SAP protection is necessary. These reviews examine whether the protected capability or critical programme information still requires special access controls, whether programmes overlap unnecessarily, and whether some SAPs could eventually be downgraded, modified, or disestablished. The governance process also reviews whether waived programmes continue to require their exceptional reporting status.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/DD/issuances/dodi/520511p.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[WHS ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">WHS ESDDoD Instruction 5205.11, &quot;Management, Administration...12 Sept 2024 — Conduct an annual review of all DoD SAPs regarding continu...</span></span></span>

In that context, milestones become indicators of programme maturity. A capability approaching deployment may require different protection decisions from one in early research, while a completed programme may warrant reassessment of whether all aspects still require SAP-level protection.

Because annual reports document programme progression rather than only financial data, they provide governance bodies and congressional overseers with evidence supporting these periodic reassessments.

## Where Milestone Oversight Has Limits

Although milestone reporting strengthens accountability, it is not a complete substitute for detailed classified oversight.

Several limitations remain:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--caution" markdown="1">

* Milestones reveal **that** progress has occurred, not necessarily **how well** technical objectives have been achieved.
* Committees may still require separate classified briefings to understand whether programme risks are acceptable.
* Schedule achievement alone cannot demonstrate operational effectiveness.
* Highly classified technical failures may remain visible only within restricted briefings rather than the annual reporting framework.

</div>

Consequently, annual reports should be understood as one layer within a broader oversight system that also includes classified briefings, [inspections]({{ 'inspections/' | relative_url }}), audits, internal DoD governance, and periodic reviews by the Special Access Program Oversight Committee and related bodies.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/DD/issuances/dodi/520511p.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[WHS ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">WHS ESDDoD Instruction 5205.11, &quot;Management, Administration...12 Sept 2024 — Conduct an annual review of all DoD SAPs regarding continu...</span></span></span>

The emphasis on milestones therefore reflects a practical compromise. Congress receives enough structured information to monitor whether sensitive programmes are advancing, remaining on schedule, and consuming resources as expected, while the operational details that justify SAP protection remain compartmented. Over successive annual reports, those milestones become a durable oversight record that enables meaningful accountability without requiring disclosure of the underlying secrets themselves.
