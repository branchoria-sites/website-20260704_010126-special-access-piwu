## Introduction

Waived special access programmes, or waived SAPs, are often misunderstood as programmes placed outside congressional oversight. The public law and Department of Defense policy say something narrower and more precise: a waived SAP is a highly sensitive unacknowledged Department of Defense SAP for which the Secretary of Defense may withhold certain normally reportable details from the standard congressional reports, but only on a case-by-case national-security determination. The information is not supposed to disappear from [Congress]({{ 'congress/' | relative_url }}) altogether; it is redirected to a much smaller channel: the chair and ranking minority member of each congressional defence committee.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/10/119" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: law.cornell.edu">[Legal Information Institute]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">law.cornell.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Legal Information Institute10 U.S. Code § 119</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Special access programs: congressional oversight &#124; U.S. Code &#124; US Law &#124; LII / Legal Information Institute...</span></span></span>



<img src="{{ "/assets/images/special-access-progr_a1c931_waived_saps_d23c4e-overview.webp" | relative_url }}" alt="Overview image for Waived SAPs" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">

That distinction is the centre of the governance issue. A waived SAP does not abolish law, budgeting, internal review, or congressional notification. It changes who is told, how much is written down in the ordinary reporting stream, and how many members and staff can realistically scrutinise a programme. The result is a formal oversight mechanism with a deliberately narrow aperture: stronger protection against exposure, but also fewer independent eyes on cost, legality, [duplication]({{ 'duplication/' | relative_url }}), and performance.

## The case-by-case waiver idea

The legal basis sits in 10 U.S.C. § 119, the [statute]({{ 'statute/' | relative_url }}) governing Department of Defense SAP reporting to Congress. The ordinary rule requires the Secretary of Defense to submit annual reports to the defence committees on SAPs, including overall budget totals and programme-level information such as descriptions, [milestones]({{ 'milestones/' | relative_url }}), previous costs, and projected costs. The statute also requires reporting on new SAP designations and on planned declassification or public classification changes.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/10/119" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: law.cornell.edu">[Legal Information Institute]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">law.cornell.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Legal Information Institute10 U.S. Code § 119</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Special access programs: congressional oversight &#124; U.S. Code &#124; US Law &#124; LII / Legal Information Institute...</span></span></span>

The waiver power is narrower than the word “waived” can make it sound. Section 119(e) allows the Secretary of Defense to waive a requirement to include “certain information” in the usual reports only if including that information would adversely affect national security, and the waiver must be made case by case. The same subsection then requires the Secretary to provide the excluded information, together with the justification for the waiver, to the chair and ranking minority member of each defence committee.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/10/119" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: law.cornell.edu">[Legal Information Institute]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">law.cornell.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Legal Information Institute10 U.S. Code § 119</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Special access programs: congressional oversight &#124; U.S. Code &#124; US Law &#124; LII / Legal Information Institute...</span></span></span>

In practical terms, the waiver is not a general authorisation to hide a programme from Congress. It is a decision to remove information from the broader committee-reporting path and give it to a smaller group of senior congressional overseers. Current Department of Defense Instruction 5205.11 reflects that structure: the Secretary may designate selected [unacknowledged SAPs]({{ 'unacknowledged/' | relative_url }}) as waived when standard congressional reporting would adversely affect national security, but the instruction expressly states that waived SAPs remain subject to congressional oversight.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/DD/issuances/dodi/520511p.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[WHS ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">WHS ESDDoD Instruction 5205.11, &quot;Management, Administration, and Oversight of DoD Special Access Programs,&quot; September 12, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 12, 2024</span></span></span>

This is why the “case-by-case” language matters. It prevents “waived” from becoming a permanent category of untouchable secrecy in theory. Each waiver is supposed to be justified by the specific risk created by normal reporting, rather than by a broad preference for keeping sensitive activity away from legislators or their staff. Whether that safeguard works well in practice depends on the quality of the justification, the willingness of senior members to challenge it, and the availability of cleared staff support.

## What reporting gets narrowed

The standard SAP reporting channel is already limited compared with ordinary public budgeting. Section 119 defines the relevant recipients as the Senate and House Armed Services Committees and Appropriations Committees, including their defence appropriations subcommittees. For non-waived SAPs, those committees receive the regular statutory reports; other members and staff who want information must work through the relevant committee leadership. A 1993 Government [Accountability]({{ 'accountability/' | relative_url }}) Office report noted that, under congressional procedures, other members and staff seeking SAP information had to request access through the chairmen of the defence committees.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/10/119" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: law.cornell.edu">[Legal Information Institute]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">law.cornell.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Legal Information Institute10 U.S. Code § 119</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Special access programs: congressional oversight &#124; U.S. Code &#124; US Law &#124; LII / Legal Information Institute...</span></span></span>

A waived SAP narrows that channel again. Instead of the normal committee flow, the excluded information and waiver justification go jointly to the chair and ranking minority member of each defence committee. In the House and Senate structure identified by the statute, that means the senior majority and minority leaders of the Armed Services and Appropriations defence oversight lanes, rather than the full membership and staff of those committees.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/10/119" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: law.cornell.edu">[Legal Information Institute]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">law.cornell.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Legal Information Institute10 U.S. Code § 119</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Special access programs: congressional oversight &#124; U.S. Code &#124; US Law &#124; LII / Legal Information Institute...</span></span></span>

Specialist summaries have long described this as the most restrictive SAP reporting path. The Federation of American Scientists, discussing Department of Defense SAPs, states that waived SAPs are the most sensitive SAPs and that only eight senior members of the congressional defence committees may be advised of such programmes. That shorthand captures the practical effect, though the statutory text is more precise: it is particular information in the standard reports that is waived, and the alternative recipients are the chair and ranking minority member of each defence committee.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://fas.org/publication/dod_saps/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: fas.org">[Federation of American Scientists]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">fas.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">dod saps</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">dod saps</span></span></span>

Service-level policy shows how this narrowed channel affects daily governance. An Air Force policy directive says members of Congress assigned to defence and [intelligence]({{ 'intelligence/' | relative_url }}) committees are generally authorised access to SAPs within their oversight role, except for waived SAPs under 10 U.S.C. § 119. Unless the Secretary or Deputy Secretary of Defense approves otherwise, access to waived SAPs is limited to the chair, ranking minority member, and staff directors of the relevant defence and intelligence committees.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://static.e-publishing.af.mil/production/1/saf_aa/publication/afpd16-7/afpd16-7.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: static.e-publishing.af.mil">[E-Publishing]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">static.e-publishing.af.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on af.mil.</span></span></span>

That last point is important because oversight is not performed by elected members alone. Staff directors and professional committee staff often carry much of the practical burden: reading budget books, comparing programme claims, preparing questions, tracking reprogramming, and detecting duplication. When a programme is waived, the legal route remains open, but the number of people able to do that detailed work can shrink sharply.

## Why oversight still formally exists

Waived SAPs remain inside a layered oversight system. At the statutory level, they are still governed by Section 119. At the Department of Defense level, current policy places SAPs inside an enterprise governance structure involving SAP oversight authorities, SAP central offices, lifecycle review, and senior approval for establishment or disestablishment at the top tier. DoD Instruction 5205.11 also requires lifecycle planning for disestablishment or transfer out of SAP protection when special controls are no longer necessary.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/DD/issuances/dodi/520511p.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[WHS ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">WHS ESDDoD Instruction 5205.11, &quot;Management, Administration, and Oversight of DoD Special Access Programs,&quot; September 12, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 12, 2024</span></span></span>

The historical reason for these controls is not theoretical. GAO’s 1993 review found that congressional and other external pressure had pushed the Department of Defense towards stronger and more centralised SAP oversight. It reported that Congress had legislated formal reporting requirements and that the Senate Armed Services Committee had pressed DoD to provide information on all SAPs to the congressional defence committees.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gao.gov/assets/nsiad-93-78.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.gov">[GAO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NSIAD-93-78 Defense Acquisition: Oversight of Special Access Programs Has Increased...</span></span></span>

GAO also explained why secrecy alone was not a sufficient management model. In its sample of special access acquisition programmes, DoD had generally followed the same resource justification and execution processes used for ordinary acquisition programmes, but GAO found earlier weaknesses involving internal controls, communication, record-keeping, and some exemptions from normal acquisition reporting. Former SAP-related acquisition examples, including aircraft and weapons programmes, experienced cost growth, schedule slippage, and performance shortfalls similar to non-SAP programmes.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gao.gov/assets/nsiad-93-78.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.gov">[GAO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NSIAD-93-78 Defense Acquisition: Oversight of Special Access Programs Has Increased...</span></span></span>

The Moynihan Commission later framed the same tension in broader secrecy-governance terms. It described SAPs as access controls beyond the normal classification system and noted that waived programmes were considered so sensitive that they were exempt from standard reporting to Congress, with notification sometimes limited orally to senior committee leaders and, on occasion, other relevant members and staff. The commission also noted that DoD had created the Special Access Program Oversight Committee in 1994 to standardise approval, termination, revalidation, and restructuring procedures and to help ensure required information reached Congress.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://info.publicintelligence.net/MoynihanReportGovernmentSecrecy.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: info.publicintelligence.net">[Public Intelligence &#124;]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">info.publicintelligence.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Public Intelligence</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Public Intelligence</span></span></span>

So the accurate governance picture is neither “fully transparent” nor “wholly unaccountable”. It is a controlled-access oversight model. Congress retains a formal foothold, but the foothold is concentrated in a small number of cleared leaders and selected staff channels.

## The real accountability tradeoff

The strongest argument for a waived SAP is that some information can be damaging even within normal classified reporting routines. A programme might reveal a fragile intelligence source, a novel military capability, a foreign partner’s involvement, a technical vulnerability, or an operational dependency that becomes more exposed as the distribution list grows. Department of Defense policy explicitly ties waived status to the judgement that standard congressional reporting would adversely affect national security.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/DD/issuances/dodi/520511p.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[WHS ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">WHS ESDDoD Instruction 5205.11, &quot;Management, Administration, and Oversight of DoD Special Access Programs,&quot; September 12, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 12, 2024</span></span></span>

The strongest concern is that [narrow reporting]({{ 'narrow-reporting/' | relative_url }}) can weaken the ordinary advantages of congressional oversight. A wider committee process allows specialised staff to compare programmes, follow money, question assumptions, spot duplication, and brief members who may not personally have time to master every classified line item. When reporting is narrowed to a small leadership group, oversight may become more dependent on a few people’s time, scepticism, memory, and willingness to press the executive branch.

That tradeoff is especially acute for acquisition SAPs. GAO’s review found that SAPs could suffer the same cost, schedule, and performance problems as non-SAP programmes, while secrecy could limit access by outside support organisations and complicate oversight. That finding cuts against the idea that extreme secrecy is mainly a technical security issue. It is also a management issue: fewer reviewers can mean fewer chances to catch mundane but expensive problems.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gao.gov/assets/nsiad-93-78.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.gov">[GAO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NSIAD-93-78 Defense Acquisition: Oversight of Special Access Programs Has Increased...</span></span></span>

The practical question is therefore not whether waived SAPs are “legal” or “illegal” as a category. The more useful question is whether the narrowed channel is strong enough for the specific programme: whether the waiver is genuinely necessary, whether the senior members receive enough detail, whether cleared staff directors can support them, whether internal audit and inspector-general channels function, and whether the programme is periodically revalidated rather than left indefinitely in its most restricted posture.

## What waived SAPs really hide

A waived SAP hides less, and more, than popular shorthand suggests. It does not necessarily hide a programme from all of Congress, from the Secretary of Defense, from internal SAP governance, from legal review, or from budgetary processes. The statute and DoD policy both preserve a formal oversight route.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/10/119" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: law.cornell.edu">[Legal Information Institute]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">law.cornell.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Legal Information Institute10 U.S. Code § 119</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Special access programs: congressional oversight &#124; U.S. Code &#124; US Law &#124; LII / Legal Information Institute...</span></span></span>

What it does hide is the programme’s sensitive information from the standard congressional reporting path. It narrows the circle of congressional recipients, reduces the number of staff and members who can examine the material, and may change the texture of oversight from committee-wide scrutiny to leadership-level notification. In the most sensitive cases, open-source accounts and historical secrecy reviews suggest that even the existence of the programme may be treated as classified, and notification may be sparse or oral.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://info.publicintelligence.net/MoynihanReportGovernmentSecrecy.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: info.publicintelligence.net">[Public Intelligence &#124;]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">info.publicintelligence.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Public Intelligence</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Public Intelligence</span></span></span>

That is the key to understanding waived SAPs within the broader world of special-access programmes, including waived and unacknowledged programmes. The waiver is not an escape hatch from oversight on paper. It is a governance intervention that trades breadth of oversight for secrecy protection. Its legitimacy depends on whether that trade is rare, justified, documented, and actively challenged by the few congressional officials who are allowed to see behind the narrowed door.

    if (!cfg) return '#';
    var template = String(cfg.url_template || 'https://www.ebay.com/sch/i.html?_nkw={query}');
    var url = template.replace('{query}', encodeQuery(query || '')).replace('{query_plain}', String(query || ''));
    var joiner = url.indexOf('?') >= 0 ? '&' : '?';
    var params = [];
    if (cfg.mkrid) params.push('mkrid=' + encodeURIComponent(cfg.mkrid));
    if (cfg.campaign_id) params.push('campid=' + encodeURIComponent(cfg.campaign_id));
    params.push('mkevt=1');
    params.push('mkcid=1');
    params.push('toolid=' + encodeURIComponent(cfg.tool_id || '10001'));
    if (reference) params.push('customid=' + encodeURIComponent(reference).slice(0, 256));
    return url + joiner + params.join('&');
  }
  function domainLabel(marketId) {
    var cfg = markets[marketId] || markets[defaultMarket] || {};
    return cfg.domain_label || 'eBay';
  }
  function flagClass(marketId) {
    return 'fr-ebay-market-flag fr-ebay-market-flag--' + String(marketId || '').toLowerCase().replace(/_/g, '-').replace(/[^a-z0-9-]+/g, '');
  }
  function closeMenu(section) {
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var picker = section.querySelector('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
    if (menu) menu.hidden = true;
    if (trigger) trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', 'false');
    if (picker) picker.classList.remove('fr-ebay-market-picker--open');
  }
  function availableMarkets(section) {
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      return Array.prototype.slice.call(select.options)
        .map(function (option) { return option.value; })
        .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
    }
    return Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]'))
      .map(function (button) { return button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'); })
      .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
  }
  function applyMarket(section, marketId, persist) {
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    if (available.indexOf(marketId)< 0) marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-localized-link]')).forEach(function (link) {
      var query = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-query') || '';
      var reference = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-reference') || '';
      link.setAttribute('href', buildUrl(query, reference, marketId));
    });
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select && available.indexOf(marketId) >= 0) select.value = marketId;
    var selectedText = marketId;
    if (select && select.selectedIndex >= 0) selectedText = select.options[select.selectedIndex].text;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-flag], [data-ebay-trigger-market-flag]')).forEach(function (flag) {
      flag.className = flagClass(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-trigger-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-domain-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = domainLabel(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.setAttribute('aria-selected', button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option') === marketId ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;
      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
})();
</script>
