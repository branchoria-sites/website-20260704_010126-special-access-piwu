## Introduction

When the United States government decides that the existence of a Special Access Program (SAP) can be publicly acknowledged, that decision is not simply a matter of issuing a press release. Before a Department of Defense SAP changes classification status or classified information about it is deliberately released to the public, [Congress]({{ 'congress/' | relative_url }}) is entitled by law to receive advance notice. This requirement ensures that elected oversight committees are informed before the Executive Branch publicly changes the status of exceptionally sensitive defence programmes. The process reflects a constitutional balance: the Executive controls classification decisions, but Congress retains oversight over how highly classified defence programmes are created, managed, modified and, where appropriate, publicly acknowledged.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&amp;num=0&amp;req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title10-section119" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uscode.house.gov">[U.S. Code]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uscode.house.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Code10 USC 119: Special access programs: congressional...(a)(1) Not later than March 1 of each year, the Secretary of Defense shall subm...</span></span></span>

## Annual SAP reporting under defence law

Congressional oversight of Department of Defense SAPs is centred on 10 U.S.C. § 119, which establishes recurring reporting obligations to the congressional defence committees. Rather than learning about SAPs only when they become public, the committees receive structured information throughout a programme's life cycle.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&amp;num=0&amp;req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title10-section119" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uscode.house.gov">[U.S. Code]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uscode.house.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Code10 USC 119: Special access programs: congressional...(a)(1) Not later than March 1 of each year, the Secretary of Defense shall subm...</span></span></span>

The [statute]({{ 'statute/' | relative_url }}) requires the Secretary of Defense to submit an annual report covering all DoD SAPs. Among other things, that report includes:

* the total SAP funding requested in the President's defence budget;
* a description of each SAP;
* major programme [milestones]({{ 'milestones/' | relative_url }});
* historical programme costs; and
* projected future costs.

Separate reporting is also required for newly designated SAPs, including the justification for placing a programme under special access controls and estimates of its overall cost. This means congressional oversight begins long before any decision about public acknowledgement arises.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&amp;num=0&amp;req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title10-section119" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uscode.house.gov">[U.S. Code]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uscode.house.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Code10 USC 119: Special access programs: congressional...(a)(1) Not later than March 1 of each year, the Secretary of Defense shall subm...</span></span></span>

Importantly, these reports are directed to the congressional defence committees rather than to Congress as a whole. Because SAP information remains classified, access is limited to the committees and cleared personnel authorised to receive it.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&amp;num=0&amp;req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title10-section119" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uscode.house.gov">[U.S. Code]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uscode.house.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Code10 USC 119: Special access programs: congressional...(a)(1) Not later than March 1 of each year, the Secretary of Defense shall subm...</span></span></span>

## Advance notice before public declassification

The clearest legal safeguard governing public acknowledgement appears in subsection (c) of 10 U.S.C. § 119.

Whenever the Department of Defense plans either:

* to change the classification status of a SAP; or
* to declassify classified information about a SAP and make that information public,

the Secretary of Defense must first provide a report to the congressional defence committees. That report must explain:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* the proposed change;
* the reasons for making it; and
* any planned public announcement accompanying the change.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&amp;num=0&amp;req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title10-section119" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uscode.house.gov">[U.S. Code]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uscode.house.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Code10 USC 119: Special access programs: congressional...(a)(1) Not later than March 1 of each year, the Secretary of Defense shall subm...</span></span></span>

</div>

In ordinary circumstances, Congress must receive this report **at least 14 days before** the planned public disclosure or classification change. The advance-notice requirement gives oversight committees time to review the proposed action, ask questions, or raise concerns before information enters the public domain.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&amp;num=0&amp;req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title10-section119" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uscode.house.gov">[U.S. Code]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uscode.house.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Code10 USC 119: Special access programs: congressional...(a)(1) Not later than March 1 of each year, the Secretary of Defense shall subm...</span></span></span>

The law also recognises that unusual operational circumstances may require faster action. If the Secretary of Defense determines that the 14-day period cannot be met because of exceptional circumstances, notification may occur at any time before the public announcement, provided the report explains why the normal notice period could not be observed. The statute therefore creates a narrow flexibility rather than eliminating congressional notification altogether.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&amp;num=0&amp;req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title10-section119" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uscode.house.gov">[U.S. Code]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uscode.house.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Code10 USC 119: Special access programs: congressional...(a)(1) Not later than March 1 of each year, the Secretary of Defense shall subm...</span></span></span>

## Why acknowledgement is a governed act

Public acknowledgement of a SAP is often misunderstood as a communications decision. In practice, it is treated as a formal governance action.

Changing the public status of a SAP can affect:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--decision" markdown="1">

* future appropriations debates;
* congressional oversight responsibilities;
* contractor disclosure rules;
* public procurement transparency;
* operational security; and
* international relationships with allies or partners.

</div>

Because these consequences extend well beyond public affairs, the decision is embedded within statutory oversight procedures instead of being left solely to executive discretion. The required report documents both the reasons for the change and the intended public messaging, ensuring that oversight committees understand not only *what* will become public but *why*.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&amp;num=0&amp;req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title10-section119" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uscode.house.gov">[U.S. Code]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uscode.house.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Code10 USC 119: Special access programs: congressional...(a)(1) Not later than March 1 of each year, the Secretary of Defense shall subm...</span></span></span>

Internal Department of Defense policy similarly treats congressional reporting as a routine component of SAP governance. Current SAP policy assigns responsibility for preparing congressional reports and coordinating required reviews, reinforcing that oversight is part of programme management rather than an afterthought once disclosure is contemplated.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/portals/54/documents/dd/issuances/dodd/520507p.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">dod directive 5205.07 special access program policy12 Sept 2024 — Coordinates and provides guidance for annual SAP reviews and reports...</span></span></span>

## Who receives the notification?

The statute defines the "defence committees" that receive these reports. They include:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* the Senate Committee on Armed Services;
* the Senate Committee on Appropriations and its Defence Subcommittee;
* the House Committee on Armed Services; and
* the House Committee on Appropriations and its Defence Subcommittee.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&amp;num=0&amp;req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title10-section119" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uscode.house.gov">[U.S. Code]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uscode.house.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Code10 USC 119: Special access programs: congressional...(a)(1) Not later than March 1 of each year, the Secretary of Defense shall subm...</span></span></span>

</div>

For the most sensitive **waived** SAPs, reporting is even more restricted. Instead of broad committee notification, certain statutory reporting requirements may be satisfied through disclosure only to the chair and ranking minority member of each defence committee, together with specified senior staff, where national security requires exceptionally limited dissemination. ## Public acknowledgement does not end congressional oversight

Advance notification before public acknowledgement is only one point in a continuing oversight relationship.

Congress continues to receive notification when:

* new SAPs are established;
* SAP designation policies are modified;
* classification [status changes]({{ 'status-changes/' | relative_url }});
* programmes are terminated or substantially altered; and
* annual reviews are submitted covering costs and programme status.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&amp;num=0&amp;req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title10-section119" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uscode.house.gov">[U.S. Code]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uscode.house.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Code10 USC 119: Special access programs: congressional...(a)(1) Not later than March 1 of each year, the Secretary of Defense shall subm...</span></span></span>

This framework illustrates an important feature of [acknowledged SAPs]({{ 'acknowledged/' | relative_url }}): the government does not first announce a programme publicly and then brief Congress afterwards.
