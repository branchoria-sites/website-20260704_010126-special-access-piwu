## Introduction

SAP physical security is the part of a special access programme that turns secrecy into a controlled place: a room, suite, vault, laboratory, hangar bay, server area or other accredited space where SAP information can be generated, discussed, processed, stored and destroyed. A Special Access Program Facility, or SAPF, is not just a locked room with classified material inside; NIST’s glossary defines it as a physical space formally accredited in writing by the cognisant programme security officer and approved for handling programme information, hardware and materials.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://csrc.nist.gov/glossary/term/special_access_program_facility" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: csrc.nist.gov">[NIST Computer Security Resource Center]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">csrc.nist.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Computer Security Resource Centerspecial access program facility</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NIST Computer Security Resource Centerspecial access program facility - Glossary &#124; CSRC...</span></span></span>

This matters because SAPs, including unacknowledged and waived SAPs, depend on more than clearances. A person may be cleared at the right level and still be excluded from the room, the system, the visitor list and the conversation. The room itself becomes part of the access-control mechanism: it narrows who can enter, what devices may cross the threshold, how workstations connect, how documents are destroyed, and how any deviation is recorded.

## Controlled spaces and storage

A SAPF is the everyday physical expression of “special access required”. DoD Manual 5205.07, reissued in January 2025, applies SAP security requirements to DoD components, contractors and consultants who require access to DoD SAPs, and it incorporates the earlier volume on SAP physical security.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/DD/issuances/dodm/520507m1.PDF" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[WHS ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">WHS ESDDoDM 5205.07, &quot;Special Access Program Security Manual,&quot; January 17, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 17, 2025</span></span></span> In practice, this means the programme does not merely mark papers differently; it accredits a place in which those papers, conversations, hardware and information systems may exist.

The most important word is **accredited**. A SAPF is approved for a defined purpose, classification level, programme and operating pattern. CDSE training material on the SAPF Fixed Facility Checklist says facilities must be accredited before receiving, generating, processing, using or storing SAP classified information. The checklist records the exact location, responsible security personnel, [accreditation]({{ 'accreditation/' | relative_url }}) data, [construction]({{ 'construction/' | relative_url }}) status and inspection status, making the room’s authority traceable rather than informal.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cdse.edu/Portals/124/Documents/student-guides/shorts/SAS0122-guide.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cdse.edu">[CDSE]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cdse.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Fixed Facility Checklist Short Student Guide</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Fixed Facility Checklist Short Student Guide</span></span></span>

SAP physical security is usually layered rather than dependent on a single impressive door. CDSE describes “[security-in-depth]({{ 'layered-security/' | relative_url }})” as layered and complementary controls sufficient to deter and detect unauthorised entry and movement; its examples include the building location, fences, lighting, guards, interior access controls and alarms.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cdse.edu/Portals/124/Documents/student-guides/shorts/SAS0122-guide.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cdse.edu">[CDSE]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cdse.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Fixed Facility Checklist Short Student Guide</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Fixed Facility Checklist Short Student Guide</span></span></span> For a reader, the useful point is that a secure room is assessed as part of a surrounding environment: the wall matters, but so do the corridor, the guard post, the alarm response and the visitor process.

The construction review looks for ordinary-looking weaknesses. CDSE’s SAPF construction course trains personnel to inspect doors, windows, ducts, ventilation, view ports, ceilings, walls, floors, intrusion detection systems, telecommunications and classified destruction methods against DoD and [Intelligence]({{ 'intelligence/' | relative_url }}) Community requirements.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cdse.edu/Training/eLearning/SA501/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cdse.edu">[CDSE]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cdse.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cdse.edu.</span></span></span> The reason is practical: many compromises do not require cinematic vault-breaking. They can exploit overlooked penetrations, poorly controlled utility routes, weak door hardware, exposed conversations, unapproved devices or records removed without inspection.

A separate DoD facilities document, UFC 4-010-05 on SCIF/SAPF planning, design and construction, frames secure-room projects as planning and budget problems as much as security problems. Its planning chapter says the requirements may affect facility scope and budget, and that SCIFs and SAPFs are established when clear operational requirements are critical to a supported command’s mission.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://exwc.navfac.navy.mil/Portals/88/Documents/EXWC/DoD_Locks/PDFs/UFC_4-010-05.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: exwc.navfac.navy.mil">[ExWC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">exwc.navfac.navy.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ex WCSCIF/SAPF Planning, Design, and Construction</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ex WCSCIF/SAPF Planning, Design, and Construction</span></span></span> That is why SAP secure rooms often feel expensive and slow to build: accreditation has to be considered before walls are closed, cables are run, systems are installed and occupancy begins.

## How SAP rooms control who enters

SAP access is personal, local and auditable. DoDM 5205.07 says only assigned SAP-indoctrinated personnel may have unescorted access to a SAPF, and that security officers should maintain rosters of people with such access where practicable. It also requires procedures to identify and control visitors seeking physical access, with personal introduction and identification used as much as possible.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/DD/issuances/dodm/520507m1.PDF" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[WHS ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">WHS ESDDoDM 5205.07, &quot;Special Access Program Security Manual,&quot; January 17, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 17, 2025</span></span></span>

That is a sharper rule than “has a clearance”. A cleared engineer, auditor, cleaner, senior official or contractor may still need escort, prior approval or exclusion if they have not been read into the programme or do not have the specific need to know. In larger SAPFs, DoD policy allows a badging system when people inside cannot personally identify everyone with access, usually when the SAPF hosts more than 25 people; if used, the badge system must be covered in the local standard operating procedure, including [accountability]({{ 'accountability/' | relative_url }}), storage, disposal and use.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/DD/issuances/dodm/520507m1.PDF" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[WHS ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">WHS ESDDoDM 5205.07, &quot;Special Access Program Security Manual,&quot; January 17, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 17, 2025</span></span></span>

Visitor control is especially important for [unacknowledged SAPs]({{ 'unacknowledged/' | relative_url }}), where even the existence or location of the activity may be sensitive. Older DoD SAP guidance, still useful for understanding the logic of visitor rules, stated that only resident SAP-accessed personnel could escort and closely control the movement of non-SAP-accessed visitors who needed access to a SAPF, and that the number of escorts depended on the number of visitors and the ability to monitor them closely.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://securityawareness.dcsa.mil/cdse/multimedia/shorts/sap-sec-incident/common/scenarios/industry_day/common/files/resources/5205.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: securityawareness.dcsa.mil">[securityawareness.dcsa.mil]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">securityawareness.dcsa.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on dcsa.mil.</span></span></span>

The door is therefore not just a barrier; it is a decision point. At that point, the programme asks: is this person indoctrinated, expected, identifiable, logged, escorted if necessary, and authorised for the information that may be visible or audible inside? A secure room fails if it admits the wrong cleared person almost as surely as if it admits an uncleared person.

## Separate systems and technical controls

Modern SAP rooms protect more than paper files. They also contain computers, displays, telephones, removable media, printers, destruction equipment and sometimes specialised hardware. DoDM 5205.07 gives the DoD Chief Information Officer a role in SAP information technology strategy, telecommunications infrastructure policy, SAP network requirements and the authorisation of SAP networks, databases and information systems under DoD SAPCO cognisance.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/DD/issuances/dodm/520507m1.PDF" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[WHS ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">WHS ESDDoDM 5205.07, &quot;Special Access Program Security Manual,&quot; January 17, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 17, 2025</span></span></span>

This is why secure-room design often joins physical security to cyber security. The CDSE Fixed Facility Checklist includes sections for telecommunications systems, equipment baselines, information systems, [TEMPEST]({{ 'tempest/' | relative_url }}) and technical security, and says the checklist should be treated as a living document updated when the facility changes.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cdse.edu/Portals/124/Documents/student-guides/shorts/SAS0122-guide.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cdse.edu">[CDSE]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cdse.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Fixed Facility Checklist Short Student Guide</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Fixed Facility Checklist Short Student Guide</span></span></span> In plain terms, moving a workstation, adding a collaboration display, changing a phone line or altering a network connection can be a security event, not merely an office change.

TEMPEST is one of the less familiar but important parts of this world. The term refers to protection against compromising emanations: signals or electronic leakage that could reveal sensitive information. CDSE says Certified TEMPEST Technical Authorities review TEMPEST checklists, assess requirements to prevent electronic emanations and recommend countermeasures based on emanations tests.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cdse.edu/Portals/124/Documents/student-guides/shorts/SAS0122-guide.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cdse.edu">[CDSE]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cdse.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Fixed Facility Checklist Short Student Guide</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Fixed Facility Checklist Short Student Guide</span></span></span> The IC technical specification for secure facilities similarly says its specifications are intended to protect against compromising emanations, inadvertent observation, unauthorised disclosure and unauthorised entry.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.dni.gov/files/Governance/IC-Tech-Specs-for-Const-and-Mgmt-of-SCIFs-v15.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dni.gov">[Director of National Intelligence]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dni.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Director of National Intelligence</span></span></span>

Portable electronic devices are another boundary issue. DoDM 5205.07 requires the facility standard operating procedure to cover portable electronic devices and other items brought into or removed from a SAPF. Unclassified devices that store, record or transmit information are generally prohibited, while narrow exceptions exist for items that lack storage, recording, microphone, camera, cellular, Bluetooth or Wi-Fi capabilities.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/DD/issuances/dodm/520507m1.PDF" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[WHS ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">WHS ESDDoDM 5205.07, &quot;Special Access Program Security Manual,&quot; January 17, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 17, 2025</span></span></span> This is why phones, smart watches, wireless earbuds, cameras, thumb drives and fitness devices are treated differently from ordinary personal belongings at many secure-room entrances.

The same policy also requires entry-exit inspection procedures for personal belongings and vehicles at entry and exit points or other designated areas. The stated purpose is to deter both unauthorised removal of classified material and introduction of prohibited items, and legal counsel must review personnel inspection procedures before they are implemented.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/DD/issuances/dodm/520507m1.PDF" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[WHS ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">WHS ESDDoDM 5205.07, &quot;Special Access Program Security Manual,&quot; January 17, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 17, 2025</span></span></span> This shows the balance: SAP rooms are restrictive, but their controls still have to be formal, reviewable and bounded by legal procedure.

## SAPF, SCIF and co-use confusion

SAPFs are often compared with SCIFs, or Sensitive Compartmented Information Facilities, because the physical security vocabulary overlaps. The Intelligence Community technical specification for ICD/ICS 705 is written for SCIFs, but it explicitly says that when its technical specifications are applied to new construction, renovation and operation of SAPFs, those SAPFs can satisfy ICD 705 standards and enable uniform use across IC elements for accreditation as SCIFs.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.dni.gov/files/Governance/IC-Tech-Specs-for-Const-and-Mgmt-of-SCIFs-v15.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dni.gov">[Director of National Intelligence]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dni.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Director of National Intelligence</span></span></span>

That does not make every SAPF a SCIF, or every SCIF a SAPF. The difference is usually the information regime being supported. SCIFs are associated with Sensitive Compartmented Information; SAPFs are associated with Special Access Programmes. The same room may sometimes be designed, accredited or co-used to support both, but the accrediting authorities, documentation and permitted information must align.

DoDM 5205.07 addresses this overlap directly. It allows arrangements such as a SCIF within a SAPF, a SAP compartmented area within a SCIF, or an SCI compartmented area within a SAPF, but requires authorisation before establishing such arrangements and requires a co-utilisation agreement before occupancy.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/DD/issuances/dodm/520507m1.PDF" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[WHS ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">WHS ESDDoDM 5205.07, &quot;Special Access Program Security Manual,&quot; January 17, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 17, 2025</span></span></span> The policy point is simple: sharing a secure room is not just a scheduling decision. It changes who the accreditors are, what information can be present, which systems are authorised and how conflicts between rules are resolved.

Reciprocity is one reason these standards matter. The IC technical specification says applying its requirements to SCIF construction and renovation is meant to enable uniform and reciprocal use across IC elements and support information sharing.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.dni.gov/files/Governance/IC-Tech-Specs-for-Const-and-Mgmt-of-SCIFs-v15.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dni.gov">[Director of National Intelligence]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dni.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Director of National Intelligence</span></span></span> For SAP spaces, this can reduce [duplication]({{ 'duplication/' | relative_url }}) when a room built for one secure purpose needs to be recognised by another authority, but reciprocity depends on documentation being complete and deviations being understood.

## Why physical security still matters

It is tempting to think SAP security now lives mainly in networks, encryption and insider-threat monitoring. Physical security still matters because it controls the conditions under which all those other protections operate. A secure network terminal is less secure if an unauthorised visitor can see the screen; a strong document-control rule is weaker if bags are never inspected; a cleared workforce is riskier if people can bring recording devices into programme spaces.

The DoD’s 2026 SAP Security Compliance Inspection Checklist shows how concrete this becomes during inspections and self-assessments. It is not a vague “secure enough” review: it asks organisations to document appointed security officials, programme roles, procedures and inspection answers against DoD Manual 5205.07.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.dcsa.mil/Portals/128/Documents/CTP/special/DoW%20SAP%20Security%20Compliance%20Checklist%20January%202026%20v2.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dcsa.mil">[DCSA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dcsa.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">DoW SAP Security Compliance Checklist January 2026 v2</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">DoW SAP Security Compliance Checklist January 2026 v2</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 2026</span></span></span> CDSE’s Fixed Facility Checklist then breaks the room itself into assessable areas: security-in-depth, SAPF security, doors, intrusion detection, telecommunications, acoustical protection, classified destruction, information systems, TEMPEST and technical security.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cdse.edu/Portals/124/Documents/student-guides/shorts/SAS0122-guide.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cdse.edu">[CDSE]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cdse.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Fixed Facility Checklist Short Student Guide</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Fixed Facility Checklist Short Student Guide</span></span></span>

Physical controls also create friction that can be politically and administratively significant. In waived or unacknowledged SAPs, the room may be one of the few places where the programme’s existence is operationally visible to those inside the compartment. That makes visitor logs, accreditation letters, access rosters, construction approvals and inspection records more than paperwork: they are part of how the government distinguishes controlled secrecy from informal concealment.

The strongest way to understand SAP secure rooms is as a chain of custody for space. The walls define where programme material may exist. The access roster defines who may enter without escort. The device policy defines what can record, transmit or leave. The technical review defines whether the room leaks information electronically or acoustically. The inspection record defines whether the room still matches the authority under which it was accredited. When any link changes, the room is no longer just a room; it is a security decision that has to be revisited.
