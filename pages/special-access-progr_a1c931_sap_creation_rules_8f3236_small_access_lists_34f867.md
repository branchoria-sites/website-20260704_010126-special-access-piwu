## Introduction

Executive Order 13526 does not merely require that Special Access Programs (SAPs) protect exceptionally sensitive information. It also requires that the number of people who ordinarily have access remain **"reasonably small"** and proportionate to the purpose of the programme. This requirement is one of the Executive Order's most practical safeguards because it prevents enhanced security controls from becoming symbolic rather than effective. A SAP is intended to create a tightly controlled compartment around particularly vulnerable information, not simply to place another administrative label on material that is already classified. When access expands too broadly, the additional protections that justify a SAP become increasingly difficult to sustain.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://obamawhitehouse.archives.gov/the-press-office/executive-order-classified-national-security-information" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: obamawhitehouse.archives.gov">[whitehouse.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">obamawhitehouse.archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">executive order classified national security information</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Order 13526- Classified National Security InformationDecember 29, 2009 — 29 Dec 2009 — (1) Special access programs shall be limited to pr...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 29, 2009</span></span></span>

The "reasonably small" rule therefore operates alongside the need-to-know principle. Even personnel with the appropriate security clearance do not automatically receive SAP access. Instead, each individual must be formally admitted because their duties require knowledge of that specific programme. The result is a controlled access list that remains manageable, auditable, and meaningful.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cdse.edu/Portals/124/Documents/student-guides/SA001-guide.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cdse.edu">[CDSE]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cdse.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Student Guide Course: Special Access Program (SAP)...February 4, 2020 — Executive Order 13526, “Classified National Security Informa...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 4, 2020</span></span></span>

## How the reasonably small rule works

Executive Order 13526 deliberately avoids prescribing a numerical limit. Instead, it requires that access be **commensurate with the objective of providing enhanced protection**. This flexibility recognises that SAPs differ widely. A narrowly focused [intelligence]({{ 'intelligence/' | relative_url }}) capability may involve only a handful of officials, while a major defence [acquisition]({{ 'acquisition/' | relative_url }}) programme may legitimately require hundreds of engineers, operators, security personnel and contractors.

The key test is not whether an access list is large or small in absolute terms, but whether every person on it genuinely needs routine access to perform assigned duties. If additional personnel can accomplish their work without exposure to the protected information, they should not be read into the programme.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://obamawhitehouse.archives.gov/the-press-office/executive-order-classified-national-security-information" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: obamawhitehouse.archives.gov">[whitehouse.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">obamawhitehouse.archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">executive order classified national security information</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Order 13526- Classified National Security InformationDecember 29, 2009 — 29 Dec 2009 — (1) Special access programs shall be limited to pr...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 29, 2009</span></span></span>

In practice, maintaining a reasonably small access population typically involves:

* formally approving each individual for SAP access rather than relying solely on clearance status;
* documenting the operational need for admission;
* reviewing access periodically to remove personnel whose duties have changed;
* creating narrower [compartments]({{ 'compartments/' | relative_url }}) or sub-compartments where only parts of a programme need wider participation.

These administrative controls help ensure that the access roster reflects current operational necessity rather than historical membership or organisational convenience.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cdse.edu/Portals/124/Documents/student-guides/SA001-guide.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cdse.edu">[CDSE]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cdse.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Student Guide Course: Special Access Program (SAP)...February 4, 2020 — Executive Order 13526, “Classified National Security Informa...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 4, 2020</span></span></span>

## Why clearance alone does not grant SAP access

A common misconception is that possession of a [Top Secret clearance]({{ 'clearance-gap/' | relative_url }}) automatically permits access to every programme classified at that level. SAP policy rejects this assumption.

Executive Order 13526 establishes SAPs precisely because the normal clearance process is considered insufficient to protect exceptionally vulnerable information. Consequently, SAP participation requires additional approval beyond the underlying security clearance. Personnel are typically "read into" a specific programme only after confirming both eligibility and a demonstrated need to know.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://obamawhitehouse.archives.gov/the-press-office/executive-order-classified-national-security-information" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: obamawhitehouse.archives.gov">[whitehouse.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">obamawhitehouse.archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">executive order classified national security information</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Order 13526- Classified National Security InformationDecember 29, 2009 — 29 Dec 2009 — (1) Special access programs shall be limited to pr...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 29, 2009</span></span></span>

This distinction serves several purposes.

First, it narrows the number of potential disclosure points. Even if thousands of individuals possess Top Secret eligibility, only a much smaller subset can discuss or access a particular SAP.

Second, it improves [accountability]({{ 'accountability/' | relative_url }}). Programme managers know exactly who has authorised access, making investigations, security reviews and incident response more manageable.

Third, it reinforces compartmentation. An individual may legitimately participate in one SAP while remaining completely excluded from another programme operating at the same classification level because their duties do not require that information.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cdse.edu/Portals/124/Documents/student-guides/SA001-guide.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cdse.edu">[CDSE]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cdse.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Student Guide Course: Special Access Program (SAP)...February 4, 2020 — Executive Order 13526, “Classified National Security Informa...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 4, 2020</span></span></span>

## What large access populations do to compartmentation

Compartmentation works because knowledge is intentionally restricted. As the authorised population grows, several practical problems emerge.

A larger access list increases the number of possible disclosure points, whether through accidental conversations, mishandled documents, [cyber]({{ 'cyber/' | relative_url }}) compromise or insider threats. Each additional authorised individual represents another location where highly sensitive information must be securely stored, transmitted and discussed.

Large populations also weaken administrative oversight. Security officers must maintain personnel records, conduct indoctrinations and debriefings, monitor continuing eligibility and ensure that access remains justified. These tasks become progressively more difficult as membership expands, increasing the likelihood that former personnel retain unnecessary access or that records become outdated.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cdse.edu/Portals/124/Documents/student-guides/SA001-guide.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cdse.edu">[CDSE]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cdse.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Student Guide Course: Special Access Program (SAP)...February 4, 2020 — Executive Order 13526, “Classified National Security Informa...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 4, 2020</span></span></span>

There is also a governance concern. If nearly everyone associated with a project requires SAP admission, the enhanced compartment no longer distinguishes between those who genuinely need exceptional access and those who simply work in the broader organisation. At that point, the rationale for maintaining a separate SAP may become harder to justify under the Executive Order's requirement that special access be reserved for exceptional circumstances.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://obamawhitehouse.archives.gov/the-press-office/executive-order-classified-national-security-information" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: obamawhitehouse.archives.gov">[whitehouse.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">obamawhitehouse.archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">executive order classified national security information</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Order 13526- Classified National Security InformationDecember 29, 2009 — 29 Dec 2009 — (1) Special access programs shall be limited to pr...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 29, 2009</span></span></span>

## Keeping access lists effective over time

The requirement for a reasonably small access population is not satisfied only when a SAP is created. It must be maintained throughout the programme's life.

Executive Order 13526 requires annual review of each SAP to determine whether it continues to satisfy the Order's requirements. Access populations are part of that continuing assessment because programme growth, mission changes or organisational restructuring may gradually expand membership beyond what enhanced compartmentation requires.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://obamawhitehouse.archives.gov/the-press-office/executive-order-classified-national-security-information" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: obamawhitehouse.archives.gov">[whitehouse.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">obamawhitehouse.archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">executive order classified national security information</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Order 13526- Classified National Security InformationDecember 29, 2009 — 29 Dec 2009 — (1) Special access programs shall be limited to pr...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 29, 2009</span></span></span>

Current Department of Defense policy similarly emphasises central oversight of SAP personnel access, programme reviews and authoritative records that allow oversight bodies to monitor who has been admitted to protected programmes. ## Why this safeguard matters

The requirement for reasonably small access lists protects the integrity of the SAP system itself. Enhanced security measures have practical value only when they are applied to a limited community whose participation is demonstrably necessary.

By requiring formal admission based on programme-specific need to know—not simply possession of a high-level clearance—the Executive Order preserves compartmentation as a genuine security control. Small, carefully managed access populations make enhanced oversight feasible, reduce opportunities for unauthorised disclosure and ensure that SAP protections remain proportionate to the exceptional risks they are intended to address.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://obamawhitehouse.archives.gov/the-press-office/executive-order-classified-national-security-information" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: obamawhitehouse.archives.gov">[archives.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">obamawhitehouse.archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">executive order classified national security information</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Order 13526- Classified National Security InformationDecember 29, 2009 — 29 Dec 2009 — (1) Special access programs shall be limited to pr...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 29, 2009</span></span></span>
