## Introduction

A contractor's involvement in a Special Access Program (SAP) does not end when the final deliverable is accepted or the period of performance expires. From a security perspective, contract close-out is a distinct event that determines whether highly sensitive information, equipment, records and access remain appropriately controlled after work has stopped. For SAPs—including acknowledged, unacknowledged and, where applicable, waived programmes—the close-out phase is designed to eliminate unnecessary possession of protected information while preserving only those materials the government specifically authorises a contractor to retain.



<img src="{{ "/assets/images/special-access-progr_a1c931_contractor_sap_acces_2f3a1e_sap_contract_closeou_436895-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="Close Out illustration 1" data-theme-src-dark="{{ "/assets/images/special-access-progr_a1c931_contractor_sap_acces_2f3a1e_sap_contract_closeou_436895-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{ "/assets/images/special-access-progr_a1c931_contractor_sap_acces_2f3a1e_sap_contract_closeou_436895-Illustration-1-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">

Unlike ordinary administrative contract closure, SAP close-out requires deliberate decisions about classified documents, digital data, prototypes, specialised hardware, secure facilities, personnel access and long-term retention. The objective is to ensure that sensitive programme information does not remain dispersed throughout industry after the contractual need has ended.

## Why contractor possession must be unwound

A contractor may accumulate far more than finished reports during the life of a SAP. Depending on the work performed, the company could possess:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--metric" markdown="1">

* Classified design files and engineering drawings.
* Test data and software repositories.
* Prototype components and specialised tooling.
* Cryptographic or communications equipment.
* Classified media and backups.
* Security documentation generated during programme execution.
* Programme-specific knowledge embedded in cleared personnel.

</div>

When active performance ends, the original "need to know" supporting possession of these materials often ends as well. SAP security policy therefore treats close-out as a controlled reduction of exposure rather than a simple administrative milestone.

This process is particularly important because classified information frequently exists in multiple forms. Destroying paper records alone is insufficient if identical information remains in engineering databases, archived storage, removable media or secure laboratory equipment. ## Inventories, destruction and retention decisions

Close-out normally begins with determining exactly what classified material exists and what its authorised disposition should be.

Rather than assuming everything must be destroyed, the government decides among several possibilities:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **Return to the government** where records or equipment remain operationally valuable.
* **Authorised destruction** using approved classified destruction procedures.
* **Transfer to a successor contract** if work continues under a replacement effort.
* **Government-approved retention** where future programme support or legal requirements justify continued possession.

</div>

The contractor cannot simply retain classified SAP material because it might prove useful later. Continued possession requires explicit government authority.

One of the mechanisms supporting this decision is the [DD Form 254]({{ 'dd-form-254/' | relative_url }}), the Contract Security Classification Specification. Its instructions provide for a **Final DD Form 254**, which is issued only after contract completion when the Government Contracting Activity determines—following a formal contractor request—that continued retention beyond the automatic retention period is justified. Without such approval, classified material is not retained indefinitely merely because it originated during contract performance. The instructions also distinguish follow-on [contracts]({{ 'contracts/' | relative_url }}), which may authorise transfer of specified classified material to the replacement contract rather than requiring destruction and recreation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/portals/54/documents/dd/forms/dd/dd0254-Inst.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">DD Form 254-Inst, &quot;Instructions for DoD Contract Security...July 15, 2025 — The purpose of this document is to levy uniform requireme...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 15, 2025</span></span></span>

## Retention is the exception, not the default

An important feature of SAP close-out is that retention decisions are documented rather than assumed.

If a contractor believes future maintenance, litigation, warranty obligations or continuing programme support require classified material to remain in company custody, that need must be explained to the Government Contracting Activity. If approved, the retention authority becomes part of the security record rather than an informal understanding.

This approach serves several purposes:

* it reduces unnecessary long-term storage of highly sensitive information;
* it creates an auditable record explaining why classified material remains outside government custody;
* it limits future [uncertainty]({{ 'uncertainty/' | relative_url }}) over ownership and authorised use; and
* it prevents obsolete copies from accumulating across multiple contractors over many years.

Because SAP information frequently exceeds ordinary classified handling requirements, programme security offices may also impose additional close-out procedures beyond the baseline industrial security rules. Those programme-specific requirements are typically incorporated into the contract itself or referenced through accompanying security guidance.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/portals/54/documents/dd/forms/dd/dd0254-Inst.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[whs.mil]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">DD Form 254-Inst, &quot;Instructions for DoD Contract Security...July 15, 2025 — The purpose of this document is to levy uniform requireme...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 15, 2025</span></span></span>

## How access termination protects residual secrets

Material [accountability]({{ 'accountability/' | relative_url }}) alone does not complete SAP close-out. Human access must also be unwound.

Once contractual duties cease, programme managers and security officials determine which personnel no longer require SAP access. Terminating programme access reduces the population of individuals who may legitimately receive updates, participate in meetings or obtain future classified information associated with that compartment.

Access termination generally accompanies other administrative actions, including:

* removal from programme [access lists]({{ 'access-lists/' | relative_url }});

<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* termination of programme-specific systems or facility access where appropriate;
* confirmation that security responsibilities continue after departure; and
* maintenance of records documenting previous access for future security and investigative purposes.

</div>

Ending SAP access does not erase an individual's knowledge or remove continuing legal obligations. Cleared personnel remain bound by non-disclosure commitments and applicable laws protecting classified information even after employment or contract performance ends. The practical purpose of [debriefing]({{ 'debriefing/' | relative_url }}) and access termination is therefore to reinforce continuing responsibilities while ensuring the individual is no longer treated as an authorised participant in the programme.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/portals/54/documents/dd/forms/dd/dd0254-Inst.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">DD Form 254-Inst, &quot;Instructions for DoD Contract Security...July 15, 2025 — The purpose of this document is to levy uniform requireme...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 15, 2025</span></span></span>

## Residual hardware and secure environments

Close-out may also involve physical infrastructure rather than documents alone.

Some contracts leave contractors with government-furnished equipment, classified laboratory systems, secure communications devices or specialised manufacturing equipment that cannot simply remain in routine industrial use after programme completion.

Depending on programme requirements, officials may require:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* return of government-furnished property;
* sanitisation or destruction of classified storage media;
* reconfiguration of secure information systems;
* removal of programme-specific identifiers or access mechanisms; and
* verification that secure work areas no longer contain programme materials.

</div>

Where Sensitive Compartmented Information Facilities (SCIFs) or SAP facilities were established specifically for contract performance, programme security authorities also determine whether those spaces remain authorised for future work or should be deactivated or repurposed under applicable security procedures. These decisions prevent residual technical capabilities from becoming uncontrolled repositories of programme information.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/portals/54/documents/dd/forms/dd/dd0254-Inst.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">DD Form 254-Inst, &quot;Instructions for DoD Contract Security...July 15, 2025 — The purpose of this document is to levy uniform requireme...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 15, 2025</span></span></span>

## Why close-out matters for compartmented industry

The compartmented industrial model deliberately distributes sensitive work across multiple contractors, subcontractors and specialised facilities. That same distribution creates a corresponding security challenge at the end of a programme phase: every location that legitimately received SAP information must also complete an orderly reduction of classified holdings.

Without structured close-out, classified information could remain scattered among inactive projects, archived engineering systems or former programme teams long after operational necessity has disappeared. By requiring inventories, documented disposition decisions, controlled retention authority and termination of unnecessary access, SAP close-out transforms the end of contract performance into a deliberate security event that reduces the long-term exposure of exceptionally sensitive national security information.
