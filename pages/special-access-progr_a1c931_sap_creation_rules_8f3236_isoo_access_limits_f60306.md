## Introduction

Executive Order 13526 does not exempt Special Access Programs (SAPs) from independent executive-branch oversight simply because they are exceptionally secret. Instead, it creates a carefully balanced rule: every SAP is subject to oversight, but the practical scope of that oversight can itself be restricted when a programme is extraordinarily sensitive. The key safeguard is the role of the Director of the Information Security Oversight Office ([ISOO]({{ 'isoo/' | relative_url }})), who is granted access to SAPs so that ISOO can perform its government-wide oversight responsibilities. At the same time, the order recognises that some programmes are so vulnerable that access may be narrowed to the ISOO Director alone, rather than the wider ISOO staff. This arrangement illustrates the executive order's central compromise between protecting the government's most sensitive information and preserving an independent mechanism for [accountability]({{ 'accountability/' | relative_url }}).<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://obamawhitehouse.archives.gov/the-press-office/executive-order-classified-national-security-information" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: obamawhitehouse.archives.gov">[whitehouse.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">obamawhitehouse.archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Order 13526- Classified National Security InformationDecember 29, 2009 — 29 Dec 2009 — (1) Special access programs shall be limited to pr...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 29, 2009</span></span></span>

## Who Watches the Most Sensitive SAPs?

Executive Order 13526 makes clear that SAPs are not intended to become oversight-free zones. Section 4.3 requires agencies to maintain [accounting]({{ 'accounting/' | relative_url }}) systems for SAPs and subjects those programmes to the executive oversight framework established elsewhere in the order. In addition, the Director of ISOO must be afforded access to SAPs so that the office can perform its statutory and executive-order responsibilities for overseeing the national security classification system.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://obamawhitehouse.archives.gov/the-press-office/executive-order-classified-national-security-information" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: obamawhitehouse.archives.gov">[whitehouse.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">obamawhitehouse.archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Order 13526- Classified National Security InformationDecember 29, 2009 — 29 Dec 2009 — (1) Special access programs shall be limited to pr...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 29, 2009</span></span></span>

This oversight role is distinctive because ISOO is not a programme operator. Located within the National Archives and Records Administration, ISOO develops implementing directives for the classification system, evaluates agency compliance, conducts [inspections]({{ 'inspections/' | relative_url }}), and reports on the effectiveness of classification policy across the executive branch. SAP oversight therefore serves a governance purpose rather than an operational one: ensuring that agencies continue to meet the executive order's legal requirements for creating, maintaining and reviewing exceptionally protected programmes.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cdse.edu/Portals/124/Documents/student-guides/SA001-guide.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cdse.edu">[CDSE]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cdse.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Student Guide Course: Special Access Program (SAP</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Student Guide Course: Special Access Program (SAP)...February 4, 2020 — Executive Order 13526, “Classified National Security Informa...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 4, 2020</span></span></span>

The order deliberately ties SAP oversight to the broader classification system rather than treating SAPs as a separate legal universe. Even programmes with additional security controls remain part of the executive order's overall framework for classification management.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://obamawhitehouse.archives.gov/the-press-office/executive-order-classified-national-security-information" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: obamawhitehouse.archives.gov">[whitehouse.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">obamawhitehouse.archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Order 13526- Classified National Security InformationDecember 29, 2009 — 29 Dec 2009 — (1) Special access programs shall be limited to pr...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 29, 2009</span></span></span>

## Why Can Access Be Limited to the ISOO Director?

The executive order contains an unusual qualification that applies only to the most sensitive programmes.

Ordinarily, an agency may limit ISOO access to:

* the Director of ISOO; and<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://nsarchive.gwu.edu/sites/default/files/documents/4106546/United-States-Department-of-Defense-Information.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nsarchive.gwu.edu">[nsarchive.gwu.edu]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nsarchive.gwu.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">United States Department of Defense Information</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Director, ISOO. Waiver requests for Special Access Programs will be forwarded to the Director. Special Programs, ODTUSD(P)PS, who will t...</span></span></span>
* no more than one additional ISOO employee.

For SAPs that are considered "extraordinarily sensitive and vulnerable", the agency head may further restrict access to the Director of ISOO alone.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://obamawhitehouse.archives.gov/the-press-office/executive-order-classified-national-security-information" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: obamawhitehouse.archives.gov">[whitehouse.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">obamawhitehouse.archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Order 13526- Classified National Security InformationDecember 29, 2009 — 29 Dec 2009 — (1) Special access programs shall be limited to pr...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 29, 2009</span></span></span>

This provision recognises that oversight itself can create security risk. Every additional person who learns programme details represents another potential avenue for accidental disclosure, insider compromise or operational exposure. Rather than eliminating oversight, the executive order reduces the number of overseers to the smallest practical level while preserving an independent point of review.

The wording is also significant because it does not permit agencies to exclude ISOO entirely. Even where the programme is extraordinarily sensitive, the Director remains the designated oversight official. The exception narrows the oversight team; it does not eliminate oversight as a legal requirement.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://obamawhitehouse.archives.gov/the-press-office/executive-order-classified-national-security-information" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: obamawhitehouse.archives.gov">[whitehouse.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">obamawhitehouse.archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Order 13526- Classified National Security InformationDecember 29, 2009 — 29 Dec 2009 — (1) Special access programs shall be limited to pr...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 29, 2009</span></span></span>

## The Balance Between Secrecy and Independent Oversight

The access limitation illustrates a recurring principle within Executive Order 13526: exceptional protection must be matched by identifiable accountability.

Without some independent review, agencies could theoretically expand SAP protections indefinitely or retain programmes that no longer satisfy the executive order's strict creation criteria. ISOO's involvement provides an institutional check by helping ensure that:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* enhanced controls remain justified by exceptional vulnerability;
* agencies maintain required accounting systems for SAPs;
* programmes continue to comply with executive-order standards; and
* classification policy remains subject to government-wide oversight rather than purely internal programme management.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://obamawhitehouse.archives.gov/the-press-office/executive-order-classified-national-security-information" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: obamawhitehouse.archives.gov">[whitehouse.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">obamawhitehouse.archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Order 13526- Classified National Security InformationDecember 29, 2009 — 29 Dec 2009 — (1) Special access programs shall be limited to pr...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 29, 2009</span></span></span>

</div>

At the same time, the oversight mechanism avoids requiring large inspection teams with routine access to the government's most sensitive capabilities. Instead, it relies on a very small number of cleared officials operating under the programme's own security requirements. The result is a deliberately narrow oversight channel rather than unrestricted external inspection.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://obamawhitehouse.archives.gov/the-press-office/executive-order-classified-national-security-information" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: obamawhitehouse.archives.gov">[whitehouse.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">obamawhitehouse.archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Order 13526- Classified National Security InformationDecember 29, 2009 — 29 Dec 2009 — (1) Special access programs shall be limited to pr...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 29, 2009</span></span></span>

## What This Means for Highly Sensitive and Waived Programmes

Readers often assume that unacknowledged or waived SAPs exist outside ordinary executive oversight because their existence is itself closely held. The executive order points in a different direction. Although public knowledge and congressional reporting rules may differ across categories of SAPs, executive-branch oversight under the classification system still applies unless another legal authority specifically provides otherwise. The ISOO access provision therefore demonstrates that greater operational secrecy does not automatically eliminate institutional review inside the executive branch.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://obamawhitehouse.archives.gov/the-press-office/executive-order-classified-national-security-information" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: obamawhitehouse.archives.gov">[whitehouse.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">obamawhitehouse.archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Order 13526- Classified National Security InformationDecember 29, 2009 — 29 Dec 2009 — (1) Special access programs shall be limited to pr...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 29, 2009</span></span></span>

* **Who is allowed to know operational details?** For extraordinarily sensitive programmes, that circle may be extremely small.
* **Who verifies that the programme is being managed within the executive order's rules?** ISOO retains a defined oversight role, even if access is restricted to the Director alone.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://obamawhitehouse.archives.gov/the-press-office/executive-order-classified-national-security-information" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: obamawhitehouse.archives.gov">[whitehouse.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">obamawhitehouse.archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Order 13526- Classified National Security InformationDecember 29, 2009 — 29 Dec 2009 — (1) Special access programs shall be limited to pr...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 29, 2009</span></span></span>

## The Practical Trade-off

The ISOO access rule reflects an intentional governance compromise rather than a contradiction.

If oversight were unrestricted, agencies could reasonably argue that inspection itself increased the risk to exceptionally sensitive capabilities. If oversight were eliminated entirely, there would be little independent assurance that agencies continued to satisfy the executive order's standards for establishing and maintaining SAPs.

Executive Order 13526 therefore adopts a middle course. It preserves independent executive oversight while allowing the oversight footprint to shrink in proportion to the programme's sensitivity. In doing so, it reinforces a broader principle running throughout the SAP framework: exceptional secrecy may justify exceptional handling, but it does not remove the requirement for accountable governance.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://obamawhitehouse.archives.gov/the-press-office/executive-order-classified-national-security-information" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: obamawhitehouse.archives.gov">[archives.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">obamawhitehouse.archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Order 13526- Classified National Security InformationDecember 29, 2009 — 29 Dec 2009 — (1) Special access programs shall be limited to pr...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 29, 2009</span></span></span>
