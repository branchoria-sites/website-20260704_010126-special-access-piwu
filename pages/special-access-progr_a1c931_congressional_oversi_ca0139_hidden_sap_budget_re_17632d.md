## Introduction

Special Access Program (SAP) funding is often described as “hidden”, but that does not mean it sits entirely outside the congressional budget process. The central feature of the system is that public budget documents may conceal the identity or purpose of especially sensitive programmes, while detailed financial information is provided through classified channels to the congressional defence committees. In practice, the House and Senate Armed Services Committees authorise programmes and establish oversight conditions, while the Defence Subcommittees of the Appropriations Committees examine classified budget justifications, approve funding, and scrutinise proposed spending changes. This arrangement seeks to balance operational secrecy with [Congress]({{ 'congress/' | relative_url }})'s constitutional power over appropriations and oversight.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://irp.fas.org/doddir/dod/5200-1r/appendix_i.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: irp.fas.org">[Intelligence Resource Program]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">irp.fas.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Page. Special Access Program Budget Format. I-2. Congressional Notification of SAP Establishment Format. I-3. Special...Read more...</span></span></span>

The result is a budgeting process that is secret from the public but not intended to be secret from the relevant oversight committees. Even unacknowledged and, within statutory limits, waived SAPs remain tied to authorisation, appropriations, reporting requirements and restrictions on moving money between accounts.

## Why public budget lines can hide SAP funding

Public Department of Defense budget books are designed for transparency where possible, but they do not disclose every sensitive programme individually. Highly classified [acquisition]({{ 'acquisition/' | relative_url }}) and research activities may be embedded within broader budget accounts or represented only by generic descriptions, preventing public identification of the underlying capability or project. In some cases, published justification books explicitly state that additional programme information is available only at a higher classification level.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://comptroller.war.gov/Portals/45/Documents/defbudget/FY2027/budget_justification/pdfs/02_Procurement/PROC_OSD_PB_2027.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: comptroller.war.gov">[comptroller.war.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">comptroller.war.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Justification BookExhibit P-40, Budget Line Item Justification: PB 2027 Office of the Secretary Of Defense. This approach serves two purposes:

* It protects operational details whose disclosure could reveal military capabilities, technologies or vulnerabilities.
* It preserves the legal appropriations structure so Congress still votes on the money, even if much of the supporting justification is classified.

Historical Department of Defense guidance even includes standard formats for classified SAP budget submissions and congressional notification packages, illustrating that classified budgeting follows established procedures rather than an improvised or extra-legal process.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://irp.fas.org/doddir/dod/5200-1r/appendix_i.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: irp.fas.org">[Intelligence Resource Program]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">irp.fas.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Page. The distinction is therefore between **public visibility** and **congressional visibility**. A programme may be invisible in published budget tables while remaining fully documented within classified congressional budget materials.

## How authorisers and appropriators review classified detail

The review process relies on parallel public and classified budget documentation.

Public budget books establish the overall appropriations framework, while classified annexes and Congressional Budget Justification Books provide cleared committee members and authorised professional staff with programme-specific information. Those classified materials typically include funding requests, programme descriptions, schedules, performance issues and justification for requested appropriations. Public budget documents occasionally acknowledge this directly by noting that detailed information is available only in classified justification books submitted to Congress.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://comptroller.war.gov/Portals/45/Documents/defbudget/FY2026/budget_justification/pdfs/03_RDT_and_E/RDTE_SOCOM_PB_2026.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: comptroller.war.gov">[comptroller.war.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">comptroller.war.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">United States Special Operations Command (SOCOM</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Special Access Program Congressional Budget Justification Book (CBJB) to Congress. 7.532. 7.943. 7.565. Within Congress, responsibilities are divided:

* **Authorising committees** review whether programmes should exist, establish legal authorities, impose reporting requirements and recommend authorised funding levels.
* **Appropriations committees**, particularly the defence subcommittees, determine whether money is actually provided and under what conditions it may be spent.

Because committee members and designated staff hold the necessary security clearances and receive programme access when required, they can question officials about costs, schedules, technical risks and execution in closed sessions even though none of those discussions appear in the public record.

This process reflects findings from Government [Accountability]({{ 'accountability/' | relative_url }}) Office reviews, which have noted that SAP acquisition programmes generally pass through the same fundamental planning, budgeting, execution and resource review processes as comparable non-SAP defence programmes, albeit using classified documentation and compartmented access controls.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://irp.fas.org/doddir/dod/5200-1r/appendix_i.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: irp.fas.org">[Intelligence Resource Program]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">irp.fas.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Page. ## How classified budget review differs from ordinary defence spending

The principal difference is not the existence of oversight but the form that oversight takes.

Instead of relying primarily on open hearings and publicly released budget exhibits, committees conduct much of their work through secure facilities, classified hearings and restricted documentation. Cleared [committee staff]({{ 'staff-access/' | relative_url }}) often perform detailed technical and financial analysis before members receive programme briefings.

Committee review can include:

* examination of life-cycle cost estimates;
* assessment of technical maturity and acquisition risk;
* comparison between requested funding and programme progress;
* evaluation of schedule delays or cost growth;
* review of [intelligence]({{ 'intelligence/' | relative_url }}) or operational justification supporting continued secrecy.

Although these deliberations remain classified, they influence the same annual authorisation and appropriations legislation that governs the wider defence budget.

## Where reprogramming rules create oversight leverage

Congressional oversight does not end once appropriations become law. One of the most important continuing controls is the review of **reprogramming**—the movement of appropriated funds between budget activities or programmes after enactment.

The Department of Defense has limited authority to shift funds administratively, but significant reprogrammings normally require prior congressional notification and, under longstanding committee practice, approval from the defence committees before the Department proceeds. These procedures apply regardless of whether the affected programme is publicly acknowledged or classified.

For SAPs, this creates an important oversight mechanism:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--decision" markdown="1">

* the Department cannot freely redirect large sums into sensitive programmes without committee review;
* committees can deny or delay requested transfers;
* lawmakers can require additional classified briefings before approving funding changes;
* repeated concerns about execution can lead committees to fence, reduce or condition future appropriations.

</div>

Consequently, even when the public cannot identify a programme's budget line, Congress retains practical leverage over funding decisions through classified reprogramming review.

## What secrecy does—and does not—remove from congressional control

A common misunderstanding is that "black" or hidden [budgets]({{ 'budgets/' | relative_url }}) operate entirely outside congressional appropriations. The statutory framework points in a different direction.

Secrecy primarily affects **who receives detailed information**, not whether Congress exercises budget authority. Most SAP funding continues to flow through ordinary appropriations accounts authorised and funded by Congress, supported by classified documentation instead of public explanations. Even for the most sensitive programmes, reporting obligations, annual reviews and notification requirements remain part of the governance framework, although access may be limited to a smaller group of committee leaders and authorised personnel in accordance with applicable law and national security requirements.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://irp.fas.org/doddir/dod/5200-1r/appendix_i.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: irp.fas.org">[Intelligence Resource Program]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">irp.fas.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Page. Special Access Program Budget Format. I-2. Congressional Notification of SAP Establishment Format. I-3. Special...Read more...</span></span></span>

The principal accountability challenge is therefore not the absence of oversight but its concentration. Public scrutiny is necessarily limited, while meaningful review depends on a relatively small number of legislators and cleared staff having sufficient access, expertise and institutional continuity to evaluate programmes whose details cannot be debated openly.
