## Introduction

SAP non-disclosure agreements are the personal promise that turns a special-access programme from a guarded file system into a guarded community of people. A worker is not simply told that a programme is classified; they are formally “[read in]({{ 'read-in/' | relative_url }})”, briefed on what must be protected, required to sign a [SAP Indoctrination]({{ 'sapia/' | relative_url }}) Agreement, and made responsible for checking whether anyone else is authorised before discussing SAP information. The key point is that the duty follows the person, not just the job. It can continue after the individual leaves the programme, changes employer, retires, or is no longer inside a SAP facility.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/DD/forms/dd/dd2836.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[WHS ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">WHS ESDspecial access program indoctrination agreementApril 16, 2007 — I further understand that all my obligations under this Agreement...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 16, 2007</span></span></span>



<img src="{{ "/assets/images/special-access-progr_a1c931_sap_ndas_20ccd4-overview.webp" | relative_url }}" alt="Overview image for NDAs" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">

That matters especially for waived and [unacknowledged SAPs]({{ 'unacknowledged/' | relative_url }}), where even acknowledging the programme’s existence may be controlled. In that setting, the NDA is not a routine HR form. It is an implementation tool: it links legal [accountability]({{ 'accountability/' | relative_url }}), security training, access records, debriefing, and day-to-day self-restraint into one enforceable personal duty.

## Why special agreements are used

Ordinary classified-information rules already require secrecy. Executive Order 13526 defines a special-access programme as a programme for a specific class of classified information that imposes safeguarding and access requirements beyond those normally required for information at the same classification level. That extra layer is meant for information judged exceptionally vulnerable or unusually damaging if exposed, not for every secret document.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://obamawhitehouse.archives.gov/the-press-office/executive-order-classified-national-security-information" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: obamawhitehouse.archives.gov">[whitehouse.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">obamawhitehouse.archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">executive order classified national security information</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Order 13526- Classified National Security InformationDecember 29, 2009 — 29 Dec 2009 — This order prescribes a uniform system for classif...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 29, 2009</span></span></span>

The SAP NDA exists because classification by itself does not solve the hardest access-control problem: people who are cleared at the right level may still not be authorised for this programme. A person with a [Top Secret clearance]({{ 'clearance-gap/' | relative_url }}) may be outside a Top Secret SAP. The SAP system therefore needs a programme-specific act of admission, usually described as indoctrination or being “read in”, that identifies the protected information, explains why it needs special protection, and records the individual’s acceptance of the rules. CDSE guidance says an indoctrination brief must be given to every programme-accessed person before access and should include the requirement to execute a non-disclosure agreement.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cdse.edu/Portals/124/Documents/jobaids/sap/SAP_Briefings.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cdse.edu">[CDSE]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cdse.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">SAP Briefings and DebriefingsAn indoctrination brief must be given to every program-accessed individual before they are granted acces...</span></span></span>

DD Form 2836, the Special Access Program Indoctrination Agreement, is the clearest public example of that promise. It states that the signer is being granted access to SAP information, acknowledges that special confidence and trust are being placed in them by the United States Government, and confirms that they have received a security indoctrination on the nature and protection of SAP information.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/DD/forms/dd/dd2836.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[WHS ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">WHS ESDspecial access program indoctrination agreementApril 16, 2007 — I further understand that all my obligations under this Agreement...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 16, 2007</span></span></span>

The practical reason is simple: SAP secrecy depends on human judgement at the edge of conversations, meetings, drafts, emails, travel, contracting and public speech. The NDA gives that judgement a formal standard. It tells the worker that before disclosing anything they must confirm that the recipient is authorised for that SAP, not merely cleared in general.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/DD/forms/dd/dd2836.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[WHS ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">WHS ESDspecial access program indoctrination agreementApril 16, 2007 — I further understand that all my obligations under this Agreement...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 16, 2007</span></span></span>

## What the worker personally promises

The central promise in the SAP Indoctrination Agreement is not just “do not leak”. It is broader and more operational. The signer agrees never to divulge anything marked as SAP information, or known by them to be SAP information, to anyone not authorised to receive it without prior written authorisation from the government department or agency that granted the access.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/DD/forms/dd/dd2836.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[WHS ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">WHS ESDspecial access program indoctrination agreementApril 16, 2007 — I further understand that all my obligations under this Agreement...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 16, 2007</span></span></span>

That wording creates several personal duties at once:

* **Do not disclose to outsiders.** The obvious rule is that SAP information cannot be shared with unauthorised people, including colleagues, family, journalists, academics, contractors, or officials who lack programme access.
* **Check authorisation before speaking.** The agreement refers to procedures for determining whether another person has been approved for access, making the worker responsible for verification rather than assumption.
* **Treat uncertainty as a security issue.** If the worker has reason to believe information may be SAP information, or may be related to or derived from SAP information, the form says they must consult appropriate management authorities.
* **Protect knowledge, not only documents.** The agreement covers what the person knows as well as what they physically hold, which is crucial when the most sensitive fact may be the programme’s existence, purpose, capability or vulnerability.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/DD/forms/dd/dd2836.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[WHS ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">WHS ESDspecial access program indoctrination agreementApril 16, 2007 — I further understand that all my obligations under this Agreement...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 16, 2007</span></span></span>

This is why SAP NDAs feel different from ordinary workplace confidentiality agreements. A private-sector NDA normally protects a company’s commercial information for a defined business purpose. A SAP NDA is tied to classified national-security information, programme access approval, government authorisation, and possible civil, administrative or criminal consequences. The standard classified-information NDA, SF 312, similarly presents access as a legally binding exchange: the person receives classified access and accepts duties not to disclose classified information without authority.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gsa.gov/system/files/2024-06/SF312-23.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gsa.gov">[U.S. General Services Administration]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gsa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">U.S. General Services Administration Standard Form 312</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">U.S. General Services Administration Standard Form 312</span></span></span>

## Continuing duties after access

The most important feature of SAP personal responsibility is that it does not end when access ends. DD Form 2836 states that the signer’s obligations continue even if they are not required to sign later agreements for other categories of SAP information. It also says the person must consult the department or agency that last authorised their SAP access, whether or not they are still employed by or associated with that agency or its contractor, if they have reason to believe information in their knowledge or control may be SAP information.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/DD/forms/dd/dd2836.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[WHS ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">WHS ESDspecial access program indoctrination agreementApril 16, 2007 — I further understand that all my obligations under this Agreement...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 16, 2007</span></span></span>

That is the mechanism behind the common security phrase “continuing obligations”. A worker may be debriefed, lose need-to-know, leave a contractor, or retire, but they do not gain the right to confirm a SAP’s existence, describe its details, retain marked material, or use programme-derived knowledge in public writing without the proper review and authorisation. CDSE termination-briefing material makes the same general classified-information point: separation from an organisation does not lessen responsibility or liability, and departing personnel may not retain classified information or controlled unclassified information.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cdse.edu/Portals/124/Documents/student-guides/shorts/GSS0066-guide.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cdse.edu">[CDSE]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cdse.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Student Guide</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Student Guide</span></span></span>

[Debriefing]({{ 'debriefing/' | relative_url }}) is therefore not a release from secrecy. It is a final controlled handoff. SAP briefing guidance treats indoctrination and debriefing as managed events in the access lifecycle, while DCSA retention guidance separately lists access briefing statements, including pre-briefings, indoctrinations and debriefings, as records maintained by programme security offices.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cdse.edu/Portals/124/Documents/jobaids/sap/SAP_Briefings.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cdse.edu">[CDSE]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cdse.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">SAP Briefings and DebriefingsAn indoctrination brief must be given to every program-accessed individual before they are granted acces...</span></span></span>

For a former SAP worker, the practical consequence is that silence is not optional nostalgia for an old job. It is part of the original bargain. Even a memoir, lecture, podcast appearance, lawsuit declaration, technical paper or informal conversation can raise a duty to seek review if it may contain SAP information or material derived from it. NSA describes prepublication review more broadly as the process for determining whether material proposed for public release contains protected information, including classified information or information protected by statute.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nsa.gov/Helpful-Links/Prepublication-Review/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nsa.gov">[nsa.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nsa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on nsa.gov.</span></span></span>

## How personal responsibility supports controls

A SAP cannot be protected by locked rooms and computer permissions alone. Its access model depends on every admitted person acting as a small access-control point. The NDA supports that model by giving each worker three linked responsibilities: understand the boundary, verify others before disclosure, and report uncertainty through authorised channels.

This personal layer is especially important in contractor and joint environments. SAP work often involves military personnel, civilian officials, laboratories, prime contractors, subcontractors and specialised facilities. DCSA describes its SAP resources as a repository for SAP-specific processes, procedures, documents, forms and templates for government organisations and contractor companies under the DoD SAP Security Manual.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.dcsa.mil/Industrial-Security/Special-Access-Programs-Templates/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dcsa.mil">[DCSA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dcsa.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on dcsa.mil.</span></span></span>

The NDA also reinforces the “need to know” principle. Need to know is not a social judgement about whether someone is trustworthy; it is an access decision tied to official duties and programme approval. DoD information-security guidance states that the individual with authorised possession, knowledge or control of classified information has responsibility for determining whether a prospective recipient’s duties require access and whether the person has the appropriate clearance. SAP rules add the further question: has that person been approved for this SAP?<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.dodig.mil/Portals/48/Documents/Policy/520001_vol3.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dodig.mil">[DOD Intelligence Agency]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dodig.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">520001 vol3</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">520001 vol3</span></span></span>

In practice, this means the worker must resist several common failure modes:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* assuming a senior official is automatically authorised;
* discussing a programme with a cleared colleague who lacks SAP access;
* moving programme details into a less protected email, chat, slide deck or contract file;
* reusing SAP-derived knowledge in an unclassified setting without review;
* treating debriefing as permission to speak once employment ends.

</div>

The NDA is therefore not just punitive. It gives the security office a repeatable way to train, record and remind people of the same rule: access is specific, documented and conditional.

## What happens if the promise is broken

The SAP Indoctrination Agreement warns that unauthorised disclosure, unauthorised retention or negligent handling of SAP information could cause irreparable injury to the United States or benefit a foreign nation. It also reminds the signer that they are bound by law and regulation not to disclose classified information in an unauthorised way.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/DD/forms/dd/dd2836.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[WHS ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">WHS ESDspecial access program indoctrination agreementApril 16, 2007 — I further understand that all my obligations under this Agreement...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 16, 2007</span></span></span>

The consequences can operate on several tracks. Administratively, a breach can lead to loss of programme access, loss of clearance eligibility, removal from a contract or employment action. Contractually, a signed NDA can support civil enforcement. In the broader secrecy-law context, the Supreme Court’s decision in *Snepp v United States* upheld enforcement of a CIA prepublication-review agreement against a former employee who published without submitting the manuscript for review, illustrating how post-employment secrecy promises can have real legal effect.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/444/507/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: supreme.justia.com">[Justia Law]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">supreme.justia.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on justia.com.</span></span></span>

Criminal law is a separate question and depends on the facts and statute. For example, 18 U.S.C. § 798 criminalises knowing and wilful unauthorised communication, publication or use of certain classified communications-[intelligence]({{ 'intelligence/' | relative_url }}) and cryptographic information. 18 U.S.C. § 793 covers several forms of gathering, transmitting or losing national-defence information. Not every NDA breach is automatically a criminal offence, but SAP information can sit inside categories where criminal exposure is possible if statutory elements are met.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/18/798" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: law.cornell.edu">[Legal Information Institute]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">law.cornell.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Legal Information Institute18 U.S. Code § 798</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Legal Information Institute18 U.S. Code § 798</span></span></span>

The strongest practical lesson is that the NDA is part of a layered enforcement system. The agreement records the promise; the access register shows who was admitted; the briefing explains the rules; the marking system identifies protected material; debriefing closes formal access; and law or policy supplies consequences if the person disregards those duties.

## Lawful reporting is not the same as public disclosure

A SAP NDA does not mean that a worker has no lawful route to report wrongdoing. The distinction is between authorised protected reporting and unauthorised public disclosure. Modern SF 312 language states that nothing in the agreement should be construed to prohibit or restrict lawful whistleblower disclosures. ISOO explained that SF 312 was revised to conform with statutes including the Whistleblower Protection Enhancement Act of 2012.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gsa.gov/system/files/2024-06/SF312-23.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gsa.gov">[U.S. General Services Administration]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gsa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">U.S. General Services Administration Standard Form 312</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">U.S. General Services Administration Standard Form 312</span></span></span>

For intelligence-community matters, 50 U.S.C. § 3033 provides that an individual may disclose classified information to the Intelligence Community Inspector General in accordance with applicable security standards and procedures. It also states that a qualifying disclosure made in accordance with those procedures is treated as authorised for covered provisions, including otherwise applicable non-disclosure agreements, while preserving the obligation to store, handle and disseminate classified information properly.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/50/3033" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: law.cornell.edu">[Legal Information Institute]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">law.cornell.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cornell.edu.</span></span></span>

This distinction is crucial for unacknowledged and waived SAPs. The existence of a tightly controlled reporting channel does not make public confirmation safe or lawful. Conversely, the existence of an NDA does not erase authorised oversight, inspector-general procedures or legally protected disclosures. The worker’s personal duty is to use the right channel, not to decide alone that public disclosure is justified.

## The core implementation trade-off

SAP NDAs make secrecy durable by attaching it to individuals, not only to buildings, networks or classification stamps. That is their strength. They help stop sensitive facts from leaking through casual conversation, career moves, contractor churn, memoirs, conference talks, litigation filings or misplaced confidence in another person’s clearance. For especially sensitive SAPs, including unacknowledged programmes where the fact of existence may be protected, that durability is central to the control design.

The trade-off is that personal secrecy duties can also make accountability harder to navigate. A conscientious worker may know something troubling but be unsure which office can hear it. A former worker may be uncertain whether a memory, anecdote or technical idea is programme-derived. A cleared official may have authority in one domain but no access to the relevant SAP. The NDA does not resolve those tensions by itself; it channels them into authorised consultation, prepublication review, programme security offices, inspector-general routes and congressional oversight mechanisms.

The best way to understand SAP non-disclosure agreements is therefore not as paperwork at the edge of a black programme, but as the personal-control layer inside the programme. They tell each admitted person: this access is specific, this knowledge remains protected, and when in doubt, you do not disclose first and sort it out later.
