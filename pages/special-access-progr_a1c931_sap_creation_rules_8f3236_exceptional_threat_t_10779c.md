## Introduction

The executive-order test for creating a Special Access Program (SAP) begins with a demanding question: **would the normal system for protecting classified information actually fail to protect this specific information?** Under Executive Order 13526, enhanced compartmentation is not justified simply because information is highly classified, politically sensitive, technologically advanced, or operationally important. Instead, an authorised official must make a specific finding that the information faces an **exceptional vulnerability or threat** and that the ordinary clearance and need-to-know system is insufficient to prevent unauthorised disclosure.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://obamawhitehouse.archives.gov/the-press-office/executive-order-classified-national-security-information" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: obamawhitehouse.archives.gov">[archives.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">obamawhitehouse.archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Order 13526- Classified National Security InformationDecember 29, 2009 — 29 Dec 2009 — (1) Special access programs shall be limited to pr...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 29, 2009</span></span></span>

This "exceptional threat" requirement is the core policy safeguard against overusing SAPs. It ensures that special access is treated as an extraordinary protection measure rather than a routine administrative preference. The threshold is deliberately higher than the standard used to classify information in the first place.

## What Counts as an Exceptional Vulnerability or Threat?

The executive order does not provide a checklist of qualifying situations. Instead, it establishes a standard that agencies must justify on the facts of the particular programme. The emphasis is on whether a defined class of classified information is exposed to risks that ordinary security controls cannot adequately manage.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://obamawhitehouse.archives.gov/the-press-office/executive-order-classified-national-security-information" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: obamawhitehouse.archives.gov">[whitehouse.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">obamawhitehouse.archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Order 13526- Classified National Security InformationDecember 29, 2009 — 29 Dec 2009 — (1) Special access programs shall be limited to pr...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 29, 2009</span></span></span>

In practice, an exceptional vulnerability may arise where disclosure would create consequences far beyond those normally associated with information at the same classification level. Examples commonly discussed in official guidance include:

* highly sensitive military capabilities whose exposure would allow an adversary to develop countermeasures before deployment;
* [intelligence]({{ 'intelligence/' | relative_url }}) [operations]({{ 'operations/' | relative_url }}) in which revealing even limited details could expose sources, methods, or covert relationships;
* advanced technologies where knowledge of design, testing, or performance would erase a critical strategic advantage;
* tightly integrated operational plans in which seemingly minor disclosures could reveal the larger capability through aggregation.

The key point is that the vulnerability must be **specific**, not hypothetical. The decision should identify the information requiring extra protection and explain why conventional classified handling would not adequately protect it.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cdse.edu/Portals/124/Documents/student-guides/SA001-guide.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cdse.edu">[cdse.edu]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cdse.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">SA001 guide</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Student Guide Course: Special Access Program (SAP)...Executive Order 13526, “Classified National Security Information,” defines a SA...</span></span></span>

## Why Ordinary Clearance Rules May Be Insufficient

The normal United States classification system already incorporates substantial safeguards. Access generally requires:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--decision" markdown="1">

* the appropriate security clearance;
* a demonstrated need to know;
* approved handling procedures for the classification level involved.

</div>

A SAP is therefore intended only for situations where these existing mechanisms leave an unacceptable residual risk. Executive Order 13526 expressly links SAP creation to a finding that "the normal criteria for determining eligibility for access" are insufficient.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://obamawhitehouse.archives.gov/the-press-office/executive-order-classified-national-security-information" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: obamawhitehouse.archives.gov">[whitehouse.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">obamawhitehouse.archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Order 13526- Classified National Security InformationDecember 29, 2009 — 29 Dec 2009 — (1) Special access programs shall be limited to pr...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 29, 2009</span></span></span>

Several practical circumstances can justify that conclusion.

First, the information may be unusually vulnerable to insider compromise because even a small number of authorised recipients would have sufficient knowledge to reveal a critical capability.

Second, disclosure of isolated technical details may enable an adversary to reconstruct an entire programme. Modern intelligence analysis frequently exploits the "mosaic effect", in which individually unremarkable facts become strategically significant when combined. Limiting access beyond ordinary clearance requirements can reduce opportunities for that reconstruction.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.justice.gov/oip/media/1402541/dl?inline=" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: justice.gov">[Department of Justice]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">justice.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Department of JusticeNational Security Classification And Exemption 1 of the...May 29, 2026 — EO 13526 recognizes that when pieces of un...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 29, 2026</span></span></span>

Third, some programmes involve technologies or operations whose value depends heavily on surprise. Once an adversary understands the capability, years of research, operational planning, or intelligence collection may lose much of their effectiveness. In those circumstances, additional compartmentation may provide protection that ordinary classified handling cannot.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/portals/54/documents/dd/issuances/dodd/520507p.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">a. The DoD will establish and apply SAP security protection to classified national security information in accordance with E.O. 13526...</span></span></span>

## The Decision Is About Risk, Not Prestige

One common misunderstanding is that SAPs exist because information is simply "more secret" than Top Secret information. Executive Order 13526 rejects that idea.

SAPs do not establish a classification level above Top Secret. Instead, they impose additional administrative and access controls on information already classified at one of the standard levels—Confidential, Secret, or Top Secret. The enhanced protection addresses an identified security problem rather than creating a higher category of classification.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cdse.edu/Portals/124/Documents/student-guides/SA001-guide.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cdse.edu">[CDSE]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cdse.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">SA001 guide</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Student Guide Course: Special Access Program (SAP)...Executive Order 13526, “Classified National Security Information,” defines a SA...</span></span></span>

This distinction matters because many highly classified activities never become SAPs. If existing classification procedures can reasonably protect the information, creating a SAP would not satisfy the executive-order standard.

## Examples That Meet the Executive-Order Threshold

While agencies rarely publish detailed justifications for individual SAPs, official policy and training materials illustrate the types of situations that fit the exceptional-threat standard.

**Advanced defence technology.** A developmental aircraft, sensor, or [cyber]({{ 'cyber/' | relative_url }}) capability whose compromise would permit foreign intelligence services to copy, defeat, or counter the technology before deployment may warrant enhanced compartmentation if ordinary classified access would expose too many people to sensitive design information.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/portals/54/documents/dd/issuances/dodd/520507p.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">a. The DoD will establish and apply SAP security protection to classified national security information in accordance with E.O. 13526...</span></span></span>

**Sensitive operational capabilities.** Programmes supporting unique military operations may require stricter access controls where premature disclosure would endanger personnel, eliminate operational surprise, or expose specialised tactics.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/portals/54/documents/dd/issuances/dodd/520507p.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">a. The DoD will establish and apply SAP security protection to classified national security information in accordance with E.O. 13526...</span></span></span>

**Exceptionally sensitive intelligence information.** Certain intelligence-related activities involve sources or methods whose compromise could permanently destroy collection capabilities or place individuals at severe risk. In such circumstances, ordinary clearance procedures may not provide sufficient protection.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://obamawhitehouse.archives.gov/the-press-office/executive-order-classified-national-security-information" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: obamawhitehouse.archives.gov">[whitehouse.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">obamawhitehouse.archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Order 13526- Classified National Security InformationDecember 29, 2009 — 29 Dec 2009 — (1) Special access programs shall be limited to pr...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 29, 2009</span></span></span>

These examples share a common feature: the enhanced controls respond to an identifiable protection problem rather than to the general importance of the programme.

## How Agencies Assess the Threat

Although Executive Order 13526 states the legal threshold, agencies translate that requirement into structured risk assessment. Security guidance emphasises evaluating:

## Why the Threshold Matters for Governance

The exceptional-threat test is also an [accountability]({{ 'accountability/' | relative_url }}) mechanism. Without it, agencies could be tempted to place particularly sensitive, controversial, or high-profile activities into increasingly restrictive [compartments]({{ 'compartments/' | relative_url }}) simply because doing so limits access.

Executive Order 13526 instead requires that SAPs remain exceptional. Creation authority is restricted to designated senior officials, the number of programmes is to be kept to an absolute minimum, and SAPs remain subject to [accounting]({{ 'accounting/' | relative_url }}) and oversight requirements despite their enhanced secrecy. The exceptional-threat finding therefore functions as both a security judgement and a governance restraint, ensuring that special access is reserved for situations where ordinary classified protections demonstrably fall short.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://obamawhitehouse.archives.gov/the-press-office/executive-order-classified-national-security-information" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: obamawhitehouse.archives.gov">[archives.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">obamawhitehouse.archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Order 13526- Classified National Security InformationDecember 29, 2009 — 29 Dec 2009 — (1) Special access programs shall be limited to pr...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 29, 2009</span></span></span>
