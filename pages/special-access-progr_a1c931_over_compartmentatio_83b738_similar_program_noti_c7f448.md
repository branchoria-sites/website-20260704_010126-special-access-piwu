## Introduction

[Congress]({{ 'congress/' | relative_url }}) does not allow the classified nature of a special access programme (SAP) to become an excuse for creating unnecessary duplicates. One of the less visible safeguards in the statutory oversight process is the requirement that proposals for certain new SAPs identify existing programmes or technologies with a similar mission or technical purpose. The aim is straightforward: before approving another highly compartmented effort, reviewers should be able to judge whether the proposed programme fills a genuine gap or merely recreates work already under way elsewhere.



<img src="{{ "/assets/images/special-access-progr_a1c931_over_compartmentatio_83b738_similar_program_noti_c7f448-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="Similar Programs illustration 1" data-theme-src-dark="{{ "/assets/images/special-access-progr_a1c931_over_compartmentatio_83b738_similar_program_noti_c7f448-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{ "/assets/images/special-access-progr_a1c931_over_compartmentatio_83b738_similar_program_noti_c7f448-Illustration-1-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">

This requirement is particularly important for waived and [unacknowledged SAPs]({{ 'unacknowledged/' | relative_url }}), where only a small group of officials may have visibility into the programme. Without a formal requirement to identify comparable efforts, compartmentation itself can make [duplication]({{ 'duplication/' | relative_url }}) difficult to detect until significant resources have already been committed. Congress therefore uses similarity disclosures as an implementation tool to preserve oversight even when programme details remain classified.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&amp;num=0&amp;req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title10-section119" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: house.gov">[U.S. Code]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">house.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">view.xhtml</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Code10 USC 119: Special access programs: congressional ...(B) an identification of existing programs or technologies that are similar to...</span></span></span>

## What Congress asks new SAPs to disclose

For Department of Defense special access programmes, the principal statutory framework appears in 10 U.S.C. §119, governing congressional oversight of SAPs. Among the information required when establishing certain new SAPs is:

* identification of existing programmes or technologies similar to the proposed technology;
* identification of programmes with a similar mission;
* an explanation of why the proposed effort cannot reasonably be conducted through an existing programme rather than creating a separate compartment.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&amp;num=0&amp;req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title10-section119" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: house.gov">[U.S. Code]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">house.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">view.xhtml</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Code10 USC 119: Special access programs: congressional ...(B) an identification of existing programs or technologies that are similar to...</span></span></span>

The significance of this requirement lies less in the existence of another programme than in the justification for organisational separation. Similarity alone is not prohibited. Defence organisations often pursue related technologies for different operational environments, security communities or technical approaches. Congress instead asks decision-makers to explain why a distinct SAP is warranted despite those similarities.

This requirement therefore functions as a structured challenge to programme sponsors. Instead of merely asserting that a capability is unique, sponsors must demonstrate that existing classified efforts do not already provide an appropriate organisational home.

## Why similarity is harder to prove inside compartments

Ordinary [acquisition]({{ 'acquisition/' | relative_url }}) programmes can often be compared through public budget documents, technical reviews, Government [Accountability]({{ 'accountability/' | relative_url }}) Office (GAO) audits or broad departmental visibility. Highly compartmented SAPs operate under very different conditions.

Several factors complicate comparison:

* **Restricted visibility.** Officials reviewing one compartment may not possess access to every related programme.
* **Different terminology.** Separate organisations may describe comparable technologies using different operational language, making overlaps difficult to recognise.
* **Independent sponsorship.** Military departments, defence agencies and [intelligence]({{ 'intelligence/' | relative_url }}) organisations may each frame similar technical problems differently.
* **Security-driven isolation.** Legitimate "need-to-know" restrictions intentionally limit the number of people able to compare programmes across organisational boundaries.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cdse.edu/Portals/124/Documents/student-guides/SA001-guide.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cdse.edu">[CDSE]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cdse.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Student Guide Course: Special Access Program (SAP)...February 4, 2020 — Special Access Programs, referred to as SAPs, aim to protect...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 4, 2020</span></span></span>

These conditions mean duplication can arise without deliberate waste. Two offices may independently conclude that a capability requires exceptional protection while lacking sufficient visibility into one another's work.

The statutory requirement to identify similar programmes therefore forces comparison at the point where the proposal is created, when sponsors generally have the best understanding of the capability they intend to protect.

## How the notice tests whether a separate programme is necessary

The "similar programme" disclosure is not simply a catalogue of related projects. It supports several practical oversight questions.

**Does an existing SAP already provide adequate protection?**

If another compartment already safeguards closely related critical programme information, reviewers can ask whether expanding that existing compartment would achieve the security objective more efficiently than establishing another one.

**Is the proposed capability genuinely novel?**

Recent Department of Defense SAP policy distinguishes between genuinely new and novel critical programme information and capabilities that fit within the existing SAP architecture. Guidance emphasises grouping similar capabilities together where appropriate rather than creating unnecessary new [compartments]({{ 'compartments/' | relative_url }}).<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/DD/issuances/dodi/520511p.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">DoD Instruction 5205.11, &quot;Management, Administration...12 Sept 2024 — The Director, DoD Special Access Program Central Office (SAPCO...</span></span></span>

**Will another compartment increase unnecessary fragmentation?**

Every additional SAP introduces separate security administration, oversight, reporting requirements and access management. Reviewers therefore consider whether creating another compartment would improve protection enough to justify the additional complexity.

**Has the sponsor seriously examined alternatives?**

Requiring discussion of comparable programmes discourages the assumption that every sensitive capability deserves its own compartment. Instead, sponsors must explain why existing classified structures cannot accommodate the work.

## Relationship to broader anti-duplication oversight

The statutory similarity notice complements, rather than replaces, broader SAP governance.

Current Department of Defense policy directs SAP oversight bodies to organise similar critical programme information within a coherent architecture, to review proposed SAP governance actions and to determine whether new capabilities should be incorporated into existing SAPs instead of creating additional compartments. During lifecycle management, proposals may result in incorporation into an existing SAP, establishment of a new SAP only when justified, or termination if SAP protection is not warranted.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/DD/issuances/dodi/520511p.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">DoD Instruction 5205.11, &quot;Management, Administration...12 Sept 2024 — The Director, DoD Special Access Program Central Office (SAPCO...</span></span></span>

Viewed together, these requirements create multiple opportunities to question unnecessary separation:

Rather than relying on a single approval decision, the process repeatedly asks whether compartmentation continues to serve a real security purpose.

## Why this safeguard matters despite limited public visibility

Because the relevant submissions are themselves classified, the public rarely sees the actual similarity analyses or the discussions they generate. That lack of visibility can make the safeguard easy to overlook.

Its value lies in requiring an explicit record inside the classified oversight process. Officials reviewing a proposed SAP cannot simply evaluate its technical merits; they are expected to consider whether comparable work already exists and whether another compartment would improve security or merely increase fragmentation.

For waived and unacknowledged SAPs, where outside scrutiny is intentionally limited, this requirement helps ensure that exceptional secrecy does not automatically justify organisational duplication. It preserves a mechanism for asking a simple but important question: if similar work already exists somewhere inside the classified system, what specific security or operational reason requires creating another separate programme rather than building upon the one already in place?
