## Introduction

Over-compartmentation is what happens when secrecy is no longer just protecting a sensitive special-access-program, but also preventing the right officials from comparing notes. In the SAP world, that can mean two offices pursue similar capabilities without knowing enough about each other, reviewers cannot test assumptions across programmes, and Congress or senior defence leaders see only fragments of a larger [acquisition]({{ 'acquisition/' | relative_url }}) picture. The risk is not simply “too much secrecy” in the abstract. It is duplication, weak challenge, hidden cost growth, and avoidable failure inside programmes that are already hard to audit because their existence, purpose or details may be restricted. Executive Order 13526 tries to limit SAPs to cases with a reasonably small access population and requires [accounting]({{ 'accounting/' | relative_url }}) and oversight systems, but the historical record shows why those safeguards matter: Congress and GAO have repeatedly worried that SAP controls can narrow oversight precisely when expensive or technically risky programmes need more scrutiny.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://obamawhitehouse.archives.gov/the-press-office/executive-order-classified-national-security-information" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: A real inventory, not just a list of codewords.">[whitehouse.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">A real inventory, not just a list of codewords.</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on archives.gov.</span></span></span>

## Why duplication happens

Special access controls are designed around “need to know”. That is often defensible: a [stealth]({{ 'stealth/' | relative_url }}) technology, [intelligence]({{ 'intelligence/' | relative_url }}) source, cyber capability or operational method may lose much of its value if too many people know it exists. But “need to know” can become too narrow when the people excluded are not curious outsiders, but acquisition reviewers, auditors, budget examiners, technical specialists or adjacent programme managers who would normally spot overlap. GAO’s 1992 unclassified summary of classified work on DoD special access acquisition programmes described tightly controlled access and stringent security measures beyond standard classified programmes; it also noted congressional concern that the growing number of SAPs and DoD’s refusal to provide access to information had limited oversight.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gao.gov/assets/nsiad-93-78.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.gov">[GAO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">NSIAD-93-78 Defense Acquisition: Oversight of Special Access Programs Has Increased</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GAONSIAD-93-78 Defense Acquisition: Oversight of Special Access Programs Has Increased</span></span></span>

Duplication does not require a foolish or corrupt decision at the start. It can emerge from ordinary institutional behaviour under extraordinary secrecy. A service, agency or contractor team may frame its own programme as uniquely sensitive, build a separate security architecture, restrict technical details to a small circle, and brief only a narrow set of officials. Another compartment may then start solving a similar problem with different language, a different threat model, or a different sponsoring office. Because each group has a plausible security reason not to share, no one below a central oversight level can easily ask the simple question: “Are we already paying for this somewhere else?”

The present DoD SAP instruction shows that the Pentagon recognises this risk directly. Officials designated as oversight authorities must ensure that work inside a SAP stays within approved scope, conduct annual reviews of continuing SAP status, and ensure SAPs “do not duplicate or overlap”. The same instruction gives the Special Access Program Oversight Committee, or SAPOC, responsibility for annual review of DoD SAPs, including whether SAP protection is still needed, whether capabilities are ready for wider military apportionment, and whether some programmes should be disestablished when extra protection is no longer warranted.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/DD/issuances/dodi/520511p.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: whs.mil">[ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">520511p</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">520511p</span></span></span>

That wording matters because it treats duplication as a governance problem, not just a budget problem. In ordinary federal programme management, GAO defines duplication, overlap and fragmentation as conditions that can create unnecessary costs, confusion or inefficiency when not managed; its government-wide duplication reports have produced hundreds of billions of dollars in reported savings and revenue gains from congressional and agency action. SAPs sit in a harder category because the inventory itself, the programme purpose and the technical overlaps may be classified or compartmented. The same anti-duplication logic applies, but the evidence needed to apply it is harder to assemble.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-26-108505" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.gov">[GAO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">gao 26 108505</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">gao 26 108505</span></span></span>

## How challenge gets reduced

The most damaging effect of over-compartmentation is not that a programme has fewer readers. It is that challenge becomes local. The people inside the compartment may be talented and patriotic, but they are often embedded in the same assumptions, incentives and technical story. They may know the programme’s classified detail better than anyone else, while lacking enough exposure to competing designs, related programmes or independent scepticism.

GAO’s 1992 review found that sampled SAPs were properly established, but had received less oversight than similarly sized non-SAP acquisition programmes. DoD had used waivers and exemptions to limit review, documentation and reporting requirements, and earlier audits had identified weak internal controls, poor internal communication and inadequate record-keeping. GAO also found that information was sometimes withheld from outside support organisations, limiting their ability to help, and that some programme offices were understaffed or had untrained staff performing important functions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gao.gov/assets/nsiad-93-78.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.gov">[GAO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">NSIAD-93-78 Defense Acquisition: Oversight of Special Access Programs Has Increased</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GAONSIAD-93-78 Defense Acquisition: Oversight of Special Access Programs Has Increased</span></span></span>

That pattern creates three practical failure modes.

**First, technical optimism is harder to test.** A compartmented programme can develop its own internal confidence loop. If outside engineers or test officials cannot see enough detail, they cannot compare the programme’s claims against similar work elsewhere or ask whether a difficult technology has been oversold.

**Second, cost and schedule warnings travel slowly.** Cost growth and delay are common in major defence acquisition, classified or not. But when documentation, review meetings and support [contracts]({{ 'contracts/' | relative_url }}) are restricted, the warning signs may remain inside the same closed circle until the programme is already politically and financially exposed.

**Third, budget comparison becomes artificial.** A congressional committee or senior official may see one programme’s request without seeing the adjacent programme that would make it look duplicative, premature or technically redundant. Even where the formal reporting channel exists, a waived or unacknowledged programme can narrow the number of people who can assemble the full picture.

The law tries to counter this by requiring annual reports to the congressional defence committees on DoD SAPs. For each new SAP, the statute requires notice, justification, current total cost estimates and identification of existing programmes or technologies similar to the technology, programme or capability being proposed. That last requirement is especially relevant to duplication: [Congress]({{ 'congress/' | relative_url }}) is not merely asking “is this secret justified?” but also “what is it similar to, and why is a separate compartmented effort needed?”<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&amp;num=0&amp;req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title10-section119" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Similarity checks at creation.">[U.S. Code]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Similarity checks at creation.</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on house.gov.</span></span></span>

## The A-12 lesson: secrecy did not cause every failure, but it made scrutiny harder

The Navy’s A-12 Avenger II is the clearest public cautionary example because it was a special access aircraft programme that collapsed under cost, schedule and technical strain. It was meant to produce a carrier-based stealth attack aircraft, but was cancelled in 1991 after major overruns and delays. A DoD Inspector General review reported that the full-scale development contract was estimated to be about $1 billion over ceiling and at least one year behind schedule, and it recommended that the Secretary of Defense address oversight of special access programmes.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://media.defense.gov/1991/Feb/28/2001714477/-1/-1/1/91-059.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: media.defense.gov">[U.S. Department of War]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">media.defense.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">U.S. Department of War Review of the A-12 Aircraft Program</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">U.S. Department of War Review of the A-12 Aircraft Program</span></span></span>(https://media.defense.gov/1991/Feb/28/2001714477/-1/-1/1/91-059.pdf)

The point is not that classification alone killed the A-12. Large unclassified or normally classified weapons programmes also overrun, slip and disappoint. GAO later found no major overall difference between sampled SAP acquisition programmes and non-SAP programmes in cost, schedule and performance outcomes; both categories suffered cost growth, schedule slippage and performance shortfalls. The narrower lesson is that SAP status can remove or weaken some of the ordinary comparison points that help leaders see a programme’s real condition in time.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gao.gov/assets/nsiad-93-78.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.gov">[GAO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">NSIAD-93-78 Defense Acquisition: Oversight of Special Access Programs Has Increased</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GAONSIAD-93-78 Defense Acquisition: Oversight of Special Access Programs Has Increased</span></span></span>

Steven Aftergood of the Federation of American Scientists described the A-12 as a “secret programme run amok” in a study of secrecy and knowledge production, arguing that special access controls strictly limited who could know enough to evaluate the programme. Specialist accounts from the period likewise emphasised that the programme appeared publicly and politically healthier than it was until shortly before cancellation. Those accounts should not be read as proof that secrecy inevitably ruins programmes; they are better read as evidence that when secrecy combines with technical ambition, fixed expectations and narrow review, bad news can remain trapped too long.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ciaotest.cc.columbia.edu/wps/rej02/rej02b.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ciaotest.cc.columbia.edu">[CIAO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ciaotest.cc.columbia.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on columbia.edu.</span></span></span>

The A-12 case also shows why duplication and over-compartmentation are linked even when the visible problem is not two identical programmes. A compartmented acquisition effort can duplicate effort in a subtler way: recreating lessons, technical assumptions, contractor claims or risk assessments that another part of the defence establishment has already encountered. If those lessons cannot cross [compartments]({{ 'compartments/' | relative_url }}), each programme pays to rediscover them.

## When secrecy stops people comparing notes

The hardest oversight problem is that valid secrecy and harmful isolation can look similar from the outside. A small access list may be exactly what protects a source or capability. But the same small list can also prevent lawful reviewers from seeing that a programme is overlapping with another effort, relying on a doubtful assumption, or continuing after its original security rationale has expired.

The Moynihan Commission on government secrecy captured this wider problem in its discussion of SAPs. It concluded that many aspects of the SAP system still needed repair and warned that additional security costs did not always produce additional security benefits. It also called for greater standardisation of security practices and noted that DoD had created SAPOC in the 1990s to standardise approval, termination, revalidation and restructuring procedures, including annual review of previously identified DoD SAPs.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://info.publicintelligence.net/MoynihanReportGovernmentSecrecy.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: info.publicintelligence.net">[Public Intelligence &#124;]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">info.publicintelligence.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on publicintelligence.net.</span></span></span>

Standardisation is not bureaucracy for its own sake. It is how one part of government learns enough about another part to ask whether a compartment is still justified. Without common records, common definitions and repeatable review cycles, each SAP can become a bespoke world with its own access rules, vocabulary, budget trail and justification. That makes cross-programme comparison difficult even for cleared officials.

Modern DoD policy therefore puts weight on central offices and governance bodies. The 2024 DoD instruction requires component SAP central offices to submit governance actions through SAP working structures, support inspections, coordinate interagency memoranda, and participate in reviews. SAPOC and related senior review bodies are meant to provide a level at which separate compartments can be compared without exposing details to everyone.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/DD/issuances/dodi/520511p.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: whs.mil">[ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">520511p</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">520511p</span></span></span>

## Ways oversight can detect overlap

The best oversight of over-compartmentation does not mean opening every compartment widely. It means creating trusted comparison points where people with lawful access, technical competence and institutional independence can see across enough boundaries to detect waste and weak assumptions.

A useful SAP anti-duplication system has several features:



<img src="{{ "/assets/images/special-access-progr_a1c931_over_compartmentatio_83b738-Illustration-3-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="Duplication illustration 3" data-theme-src-dark="{{ "/assets/images/special-access-progr_a1c931_over_compartmentatio_83b738-Illustration-3-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{ "/assets/images/special-access-progr_a1c931_over_compartmentatio_83b738-Illustration-3-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="lazy" decoding="async" fetchpriority="low">

1. **A real inventory, not just a list of codewords.** Executive Order 13526 requires agencies to maintain an accounting system for SAPs, and DoD’s internal process requires annual review. For duplication control, that inventory must capture mission, capability, sponsor, cost, maturity and relationship to other programmes at a level that central reviewers can compare. [whitehouse.gov](https://obamawhitehouse.archives.gov/the-press-office/executive-order-classified-national-security-information)

2. **Similarity checks at creation.** The statutory requirement to identify existing programmes or technologies similar to a new SAP is a key guardrail. It forces a sponsor to explain why the proposed programme is not merely a secret duplicate of something already funded. [U.S. Code](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&num=0&req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title10-section119)

3. **Annual revalidation with authority to narrow, merge or end.** A SAP may be justified at birth but unnecessary later. Technology can mature, a capability can move into operational use, or a threat can change. DoD policy explicitly directs annual review of the continuing need for SAP protection and consideration of disestablishment where extra protection is no longer warranted. [ESD](https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/DD/issuances/dodi/520511p.pdf)

4. **Independent technical and audit access.** GAO’s older findings about weak controls, poor communication and withheld information show why central oversight cannot rely only on programme self-reporting. Reviewers need enough access to test cost, schedule, performance and technical claims against evidence. [GAO](https://www.gao.gov/assets/nsiad-93-78.pdf)

    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select && available.indexOf(marketId) >= 0) select.value = marketId;
    var selectedText = marketId;
    if (select && select.selectedIndex >= 0) selectedText = select.options[select.selectedIndex].text;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-flag], [data-ebay-trigger-market-flag]')).forEach(function (flag) {
      flag.className = flagClass(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-trigger-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-domain-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = domainLabel(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.setAttribute('aria-selected', button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option') === marketId ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;
      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
})();
