## Introduction

Special Access Program (SAP) security places an unusual burden on individual judgement. A person with SAP access is not expected to decide alone whether uncertain information is safe to discuss. Instead, uncertainty itself becomes a trigger for action. Publicly available SAP non-disclosure agreements and security guidance make clear that if an individual has reason to believe information might be SAP information, or might be related to or derived from it, the obligation is to consult authorised security management rather than make a personal judgement and proceed. This approach reflects the central purpose of SAP controls: preventing accidental disclosure where ordinary classification rules may not provide enough protection.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/DD/forms/dd/dd2836.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[WHS ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">WHS ESDspecial access program indoctrination agreementApril 16, 2007 — I hereby acknowledge that I have received a security indoctrinatio...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 16, 2007</span></span></span>

Within the broader framework of SAP non-disclosure agreements and personal duties, this mechanism is significant because it shifts responsibility away from guessing. The duty is not only to protect information already known to be covered by a SAP, but also to recognise uncertainty as a security condition that requires formal consultation.

## Information That May Be SAP-Derived

The publicly released [SAP Indoctrination]({{ 'sapia/' | relative_url }}) Agreement (DD Form 2836) deliberately uses broad language. It does not limit obligations to information explicitly marked as SAP information. Instead, it extends the duty to material that a person has reason to believe "might be" SAP information or is "related to or derived from" SAP information. That wording acknowledges that sensitive knowledge often exists outside a single classified document. It may appear in notes, draft papers, technical discussions, presentations, recollections, or combinations of otherwise ordinary facts.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/DD/forms/dd/dd2836.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[WHS ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">WHS ESDspecial access program indoctrination agreementApril 16, 2007 — I hereby acknowledge that I have received a security indoctrinatio...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 16, 2007</span></span></span>

This distinction matters because SAP personnel frequently acquire knowledge through experience rather than from a single labelled source. A design feature remembered from meetings, an operational method discussed during programme work, or an apparently harmless technical description may all reflect protected programme knowledge even if no classification marking is visible at the moment of disclosure.

Rather than expecting individuals to reconstruct classification boundaries from memory, the agreement directs them to seek guidance from the government department or agency that authorised their SAP access. The responsibility therefore begins before disclosure, not after a mistake has been made.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/DD/forms/dd/dd2836.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[WHS ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">WHS ESDspecial access program indoctrination agreementApril 16, 2007 — I hereby acknowledge that I have received a security indoctrinatio...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 16, 2007</span></span></span>

## Why Guessing Creates Disclosure Risk

SAPs exist because ordinary clearance levels alone are considered insufficient to protect exceptionally sensitive information. Someone may possess a high-level security clearance while lacking authorisation for a specific programme. Because of that, an individual's personal confidence that information is harmless cannot substitute for programme-specific review.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cdse.edu/Portals/124/Documents/student-guides/SA002-guide.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cdse.edu">[CDSE]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cdse.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Special Access Program Security Annual RefresherIn this course, you will review the types of. The principal risks created by guessing include:

* **Misidentifying programme-derived information.** Information may not be obviously labelled but may nevertheless reveal protected capabilities or activities when combined with other knowledge.
* **Assuming declassification without confirmation.** Public discussion, media reporting, or partial official acknowledgement does not automatically remove SAP protection from related information.
* **Believing familiarity equals authority.** Knowing colleagues possess similar clearances does not establish that they are authorised for the same SAP.
* **Accumulating small disclosures.** Individual facts that appear insignificant may collectively expose protected programme details, an issue closely related to [operations]({{ 'operations/' | relative_url }}) security (OPSEC).<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cdse.edu/Portals/124/Documents/student-guides/SA002-guide.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cdse.edu">[CDSE]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cdse.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Special Access Program Security Annual RefresherIn this course, you will review the types of. SAPs and SAP facilities, or SAPFs, and...</span></span></span>

The DD Form 2836 language is notable because it removes discretion at precisely this point. Instead of allowing the employee to resolve uncertainty privately, it requires consultation with the appropriate management authority whenever doubt exists about whether information falls within SAP protection.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/DD/forms/dd/dd2836.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[WHS ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">WHS ESDspecial access program indoctrination agreementApril 16, 2007 — I hereby acknowledge that I have received a security indoctrinatio...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 16, 2007</span></span></span>

## Who a Worker Should Consult

The SAP Indoctrination Agreement identifies the appropriate point of contact as the government department or agency that most recently authorised the person's SAP access. This requirement applies even after employment changes or departure from the programme, reflecting the continuing nature of SAP obligations. Former programme personnel are therefore expected to seek guidance rather than rely on memory when considering public discussion, publication, or other disclosure.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/DD/forms/dd/dd2836.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[WHS ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">WHS ESDspecial access program indoctrination agreementApril 16, 2007 — I hereby acknowledge that I have received a security indoctrinatio...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 16, 2007</span></span></span>

In practice, consultation commonly occurs through established programme security channels, such as:

* the responsible Special Security Officer (SSO), where applicable;
* the organisation's authorised security management chain; or
* the government SAP office responsible for the programme.

## How Consultation Changes Personal Responsibility

An important feature of the SAP framework is that consultation transfers the classification determination to officials with programme authority. The worker's responsibility is therefore procedural rather than interpretive.

Instead of asking, "Am I certain this is protected?", the practical question becomes, "Do I have reason to believe this might involve SAP information?" Once that threshold is reached, the obligation is to seek official guidance before disclosure. This reduces the chance that individual judgement, incomplete recollection, or changing programme status will lead to an unauthorised release.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/DD/forms/dd/dd2836.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[WHS ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">WHS ESDspecial access program indoctrination agreementApril 16, 2007 — I hereby acknowledge that I have received a security indoctrinatio...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 16, 2007</span></span></span>

The same principle appears in the agreement's pre-publication review requirement. If a person intends to release any writing or other material that contains, appears to contain, or is reasonably believed to derive from SAP information, it must be submitted for security review before public disclosure. Again, uncertainty does not authorise independent decision-making; it initiates a formal review process.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/DD/forms/dd/dd2836.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[WHS ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">WHS ESDspecial access program indoctrination agreementApril 16, 2007 — I hereby acknowledge that I have received a security indoctrinatio...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 16, 2007</span></span></span>

## The Practical Effect of Treating Doubt as a Duty

This approach reflects a broader philosophy within SAP security: uncertainty is managed through reporting and consultation rather than individual interpretation. The objective is not to punish uncertainty but to prevent it from becoming an accidental disclosure.

For workers operating within acknowledged, unacknowledged, or waived SAP environments, the practical rule is therefore straightforward. If information may originate from, relate to, or derive from SAP knowledge—and there is genuine uncertainty about its status—the expected response is not to guess, but to consult authorised security management before any disclosure occurs. That procedural obligation is itself a central personal duty created by SAP access.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/DD/forms/dd/dd2836.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[WHS ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">WHS ESDspecial access program indoctrination agreementApril 16, 2007 — I hereby acknowledge that I have received a security indoctrinatio...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 16, 2007</span></span></span>
