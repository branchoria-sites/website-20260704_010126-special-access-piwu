## Introduction

Portion markings are the mechanism that keeps the protection applied to Special Access Program (SAP) information attached to individual pieces of content rather than only to the document that contains them. While banner markings warn that an entire document contains SAP-controlled material, portion markings identify the classification level and SAP restrictions for each paragraph, table, chart, [title]({{ 'statute/' | relative_url }}), image, slide, or attachment. This matters because classified information is routinely reused in briefings, reports, emails, and extracts. Without portion markings, an isolated paragraph or graphic could lose the handling instructions that identify who is authorised to receive it. US classification policy therefore treats portion marking as a core safeguard for derivative classification and controlled reuse, not merely as an administrative formatting requirement.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cdse.edu/Portals/124/Documents/student-guides/IF105-guide.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cdse.edu">[cdse.edu]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cdse.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Marking Special Categories of Classified Information</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Marking Special Categories of Classified InformationJuly 11, 2025 — Banner markings provide information about the overall document, p...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 11, 2025</span></span></span>

## Banner markings versus portion markings

Banner markings and portion markings serve related but different purposes.

A banner marking communicates the highest overall classification and any special controls that apply to the document as a whole. For a SAP document, that typically combines the classification level with the "Special Access Required" (SAR) caveat and the authorised programme identifier.

Portion markings, by contrast, apply at the smallest practical unit of information. Department of Defense guidance requires individual portions—including titles, paragraphs, bullet points, tables, figures, photographs, signature blocks and other discrete elements—to carry markings reflecting the highest classification and control requirements within that specific portion. For SAP material, the portion mark combines the classification level with the SAR identifier and the authorised programme abbreviation or digraph.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.dodig.mil/Portals/48/Documents/Policy/520001_vol2.pdf?ver=2017-04-25-160926-223" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dodig.mil">[dodig.mil]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dodig.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">520001 vol2</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">DoD Manual 5200.01, Volume 2, February 24, 201224 Feb 2012 — SAP control markings used in the banner line and at portions denote classifi...</span></span></span>

The distinction becomes important whenever information is separated from its original document. A paragraph copied into another report, a chart inserted into a briefing, or a table forwarded in an email retains its own security markings rather than relying on the original document's banner to communicate handling requirements.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-32/subtitle-B/chapter-XX/part-2001" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ecfr.gov">[eCFR]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ecfr.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">part 2001</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Each portion shall be marked to reflect the highest level of information contained in that...</span></span></span>

## How excerpts become derivative products

Most classified documents are not written entirely from original sources. Instead, they are derivative products assembled from existing classified material under an approved security classification guide.

Portion markings support this process by allowing an author to determine precisely what protection each reused element requires. Rather than assuming that every extracted sentence inherits the highest classification found anywhere in the source document, the classifier can identify the marking attached to the specific paragraph or graphic being reused. This improves both security and accuracy by protecting genuinely sensitive information while avoiding unnecessary over-classification. Executive Order 13526 and its implementing regulations require uniform marking practices precisely to support this kind of controlled derivative classification.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-32/subtitle-B/chapter-XX/part-2001" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ecfr.gov">[ecfr.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ecfr.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">part 2001</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Each portion shall be marked to reflect the highest level of information contained in that...</span></span></span>

An important practical consequence follows: documents that lack required portion markings generally should not be used as source documents for derivative classification because the classification of individual elements cannot be reliably determined. The Information Security Oversight Office highlights this as one of the principal reasons portion marking is mandatory across classified documentation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/files/isoo/training/marking-booklet-revision.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">marking booklet revision</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesMarking Classified National Security Information Handbook4 Jan 2018 — - Executive Order (E.O.) 13526 32 CFR Part 2001, P...</span></span></span>

## Why every table, chart and title needs markings

A common misconception is that only narrative paragraphs require classification markings. In practice, guidance extends the requirement much further.

Examples of portions include:

* Document titles and subject lines.

<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Individual paragraphs and sub-paragraphs.
* Bullet lists.
* Tables and spreadsheets.
* Charts and graphs.
* Maps, photographs and illustrations.
* Figure captions.
* Slides in presentations.
* Attachments and annexes.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cdse.edu/Portals/124/Documents/student-guides/IF105-guide.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cdse.edu">[CDSE]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cdse.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Marking Special Categories of Classified Information</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Marking Special Categories of Classified InformationJuly 11, 2025 — Banner markings provide information about the overall document, p...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 11, 2025</span></span></span>

</div>

This broad definition recognises that sensitive information often resides in visual or structured data rather than prose. A chart showing aircraft performance, a photograph revealing sensor placement, or even a presentation title may independently disclose SAP-controlled information. Marking each portion prevents these elements from becoming detached from the access restrictions that apply to them.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cdse.edu/Portals/124/Documents/student-guides/IF105-guide.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cdse.edu">[CDSE]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cdse.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Marking Special Categories of Classified Information</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Marking Special Categories of Classified InformationJuly 11, 2025 — Banner markings provide information about the overall document, p...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 11, 2025</span></span></span>

## Protecting mixed-content SAP documents

SAP documents frequently contain information with different protection requirements.

For example, one paragraph may contain only Secret information, another may include SAP-controlled operational details, while an appendix could combine material from several authorised programmes. Portion markings make these distinctions explicit.

This allows:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* authorised readers to identify which passages require SAP access;
* reviewers to sanitise documents more accurately;
* editors to remove only the protected portions when preparing releasable versions;
* derivative authors to reuse only appropriately authorised material.

</div>

Without portion-level markings, reviewers would often have to treat every extracted element as though it carried the document's highest overall restrictions, reducing the usefulness of legitimate information sharing.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.dodig.mil/Portals/48/Documents/Policy/520001_vol2.pdf?ver=2017-04-25-160926-223" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dodig.mil">[dodig.mil]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dodig.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">520001 vol2</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">DoD Manual 5200.01, Volume 2, February 24, 201224 Feb 2012 — SAP control markings used in the banner line and at portions denote classifi...</span></span></span>

## Supporting electronic documents and collaborative systems

The same principles apply beyond printed reports.

Federal marking regulations require portion markings for classified emails, classified web pages, blogs, wikis and other collaborative environments. Subject lines, URLs, individual postings and modified content are each marked according to their own sensitivity rather than automatically inheriting the classification of surrounding material. When users edit collaborative documents, the markings must be updated to reflect the resulting content.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-32/subtitle-B/chapter-XX/part-2001" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ecfr.gov">[eCFR]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ecfr.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">part 2001</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Each portion shall be marked to reflect the highest level of information contained in that...</span></span></span>

Presentation slides receive similar treatment. Guidance specifies that titles, bullet points, captions, embedded graphs, charts, images and figures all require portion markings so that individual slides or graphics can be reused without losing their security context.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cdse.edu/Portals/124/Documents/student-guides/IF105-guide.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cdse.edu">[CDSE]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cdse.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Marking Special Categories of Classified Information</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Marking Special Categories of Classified InformationJuly 11, 2025 — Banner markings provide information about the overall document, p...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 11, 2025</span></span></span>

## What happens when SAP status changes

Portion markings also simplify later lifecycle management.

If information is formally removed from SAP protection while remaining classified, Department of Defense guidance directs that SAP identifiers in both the [banner lines]({{ 'banner-lines/' | relative_url }}) and the portion markings be redacted or struck through to reflect the loss of SAP status. The underlying classification level may remain unchanged, but the access restriction attached to each marked portion is updated consistently throughout the document.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/DD/issuances/dodm/520507-V4p.PDF" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[WHS ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">520507 V4p</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">WHS ESDDoDM 5205.07, Volume 4, &quot;Special Access Program (SAP)...September 9, 2020 — 10 Oct 2013 — (2) SAP markings on the document (in th...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 9, 2020</span></span></span>

## Why portion markings are central to SAP protection

Banner markings tell a reader that a document belongs within a Special Access Program, but portion markings ensure that protection survives when information is separated from the original document. They provide the security metadata that accompanies each reusable element, allowing excerpts to retain their classification level and programme restrictions wherever they are copied.

In practice, this makes portion markings one of the most important implementation tools in the SAP marking system. They enable accurate derivative classification, support selective sanitisation, preserve access controls during reuse, and reduce the risk that an isolated paragraph, table or graphic will lose the handling instructions that distinguish SAP information from ordinary classified material.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.dodig.mil/Portals/48/Documents/Policy/520001_vol2.pdf?ver=2017-04-25-160926-223" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dodig.mil">[dodig.mil]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dodig.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">520001 vol2</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">DoD Manual 5200.01, Volume 2, February 24, 201224 Feb 2012 — SAP control markings used in the banner line and at portions denote classifi...</span></span></span>
