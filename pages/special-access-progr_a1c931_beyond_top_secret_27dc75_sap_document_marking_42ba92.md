## Introduction

Special Access Program (SAP) markings change document handling by signalling that ordinary Secret or Top Secret procedures are not sufficient. A document carrying SAP control markings is still classified at its underlying level, but the markings tell every authorised reader that additional programme-specific rules apply. Those rules affect who may read the document, how it is distributed, where it may be stored, how individual paragraphs are marked, and even how copies are tracked. Rather than creating a new classification level, [SAP markings]({{ 'markings/' | relative_url }}) act as operational instructions embedded into the document itself, helping prevent accidental disclosure by making the programme's special handling requirements immediately visible.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cdse.edu/Portals/124/Documents/student-guides/shorts/SAS0030-guide.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cdse.edu">[CDSE]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cdse.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Special Access Programs: Markings Short Student GuideJuly 1, 2025 — Control markings in the banner lines, including SAP markings, mus...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 1, 2025</span></span></span>

## What SAP markings signal

The most visible change is the document's **banner line**—the classification marking that appears prominently at the top and bottom of each page. For SAP material, this banner contains more than simply "SECRET" or "TOP SECRET". Department of Defense guidance requires it to include:

* The overall classification level written in full (for example, **TOP SECRET**).
* The caveat **SPECIAL ACCESS REQUIRED**, or the authorised abbreviation **SAR**.
* The programme nickname or codeword.
* Any applicable dissemination controls, such as **WAIVED**, **NOFORN**, or **REL TO**, placed at the end of the marking sequence where required.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/DD/issuances/dodm/520507-V4p.PDF" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[WHS ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Banner Line. Portion Markings. SECRET//SPECIAL ACCESS REQUIRED-TAXI.Read more</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">WHS ESDDoDM 5205.07, Volume 4, &quot;Special Access Program (SAP)...10 Oct 2013 — Examples of SAP Banner Line, Portion, and Dissemination Con...</span></span></span>

A typical banner therefore communicates several instructions simultaneously. It tells a cleared reader not only the classification level but also that the material belongs to a specific compartment requiring separate authorisation.

## How markings prevent routine mishandling

SAP markings are designed to interrupt normal document workflows. Someone accustomed to handling ordinary Top Secret information should immediately recognise that additional procedures apply before the document is copied, discussed, transmitted or stored.

The markings support this in several ways:

* **Immediate visual warning.** Large, conspicuous [banner lines]({{ 'banner-lines/' | relative_url }}) at the top and bottom of every page alert anyone handling the document that special programme rules govern the information.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cdse.edu/Portals/124/Documents/student-guides/shorts/SAS0030-guide.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cdse.edu">[CDSE]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cdse.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Special Access Programs: Markings Short Student GuideJuly 1, 2025 — Control markings in the banner lines, including SAP markings, mus...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 1, 2025</span></span></span>
* **Programme-specific access control.** Naming the programme reminds document custodians that possession of the appropriate security clearance alone does not authorise access. Personnel must also be approved for that specific SAP.
* **Correct distribution.** Dissemination controls incorporated into the banner indicate whether sharing is prohibited, restricted to certain governments, or subject to additional programme limitations.
* **Reduced administrative error.** Standardised marking syntax gives security personnel, records managers and automated systems a consistent way to identify SAP material and apply the correct handling procedures.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cdse.edu/Portals/124/Documents/student-guides/shorts/IFS0048-guide.pdf?ver=EkWluJvX44GlBI9P6fg8Mw%3D%3D" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cdse.edu">[CDSE]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cdse.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Marking Syntax Short Student GuideApril 21, 2025 — There are four control marking categories: • Sensitive Compartmented Information (...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 21, 2025</span></span></span>

Without these additional markings, a document could be mistaken for ordinary classified material and processed under less restrictive procedures than the programme requires.

## Banner lines, nicknames and control language

SAP documents use specialised control language that communicates handling requirements without revealing operational details.

The **programme nickname or codeword** identifies the relevant access compartment. Department of Defense guidance specifies that the full nickname or codeword appears in the banner line rather than an abbreviated programme identifier. By contrast, abbreviated programme identifiers are used in paragraph-level markings to reduce clutter while still identifying the controlling programme.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/DD/issuances/dodm/520507-V4p.PDF" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[WHS ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Banner Line. Portion Markings. SECRET//SPECIAL ACCESS REQUIRED-TAXI.Read more</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">WHS ESDDoDM 5205.07, Volume 4, &quot;Special Access Program (SAP)...10 Oct 2013 — Examples of SAP Banner Line, Portion, and Dissemination Con...</span></span></span>

When information from [multiple SAPs]({{ 'multiple-saps/' | relative_url }}) appears in a single document, the markings also change. For documents involving three or more programmes, the banner uses **MULTIPLE PROGRAMS** instead of listing every nickname, while the detailed source information is maintained elsewhere in the document's classification instructions. This avoids excessively long banner lines without weakening handling controls.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/DD/issuances/dodm/520507-V4p.PDF" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[WHS ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Banner Line. Some SAPs also require additional dissemination language. For example, Department of Defense policy requires the marking **WAIVED** on documents belonging to waived Special Access Programs, where it functions as a dissemination control marking in addition to the SAP designation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/DD/issuances/dodm/520507-V4p.PDF" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[WHS ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Banner Line. ## Why portion markings matter

SAP handling requirements extend beyond the banner line. Individual paragraphs, tables and other classified portions carry **[portion markings]({{ 'portion-markings/' | relative_url }})** indicating both their classification and SAP status.

Instead of showing only a classification abbreviation such as "(TS)", SAP portions include the SAR caveat together with the assigned programme identifier. This allows editors to combine material from different sources while preserving the correct handling requirements for each section. A document may therefore contain multiple SAP-controlled portions that require separate treatment during extraction, quotation or derivative classification.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/DD/issuances/dodm/520507-V4p.PDF" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[WHS ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Banner Line. Portion Markings. SECRET//SPECIAL ACCESS REQUIRED-TAXI.Read more</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">WHS ESDDoDM 5205.07, Volume 4, &quot;Special Access Program (SAP)...10 Oct 2013 — Examples of SAP Banner Line, Portion, and Dissemination Con...</span></span></span>

This paragraph-level marking is particularly important during derivative classification because individual passages may later appear in new documents. The markings help ensure that SAP restrictions follow the information rather than remaining attached only to the original document.

## How the markings affect everyday document control

Once a document carries SAP markings, ordinary classified document procedures are supplemented by programme-specific controls.

Depending on the programme and governing policy, this commonly includes:

* restricting storage to approved SAP facilities or information systems;
* limiting reproduction and distribution to authorised programme personnel;
* maintaining stricter [accountability]({{ 'accountability/' | relative_url }}) records for highly classified SAP copies;
* ensuring derivative documents preserve the appropriate SAP markings; and

## The practical purpose of extra markings

The added markings do not make information "more than Top Secret" in terms of classification level. Instead, they provide a visible, standardised reminder that the document exists within a restricted programme environment where access depends on programme approval as well as clearance.

In practice, the banner lines, programme nicknames, portion markings and dissemination controls work together as operational safeguards. They help prevent routine administrative handling from becoming a security vulnerability by ensuring that anyone authorised to encounter the document immediately understands that ordinary collateral classification rules are not sufficient.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cdse.edu/Portals/124/Documents/student-guides/shorts/SAS0030-guide.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cdse.edu">[cdse.edu]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cdse.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Special Access Programs: Markings Short Student GuideJuly 1, 2025 — Control markings in the banner lines, including SAP markings, mus...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 1, 2025</span></span></span>
