## Introduction

The annual review of a Department of Defense (DoD) Special Access Program (SAP) is intended to answer a practical question rather than simply satisfy an administrative requirement: **does this programme still require extraordinary security controls, or can those protections be reduced, shared more widely, or removed altogether?** Under modern DoD policy, SAP status is not supposed to continue by inertia. Each year, programme owners and senior oversight bodies must reassess whether the additional compartmentation remains justified in light of operational needs, classification principles, and the maturity of the capability. This review complements the annual reporting framework under 10 U.S.C. §119 by ensuring that secrecy itself is periodically revalidated rather than assumed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/DD/issuances/dodi/520511p.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[WHS ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">WHS ESDDoD Instruction 5205.11, &quot;Management, Administration...September 11, 2024 — 12 Sept 2024 — Conduct an annual review of DoD SAPs...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 11, 2024</span></span></span>

## Revalidation as a recurring checkpoint

The annual review functions as a formal revalidation process. Instead of asking only whether a programme is succeeding technically or remaining within budget, reviewers examine whether the **special-access controls themselves** continue to provide a necessary security benefit.

Earlier DoD security regulations made this principle unusually explicit. The historical SAP regulation described the annual reports as the mechanism for "revalidation and approval for continuation" of every DoD SAP, stating that any programme not approved to continue as a SAP was to be terminated in that status. Programme managers were required to justify not only continuation of the programme but continuation of its SAP designation, any carve-out industrial security arrangements, and, where applicable, continued waived status.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://irp.fas.org/doddir/dod/5200-1r/chapter_8.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: irp.fas.org">[Intelligence Resource Program]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">irp.fas.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Intelligence Resource ProgramChapter 8 SPECIAL ACCESS PROGRAMSThese annual reports also serve as the vehicle for revalidation and approva...</span></span></span>

Although DoD policy has been rewritten since then, the underlying concept remains. The 2024 DoD Instruction 5205.11 requires annual reviews of every DoD SAP regarding the **continuation of SAP security protection**, with recommendations moving through the Senior Review Group (SRG) to the Special Access Program Oversight Committee (SAPOC) for approval.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/DD/issuances/dodi/520511p.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[WHS ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">WHS ESDDoD Instruction 5205.11, &quot;Management, Administration...September 11, 2024 — 12 Sept 2024 — Conduct an annual review of DoD SAPs...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 11, 2024</span></span></span>

This means the recurring review is not merely a compliance exercise attached to the congressional reporting calendar. It is a governance mechanism designed to challenge whether exceptional secrecy still has a defensible purpose.

## What modern SAPOC reviews assess

Current policy broadens the annual review beyond the simple question of whether information remains classified. The SAPOC examines several related issues that together determine whether SAP controls remain proportionate.

These assessments include:

* whether the protected capability is ready to be integrated into wider operational planning through the Integrated Joint Special Technical [Operations]({{ 'operations/' | relative_url }}) (IJSTO) process;

<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* whether information should be shared with other federal agencies or trusted foreign partners;
* whether additional SAP protection methods are still warranted or whether the programme could be disestablished as a SAP;
* whether programmes designated as waived SAPs continue to require that exceptional reporting status.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/DD/issuances/dodi/520511p.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[WHS ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">WHS ESDDoD Instruction 5205.11, &quot;Management, Administration...September 11, 2024 — 12 Sept 2024 — Conduct an annual review of DoD SAPs...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 11, 2024</span></span></span>

</div>

This reflects an important distinction. The review is not limited to asking whether information is still classified under Executive Order 13526. Information may remain classified while no longer requiring the additional access restrictions that distinguish a SAP from ordinary classified programmes. Conversely, some particularly sensitive capabilities may continue to justify compartmented protection even after years of operation if broader exposure would create unacceptable [intelligence]({{ 'intelligence/' | relative_url }}), operational or technological risks.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/DD/issuances/dodi/520511p.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[WHS ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">WHS ESDDoD Instruction 5205.11, &quot;Management, Administration...September 11, 2024 — 12 Sept 2024 — Conduct an annual review of DoD SAPs...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 11, 2024</span></span></span>

## Why a programme may no longer need SAP controls

Several developments can reduce the need for special-access protection even when the underlying project continues.

A capability may have matured from experimental development into routine military use, making wider operational access more valuable than strict compartmentation. Operational commanders may need broader planning access, logistics personnel may require additional technical information, or allied governments may need participation in combined operations. In these situations, maintaining extremely narrow access can become an obstacle to effective employment rather than a security advantage. Current policy therefore specifically instructs reviewers to consider broader sharing and operational integration during annual assessments.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/DD/issuances/dodi/520511p.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[WHS ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">WHS ESDDoD Instruction 5205.11, &quot;Management, Administration...September 11, 2024 — 12 Sept 2024 — Conduct an annual review of DoD SAPs...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 11, 2024</span></span></span>

Similarly, advances in adversary knowledge or public disclosure may alter the balance between secrecy and utility. Although classification decisions remain governed by national security rules, the added protections of SAP status may no longer provide meaningful additional protection if equivalent information has already been incorporated into broader classified planning.

The annual review therefore asks whether SAP controls still provide security value commensurate with their administrative, operational and oversight costs.

## Why compartmented programmes can become sticky

One reason formal annual reviews matter is that compartmented programmes naturally resist change.

SAPs develop specialised security infrastructures, dedicated facilities, unique access approval systems and carefully controlled information channels. Over time, programme personnel often become accustomed to operating inside these [compartments]({{ 'compartments/' | relative_url }}), and expanding access may appear to increase security risk even when operational circumstances have changed.

Institutional factors also contribute. Programme managers understandably wish to avoid unnecessary disclosure of sensitive technology, while organisations that have invested heavily in SAP-specific security procedures may be reluctant to dismantle established arrangements.

Without periodic senior-level review, these organisational incentives can allow SAP status to persist simply because it already exists.

Modern policy attempts to counter that tendency by requiring structured annual consideration of whether compartmentation remains justified, whether broader sharing would better support defence missions, and whether the programme should eventually leave SAP protection altogether.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/DD/issuances/dodi/520511p.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[WHS ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">WHS ESDDoD Instruction 5205.11, &quot;Management, Administration...September 11, 2024 — 12 Sept 2024 — Conduct an annual review of DoD SAPs...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 11, 2024</span></span></span>

## Annual review is about proportional secrecy, not automatic declassification

A common misunderstanding is that an [annual SAP review]({{ 'annual-review-d72a1d/' | relative_url }}) determines whether a programme should become public. That is not its purpose.

Instead, reviewers distinguish between several possibilities:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* retaining SAP protection because exceptional controls remain necessary;
* modifying the scope of SAP protection while keeping the underlying information classified;
* increasing authorised sharing with military commands, other government agencies or approved foreign partners;
* ending SAP status because ordinary classification controls are now sufficient.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/DD/issuances/dodi/520511p.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[WHS ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">WHS ESDDoD Instruction 5205.11, &quot;Management, Administration...September 11, 2024 — 12 Sept 2024 — Conduct an annual review of DoD SAPs...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 11, 2024</span></span></span>

</div>

Public acknowledgement, declassification and removal from SAP status are separate decisions governed by different legal and policy authorities. A programme may cease to require SAP compartmentation yet remain highly classified, while another may continue as a SAP despite limited public awareness of some of its broader mission.

## The annual review keeps exceptional secrecy tied to current need

Taken together, the annual review process reinforces a core principle of DoD SAP governance: **special-access protection should exist because current conditions require it, not because it existed last year**.

By requiring recurring justification, examining opportunities for operational sharing, reviewing the continued need for waived status, and considering whether programmes can leave the SAP system entirely, the [SAPOC review]({{ 'sapoc-review/' | relative_url }}) provides a structured check against permanent compartmentation by default. It ensures that extraordinary secrecy remains linked to demonstrable security and operational requirements rather than institutional habit.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/DD/issuances/dodi/520511p.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[whs.mil]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">WHS ESDDoD Instruction 5205.11, &quot;Management, Administration...September 11, 2024 — 12 Sept 2024 — Conduct an annual review of DoD SAPs...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 11, 2024</span></span></span>
