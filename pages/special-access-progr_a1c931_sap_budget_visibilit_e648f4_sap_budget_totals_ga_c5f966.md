## Introduction

A published total for special access programme (SAP) spending can create the impression that the public knows how much is being spent, even when it reveals very little about what the money buys, where it is going, or whether it represents good value. This is the central limitation of aggregate SAP budget figures. They can satisfy formal reporting requirements while leaving the underlying spending patterns hidden behind classified programme structures, omitted public budget exhibits, or broad [accounting]({{ 'accounting/' | relative_url }}) categories.

The result is not necessarily that money disappears from oversight. Rather, oversight is divided. Selected members of [Congress]({{ 'congress/' | relative_url }}) and cleared officials may receive classified programme-level information, while the public—and often many other policymakers—see only aggregate totals or heavily redacted budget documents. That separation makes it difficult for outside observers to judge affordability, compare priorities, identify [duplication]({{ 'duplication/' | relative_url }}), or understand why spending changes from year to year.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&amp;num=0&amp;req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title10-section119" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uscode.house.gov">[U.S. Code]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uscode.house.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Code10 USC 119: Special access programs: congressional...Special access programs: congressional oversight. ## Aggregate totals versus programme-level meaning

A single budget number answers only one question: how much money has been allocated in total. It does not answer the questions that usually matter most in public debate:

* Which capabilities account for most of the increase?
* Is the funding supporting research, procurement, [operations]({{ 'operations/' | relative_url }}), or long-term sustainment?

<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--comparison" markdown="1">

* Are several organisations funding similar work independently?
* Have programme costs grown because of technical problems, schedule delays, or deliberate expansion?

</div>

These distinctions matter because defence [budgets]({{ 'budgets/' | relative_url }}) are judged not only by their size but also by how efficiently resources are distributed. When programme identities remain classified, the public cannot distinguish between an increase caused by a new satellite system, an advanced aircraft programme, cyber capabilities, [intelligence]({{ 'intelligence/' | relative_url }}) infrastructure, or higher operating costs.

Congressional reporting requirements under U.S. law recognise this distinction. The Department of Defense is required to provide classified programme-level information—including estimated total costs, annual funding and milestones—to the congressional defence committees, while allowing especially sensitive details to remain outside public budget documents where disclosure would damage national security.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&amp;num=0&amp;req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title10-section119" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uscode.house.gov">[U.S. Code]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uscode.house.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Code10 USC 119: Special access programs: congressional...Special access programs: congressional oversight. ## What public budget exhibits leave out

The difference between classified accounting and public visibility is illustrated by the way some defence budget displays are assembled.

A useful example comes from the Government Accountability Office's review of the Pacific Deterrence Initiative (PDI). GAO found that some funded activities identified as special access programmes were deliberately excluded from the publicly presented PDI budget exhibit because Department of Defense guidance treated SAPs differently. One cited example was approximately $1.3 billion in fiscal year 2025 research and development funding for a next-generation national defence space architecture SAP. The funding existed inside defence planning and budgeting, yet readers relying only on the published PDI exhibit would not see it reflected there.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://files.gao.gov/reports/GAO-26-107698/index.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: files.gao.gov">[files.gao.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">files.gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">PDI Reporting Requirements. That example demonstrates an important mechanism rather than an isolated anomaly. A public budget display may accurately report the programmes selected for inclusion while still omitting other funded activities that influence the government's overall investment in a mission area.

Consequently, two official budget documents discussing the same strategic objective can produce different spending pictures because they serve different reporting purposes.

## Why totals alone cannot test affordability

Affordability depends on more than the total amount appropriated.

Analysts normally examine whether:

* individual programmes remain within original cost estimates;
* technical risks are increasing programme costs;
* multiple organisations are funding overlapping capabilities;
* spending growth reflects inflation, changing requirements or management problems; and
* future operating and sustainment costs are becoming disproportionate to [acquisition]({{ 'acquisition/' | relative_url }}) costs.

Aggregate SAP totals reveal almost none of these factors.

For example, a stable overall SAP budget could conceal major cost growth in one programme offset by reductions elsewhere. Conversely, a significant increase in total classified spending might result from several unrelated initiatives rather than expansion of a single high-profile capability. Without programme-level context, outsiders cannot determine which explanation is correct.

This limitation affects public accountability rather than necessarily congressional oversight, because cleared committees may receive the classified information needed to make those judgements while the broader public cannot independently verify them.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&amp;num=0&amp;req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title10-section119" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uscode.house.gov">[U.S. Code]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uscode.house.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Code10 USC 119: Special access programs: congressional...Special access programs: congressional oversight. ## Hidden composition can obscure strategic priorities

Another consequence of aggregate reporting is that it hides the composition of spending.

A total classified budget cannot show whether investment is shifting towards:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* space systems instead of aircraft;
* research rather than procurement;
* intelligence collection rather than operational capability;
* regional priorities such as the Indo-Pacific instead of Europe; or
* modernisation rather than sustaining existing systems.

</div>

These choices are often central to defence strategy debates. When only aggregate figures are public, observers can detect that classified spending has changed but cannot reliably explain why.

The GAO's examination of the Pacific Deterrence Initiative illustrates this broader issue. It found inconsistencies between the public budget exhibit and the Indo-Pacific Command's independent assessment, along with varying interpretations across military services about which programmes should appear in the published display. ## Why this distinction matters

The key point is that a published SAP budget total is not the same as meaningful budget transparency.

A classified oversight system may successfully account for every dollar within authorised government channels while still preventing the public from understanding the practical drivers of spending. Aggregate figures confirm that resources exist, but they do not identify the capabilities consuming those resources, explain changes over time, or allow independent assessment of efficiency and affordability.

For that reason, debates over so-called "black budgets" are often less about whether totals exist than about whether totals alone provide enough information to understand what defence resources are actually purchasing.

    try { tz = String(Intl.DateTimeFormat().resolvedOptions().timeZone || ''); } catch (err) {}
    if (!tz) return '';
    for (var i = 0; i< timezoneRules.length; i += 1) {
      var rule = timezoneRules[i] || {};
      try {
        if (new RegExp(rule.pattern).test(tz)) return rule.market;
      } catch (err) {}
    }
    return '';
  }
  function inferMarket(available, options) {
    available = Array.isArray(available) ? available : [];
    options = options || {};
    var storageKey = options.storageKey || 'phoenix-affiliate-market';
    try {
      var saved = window.localStorage && localStorage.getItem(storageKey);
      if (saved && available.indexOf(saved) >= 0) return saved;
    } catch (err) {}
    var timezoneMarket = inferFromTimezone();
    if (timezoneMarket && available.indexOf(timezoneMarket) >= 0) return timezoneMarket;
    var localeMarkets = localeMatches();
    if (localeMarkets.exact && available.indexOf(localeMarkets.exact) >= 0) return localeMarkets.exact;
    if (localeMarkets.language && available.indexOf(localeMarkets.language) >= 0) return localeMarkets.language;
    if (options.defaultMarket && available.indexOf(options.defaultMarket) >= 0) return options.defaultMarket;
    return available[0] || '';
  }
  window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation = {
    normalize: normalize,
    inferMarket: inferMarket,
    inferFromLocale: inferFromLocale,
    inferFromTimezone: inferFromTimezone
  };
})();
