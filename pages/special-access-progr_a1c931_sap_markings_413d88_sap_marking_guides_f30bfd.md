## Introduction

SAP (Special Access Program) markings are not chosen by individual authors or security managers on personal judgement. They are determined by programme-specific security classification guides that translate classification decisions into the exact [banner lines]({{ 'banner-lines/' | relative_url }}), [portion markings]({{ 'portion-markings/' | relative_url }}), abbreviations and handling instructions required for that programme. In practice, the guide is the authoritative bridge between a programme's security policy and the markings that appear on documents, presentations, electronic media and derivative products. This matters because SAP controls add programme-specific access restrictions on top of the normal national security classification system. Without consistent guidance, different authors could apply inconsistent markings, increasing the risk of over-protection, under-protection or improper dissemination.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/DD/issuances/dodm/520507-V4p.PDF" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[WHS ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">WHS ESDDoDM 5205.07, Volume 4, &quot;Special Access Program (SAP)...September 9, 2020 — 10 Oct 2013 — (1) Provides guidance and procedures fo...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 9, 2020</span></span></span>

## Who decides the right SAP marking?

The immediate answer is that the programme's approved security classification guide decides. Original classification authorities establish the classification decisions that are then recorded in a formal guide. Authors who create new documents from existing classified sources act as derivative classifiers: they apply the guide rather than making new classification judgements themselves. Executive Order 13526 requires derivative classification to follow authorised classification guidance rather than individual discretion, and DoD policy extends that principle to SAP information through programme-specific marking requirements.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/DD/issuances/dodm/520507-V4p.PDF" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[whs.mil]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">WHS ESDDoDM 5205.07, Volume 4, &quot;Special Access Program (SAP)...September 9, 2020 — 10 Oct 2013 — (1) Provides guidance and procedures fo...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 9, 2020</span></span></span>

This means that a document author generally does not ask, "What marking seems appropriate?" Instead, the author asks, "What does the programme guide require for this information?" The resulting banner line, portion markings and associated controls are intended to be reproducible by anyone using the same authorised guidance.

## What the programme guide supplies

A SAP security classification guide does considerably more than identify whether information is Secret or Top Secret. It provides detailed instructions that allow different authorised personnel to mark information consistently throughout the programme.

Typical guidance includes:

* Which subjects, technologies, [operations]({{ 'operations/' | relative_url }}) or capabilities require SAP protection.
* The appropriate national security classification level for each category of information.
* The authorised SAP nickname, abbreviation or codeword used in banner and portion markings.
* Required document banner formats and any mandatory warning notices.
* Rules for marking attachments, graphics, presentations, electronic files and removable media.
* Declassification or review instructions where applicable.
* Special handling instructions for unique programme circumstances, such as multiple-programme material or programme transitions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/DD/issuances/dodm/520507-V4p.PDF" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[whs.mil]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">WHS ESDDoDM 5205.07, Volume 4, &quot;Special Access Program (SAP)...September 9, 2020 — 10 Oct 2013 — (1) Provides guidance and procedures fo...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 9, 2020</span></span></span>

Because these instructions are programme-specific, two SAPs protecting similar technical subjects may legitimately require different document markings if their governing classification guides differ.

## How derivative classifiers rely on guidance

Derivative classification is designed to prevent each document author from independently determining national security classifications. Instead, the derivative classifier extracts authorised decisions from existing classified sources or approved classification guides and applies them to newly created material.

For SAP information, this reliance becomes even more important because programme access controls are layered onto the normal classification system. A derivative classifier typically uses the guide to determine:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* whether information belongs inside a particular SAP;
* the correct classification level;
* the correct SAP identifier or authorised abbreviation;
* the proper portion markings for individual paragraphs or graphics;
* the overall document banner;<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.cdse.edu/Portals/124/Documents/student-guides/IF105-guide.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cdse.edu">[cdse.edu]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cdse.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Marking Special Categories of Classified InformationBanner markings provide information about the overall document, portion markings prov...</span></span></span>
* any additional dissemination controls required alongside the SAP marking.

</div>

Training materials used throughout the Department of Defense emphasise that derivative classifiers must use approved sources of classification guidance and accurately reproduce the prescribed markings. This approach promotes uniform handling across government offices, contractors and authorised information systems.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.lejeune.marines.mil/Portals/33/Documents/Adjutant/Security%20Manager/SharePortal%20Documents/Derivative%20Classification%20Job%20Aid.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: lejeune.marines.mil">[marines.mil]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">lejeune.marines.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">This job aid...Read more...</span></span></span>

## Why programme-specific rules matter

Programme-specific guidance exists because SAPs protect exceptionally sensitive information whose security requirements cannot always be expressed through ordinary classification levels alone.

For example, two documents may both contain Top Secret information, yet only one belongs within a particular SAP. The classification guide specifies that additional programme identifier because access depends not merely on clearance level but also on formal indoctrination into the relevant programme.

Programme-specific guidance also helps address practical situations such as:

* documents combining information from [multiple SAPs]({{ 'multiple-saps/' | relative_url }});

<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--decision" markdown="1">

* derivative products that summarise several classified sources;
* engineering drawings or software generated from SAP data;
* briefing slides, databases and digital storage media;
* successor programmes or disestablished programmes whose markings require controlled transition.

</div>

DoD marking policy specifically assigns responsibility to cognisant authorities and programme oversight organisations to develop and issue marking guidance for the SAP material they create, receive and protect, ensuring that programme rules are reflected consistently in documentation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/DD/issuances/dodm/520507-V4p.PDF" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[WHS ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">WHS ESDDoDM 5205.07, Volume 4, &quot;Special Access Program (SAP)...September 9, 2020 — 10 Oct 2013 — (1) Provides guidance and procedures fo...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 9, 2020</span></span></span>

## Consistency supports both security and information sharing

Accurate classification guides are intended to reduce two opposite risks.

The first is under-marking. If a required SAP identifier or handling instruction is omitted, authorised personnel elsewhere in the system may mistakenly treat the material as ordinary classified information, increasing the possibility of unauthorised access.

The second is unnecessary over-marking. Applying SAP controls where the guide does not require them can impede legitimate information sharing, complicate records management and increase administrative burden. Executive branch information security policy emphasises accurate and uniform marking because consistent markings enable both effective protection and appropriate dissemination among authorised recipients.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://fam.state.gov/fam/05fam/05fam0480.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: fam.state.gov">[Family Assistance Module]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">fam.state.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ily Assistance Module5 FAM 480 CLASSIFYING AND DECLASSIFYING NATIONAL...EO 13526 establishes a uniform system for classifying, markin...</span></span></span>

Classification guides therefore function as governance tools as much as marking references: they standardise security decisions across organisations while limiting unnecessary variation between individual classifiers.

## When guidance changes

Classification guides are living governance documents rather than permanent templates. As programmes evolve, technologies mature or operational sensitivities change, authorised officials may revise the guide to alter classification levels, marking formats or handling requirements.

When that occurs, new derivative products are expected to follow the updated guidance rather than relying indefinitely on older marking practices.
