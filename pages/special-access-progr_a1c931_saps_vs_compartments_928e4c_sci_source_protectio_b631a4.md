## Introduction

Sensitive Compartmented Information (SCI) control systems exist for a specific purpose: protecting classified national [intelligence]({{ 'intelligence/' | relative_url }}) whose value or sensitivity depends not only on its classification level but on how it was collected, analysed or derived. Within the broader discussion of Special Access Programs (SAPs) versus intelligence [compartments]({{ 'compartments/' | relative_url }}), SCI is not a programme management tool. Instead, it is a formal intelligence access-control framework established under the authority of the Director of National Intelligence (DNI) to protect intelligence sources, methods and analytical processes. Executive Order 13526 provides the overall classification framework, while Intelligence Community Directive (ICD) 703 defines SCI as classified national intelligence concerning or derived from intelligence sources, methods or analytical processes that must be protected within formal access-control systems established by the DNI.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.odni.gov/files/documents/ICD/ICD-703.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: odni.gov">[ODNI]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">odni.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">25, 2023 — 21 Jun 2013 — The Director of National Intelligence (DNI) is responsible for the protection of intelligence sources and method...</span></span></span>



<img src="{{ "/assets/images/special-access-progr_a1c931_saps_vs_compartments_928e4c_sci_source_protectio_b631a4-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="SCI Controls illustration 1" data-theme-src-dark="{{ "/assets/images/special-access-progr_a1c931_saps_vs_compartments_928e4c_sci_source_protectio_b631a4-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{ "/assets/images/special-access-progr_a1c931_saps_vs_compartments_928e4c_sci_source_protectio_b631a4-Illustration-1-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">

Understanding this distinction explains why documents marked with SCI control systems often relate to intelligence collection or intelligence analysis rather than to a particular [acquisition]({{ 'acquisition/' | relative_url }}) programme or military capability. The control system protects the intelligence channel itself—the means by which information was obtained or interpreted—not merely the information's sensitivity.

## Why SCI Compartments Exist

The Intelligence Community faces a security problem different from many other parts of government. Intelligence often becomes valuable precisely because adversaries do not know:

* how information was collected;
* which individuals or technical systems supplied it;
* what analytical techniques revealed its significance; or
* what collection capabilities remain available for future [operations]({{ 'operations/' | relative_url }}).

If any of those elements become known, an adversary may change behaviour, expose human sources, defeat surveillance systems or develop countermeasures that permanently reduce intelligence capability. Consequently, SCI control systems are designed around protecting the collection process as much as protecting the finished intelligence product. ICD 703 expressly links SCI protection to safeguarding intelligence sources, methods, analytical processes, personnel and mission capabilities.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.odni.gov/files/documents/ICD/ICD-703.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: odni.gov">[ODNI]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">odni.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">25, 2023 — 21 Jun 2013 — The Director of National Intelligence (DNI) is responsible for the protection of intelligence sources and method...</span></span></span>

Unlike ordinary classified information, SCI therefore introduces an additional access barrier beyond security clearance. A Top Secret clearance alone does not authorise access to every SCI control system.

## DNI Authority Over SCI Access Systems

The legal and policy authority for SCI differs from the authority used to establish most SAPs.

The National Security Act assigns the Director of National Intelligence responsibility for protecting intelligence sources and methods from unauthorised disclosure. Building on that statutory responsibility, Executive Order 13526 authorises the DNI to issue implementing directives governing classified intelligence information, while ICD 703 establishes uniform policy for protecting classified national intelligence, including SCI.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.odni.gov/files/documents/ICD/ICD-703.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: odni.gov">[ODNI]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">odni.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">25, 2023 — 21 Jun 2013 — The Director of National Intelligence (DNI) is responsible for the protection of intelligence sources and method...</span></span></span>

Under this framework, the DNI is responsible for:

* establishing formal SCI access-control systems;
* setting standards for granting SCI access;
* issuing protection requirements across the Intelligence Community;
* ensuring consistent handling, marking and safeguarding practices; and
* overseeing protection through [inspections]({{ 'inspections/' | relative_url }}), reviews and security policies.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.odni.gov/files/documents/ICD/ICD-703.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: odni.gov">[ODNI]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">odni.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">25, 2023 — 21 Jun 2013 — The Director of National Intelligence (DNI) is responsible for the protection of intelligence sources and method...</span></span></span>

Individual departments may administer SCI within their organisations, but they do so under this common Intelligence Community framework rather than creating independent compartment systems.

## What "Sources, Methods and Analytical Processes" Actually Means

The phrase "sources and methods" appears frequently in intelligence policy but is often misunderstood. SCI compartments are intended to protect three related categories.

### Intelligence sources

A source is where intelligence originates. Depending on the discipline, this may include:

* confidential human sources;
* intercepted communications;
* satellite imagery;
* specialised technical sensors;
* foreign liaison reporting; or
* other classified collection capabilities.

Disclosure may reveal who is cooperating with intelligence agencies or expose technical systems whose effectiveness depends upon remaining undisclosed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.odni.gov/files/documents/ICD/ICD-703.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: odni.gov">[ODNI]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">odni.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">25, 2023 — 21 Jun 2013 — The Director of National Intelligence (DNI) is responsible for the protection of intelligence sources and method...</span></span></span>

### Intelligence methods

Methods describe how intelligence is obtained, processed or exploited.

Examples include:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--phrase-pills" markdown="1">

* communications interception techniques;
* imagery processing methods;
* technical collection platforms;
* sensor capabilities;
* targeting procedures; and
* specialised exploitation technologies.

</div>

Even when the underlying intelligence appears routine, revealing collection methodology may enable an adversary to evade future collection.

### Analytical processes

ICD 703 also includes analytical processes, an important but sometimes overlooked category.

Analysis itself can reveal sensitive information. For example:(#endnote-4 "Endnote 4")<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sensitive_compartmented_information" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Source details in endnotes.</span></span></span>

* unique fusion of multiple intelligence streams;
* proprietary analytic models;
* specialised pattern-recognition techniques;
* methods for attributing [cyber]({{ 'cyber/' | relative_url }}) activity;
* indicators used to assess military readiness; or
* processes that reveal how separate pieces of intelligence are combined.

Protecting analytical methodology prevents adversaries from reverse-engineering intelligence capabilities simply by studying finished assessments.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.odni.gov/files/documents/ICD/ICD-703.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: odni.gov">[ODNI]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">odni.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">25, 2023 — 21 Jun 2013 — The Director of National Intelligence (DNI) is responsible for the protection of intelligence sources and method...</span></span></span>

## Examples of SCI Control Systems

SCI is organised through formal control systems rather than through a single universal compartment. Many historical codewords remain classified, but several major systems have been publicly acknowledged or declassified.

### Human Intelligence Control System (HCS)

The Human Intelligence Control System protects particularly sensitive human-source reporting.

Rather than merely classifying the information, HCS limits access because disclosure could reveal operational details, recruitment methods or the identities of human sources whose safety depends upon continued secrecy. Training material issued for SCI personnel identifies HCS as the current control system replacing the older HUMINT control marking.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cdse.edu/Portals/124/Documents/student-guides/SCI100-guide.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cdse.edu">[CDSE]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cdse.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Sensitive Compartmented Information (SCI) RefresherSCI control systems are additional measures used to protect intelligence sources a...</span></span></span>

### Special Intelligence (SI)

Special Intelligence (SI), formerly known as COMINT, protects communications intelligence and associated processing.

Its purpose is not simply to conceal intercepted communications but also to protect interception capabilities, cryptologic methods and exploitation techniques whose disclosure could rapidly reduce intelligence effectiveness.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cdse.edu/Portals/124/Documents/student-guides/SCI100-guide.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cdse.edu">[CDSE]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cdse.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Sensitive Compartmented Information (SCI) RefresherSCI control systems are additional measures used to protect intelligence sources a...</span></span></span>

### TALENT KEYHOLE (TK)

TALENT KEYHOLE protects highly sensitive intelligence associated with overhead collection systems, including certain imagery, signals and measurement intelligence derived from national technical means.

Historically, TALENT and KEYHOLE originated as separate compartments protecting reconnaissance aircraft and satellite programmes before being consolidated into a single control system. Today the marking primarily indicates that particularly sensitive overhead collection capabilities require additional compartmented protection beyond ordinary classification.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sensitive_compartmented_information" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on wikipedia.org.</span></span></span>

### Legacy control systems

Readers examining historical documents may encounter retired or legacy SCI control systems such as BYEMAN, COMINT or earlier HUMINT markings. Many have been replaced administratively while preserving the underlying principle: compartmentalised protection tailored to particularly sensitive intelligence sources and collection capabilities.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sensitive_compartmented_information" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on wikipedia.org.</span></span></span>

* explicit approval for a specific SCI control system;

## Why SCI Differs from Programme-Based Protection

Within the broader comparison between SAPs and intelligence compartments, SCI answers a different governance question.

A SAP asks whether a particular programme, capability or activity requires exceptional administrative protection.

An SCI control system asks whether particular intelligence sources, collection methods or analytical processes require compartmented access under the DNI's intelligence protection authorities.

The two mechanisms can coexist. A defence programme operating under a SAP may generate intelligence handled within SCI systems, while intelligence may inform highly protected acquisition or operational programmes. Nevertheless, their organising principle remains different: SAPs are generally programme-centred, whereas SCI control systems are intelligence-centred. This distinction explains why SCI governance is anchored in protecting enduring intelligence capabilities rather than managing a specific classified project.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.odni.gov/files/documents/ICD/ICD-703.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: odni.gov">[odni.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">odni.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">25, 2023 — 21 Jun 2013 — The Director of National Intelligence (DNI) is responsible for the protection of intelligence sources and method...</span></span></span>
