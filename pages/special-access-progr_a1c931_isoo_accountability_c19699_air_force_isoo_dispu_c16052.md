## Introduction

The dispute over the Air Force's 2017 Special Access Program (SAP) policy was not about whether SAPs required tight security. Instead, it centred on a narrower but important governance question: could an Air Force policy describing SAP oversight omit the statutory role of the Information Security Oversight Office ([ISOO]({{ 'isoo/' | relative_url }})), even though Executive Order 13526 explicitly grants [ISOO access]({{ 'isoo-access/' | relative_url }}) to SAPs for oversight purposes? The episode became a revealing example of the tension between internal military management of highly compartmented programmes and the government-wide classification system that is supposed to oversee them. Although the disagreement did not become a major public legal battle, it illustrated how bureaucratic practice can drift towards treating SAP oversight as an entirely internal matter, even when executive policy says otherwise.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://static.e-publishing.af.mil/production/1/saf_aa/publication/afpd16-7/afpd16-7.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: static.e-publishing.af.mil">[af.mil]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">static.e-publishing.af.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">pd16 7</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">E-PublishingAFPD16-7November 21, 2017 — 21 Nov 2017 — Special Access Programs shall be established and maintained only when absolutely ne...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 21, 2017</span></span></span>

## What the Air Force policy appeared to omit

In November 2017, the Air Force issued Air Force Policy Directive (AFPD) 16-7, setting out its framework for managing Special Access Programs. The directive described an extensive internal governance structure, including the Special Programs Oversight Committee, the Special Programs Review Group and the Air Force Special Access Program Central Office. It also identified responsibilities for senior Air Force leaders, inspectors general, legal offices and congressional reporting requirements, particularly those established under Department of Defense policy and [statute]({{ 'statute/' | relative_url }}).<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://static.e-publishing.af.mil/production/1/saf_aa/publication/afpd16-7/afpd16-7.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: static.e-publishing.af.mil">[E-Publishing]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">static.e-publishing.af.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">pd16 7</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">E-PublishingAFPD16-7November 21, 2017 — 21 Nov 2017 — Special Access Programs shall be established and maintained only when absolutely ne...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 21, 2017</span></span></span>

The directive repeatedly emphasised that SAPs were to be established only when "absolutely necessary", reflecting the language of Executive Order 13526. It also recognised congressional access under the rules governing acknowledged, unacknowledged and waived SAPs, including the tighter notification rules that apply to waived programmes.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://static.e-publishing.af.mil/production/1/saf_aa/publication/afpd16-7/afpd16-7.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: static.e-publishing.af.mil">[E-Publishing]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">static.e-publishing.af.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">pd16 7</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">E-PublishingAFPD16-7November 21, 2017 — 21 Nov 2017 — Special Access Programs shall be established and maintained only when absolutely ne...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 21, 2017</span></span></span>

What attracted attention, however, was what the directive did not say. Nowhere did it acknowledge the oversight role assigned to ISOO under Executive Order 13526. That omission stood out because the executive order expressly provides that the Director of ISOO "shall be afforded access" to special access programmes, subject to each programme's security requirements and limited exceptions for exceptionally sensitive [intelligence]({{ 'intelligence/' | relative_url }}) activities.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/isoo/policy-documents/cnsi-eo.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">cnsi eo</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesThe President Executive Order 1352629 Dec 2009 — This order prescribes a uniform system for classifying, safeguarding, a...</span></span></span>

The omission did not itself remove ISOO's legal authority. Executive orders remain controlling across the executive branch unless lawfully superseded. Nevertheless, policy documents are important because they communicate institutional expectations to programme managers, security officials and [acquisition]({{ 'acquisition/' | relative_url }}) organisations. Leaving ISOO out of the Air Force's principal SAP policy risked reinforcing the impression that [accountability]({{ 'accountability/' | relative_url }}) was confined to internal military channels.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://static.e-publishing.af.mil/production/1/saf_aa/publication/afpd16-7/afpd16-7.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: static.e-publishing.af.mil">[E-Publishing]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">static.e-publishing.af.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">pd16 7</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">E-PublishingAFPD16-7November 21, 2017 — 21 Nov 2017 — Special Access Programs shall be established and maintained only when absolutely ne...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 21, 2017</span></span></span>

## How ISOO defended its access authority

The disagreement became public through reporting by the Federation of American Scientists' *Secrecy News*, which noted that AFPD 16-7 contained detailed provisions for internal oversight while failing to recognise ISOO's oversight authority.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://fas.org/publication/usaf-saps/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: fas.org">[Federation of American Scientists]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">fas.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Federation of American ScientistsUS Air Force Updates Policy on Special Access ProgramsThe new Air Force policy makes provisions for inte...</span></span></span>

ISOO Director Mark Bradley responded directly. Rather than arguing for broader access than the executive order already provided, Bradley pointed to the existing legal framework. Executive Order 13526, section 4.3, states that ISOO's Director is to be afforded access to SAPs in order to carry out assigned oversight responsibilities. According to Bradley, the Air Force directive was simply inconsistent with that requirement, and ISOO intended to communicate the issue to the Air Force.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/isoo/policy-documents/cnsi-eo.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">cnsi eo</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesThe President Executive Order 1352629 Dec 2009 — This order prescribes a uniform system for classifying, safeguarding, a...</span></span></span>

This response is significant because ISOO did not claim an unlimited right to inspect every aspect of every compartment. The executive order already balances oversight with operational security by requiring access to occur in accordance with programme security requirements and by recognising limited restrictions for exceptionally sensitive intelligence activities. ISOO's position was therefore that government-wide oversight remained part of the SAP system rather than an external intrusion into it.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/isoo/policy-documents/cnsi-eo.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">cnsi eo</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesThe President Executive Order 1352629 Dec 2009 — This order prescribes a uniform system for classifying, safeguarding, a...</span></span></span>

The dispute also demonstrated that oversight disagreements may arise through administrative drafting rather than through dramatic confrontations over classified [operations]({{ 'operations/' | relative_url }}). A single omitted institutional role in a policy directive was enough to raise questions about whether executive-branch accountability mechanisms were being fully recognised.

## What the episode says about compartmented culture

The 2017 episode illustrates a recurring tendency within highly compartmented organisations. Because SAPs are deliberately isolated from ordinary administrative processes, programme managers naturally focus on the oversight bodies they encounter most frequently: departmental leadership, inspectors general, security offices and designated congressional committees.

That culture can unintentionally encourage the perception that oversight exists entirely inside the organisation operating the programme. Yet Executive Order 13526 establishes a different model. It treats SAPs as part of a single government-wide classification system rather than as autonomous islands of secrecy. ISOO's role exists precisely to ensure that agencies continue to justify enhanced compartmentation, maintain proper records and comply with common classification standards across the executive branch.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/isoo/policy-documents/cnsi-eo.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">cnsi eo</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesThe President Executive Order 1352629 Dec 2009 — This order prescribes a uniform system for classifying, safeguarding, a...</span></span></span>

The Air Force directive itself reflected this tension. It created elaborate mechanisms for Air Force governance while extensively cross-referencing Department of Defense SAP policy, but it omitted the one office specifically designated to oversee the classification system across all executive agencies. ## Why the dispute mattered beyond one directive

The practical consequences of the 2017 dispute were less about operational access than about constitutional and administrative accountability.

Several broader lessons emerge:

* **Government-wide oversight is distinct from internal management.** A military department may administer its own SAPs, but Executive Order 13526 places those programmes within a broader executive classification framework.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/isoo/policy-documents/cnsi-eo.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">cnsi eo</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesThe President Executive Order 1352629 Dec 2009 — This order prescribes a uniform system for classifying, safeguarding, a...</span></span></span>
* **Policy language shapes institutional behaviour.** Even when higher-level legal authorities remain controlling, omissions in implementing directives can influence how officials understand reporting relationships and oversight responsibilities.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://static.e-publishing.af.mil/production/1/saf_aa/publication/afpd16-7/afpd16-7.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: static.e-publishing.af.mil">[E-Publishing]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">static.e-publishing.af.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">pd16 7</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">E-PublishingAFPD16-7November 21, 2017 — 21 Nov 2017 — Special Access Programs shall be established and maintained only when absolutely ne...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 21, 2017</span></span></span>
* **Compartmentation can obscure oversight structures.** The more restrictive a programme becomes, the easier it is for participants to regard external oversight as exceptional rather than routine.
* **Oversight disputes often concern process rather than disclosure.** ISOO was not seeking public transparency into classified programmes; it was asserting the executive branch's own internal accountability mechanisms established by presidential order.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/isoo/policy-documents/cnsi-eo.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">cnsi eo</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesThe President Executive Order 1352629 Dec 2009 — This order prescribes a uniform system for classifying, safeguarding, a...</span></span></span>

For students of special access programmes, the Air Force policy episode is therefore valuable not because it revealed classified activities, but because it exposed an enduring governance challenge: maintaining independent oversight within systems deliberately designed to minimise the number of people who know they exist.
