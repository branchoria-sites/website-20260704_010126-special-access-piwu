## Introduction

Before the United States Department of Defense (DoD) publicly acknowledges, declassifies, or changes the status of a Special Access Program (SAP), [Congress]({{ 'congress/' | relative_url }}) is normally entitled to advance notice. This is commonly described as the **14-day notice rule**. Rather than allowing a surprise public announcement, DoD policy requires that the appropriate congressional committees receive a formal report describing the proposed classification change, the reasons for it, and any planned public announcement. Except where a statutory exception applies, the Department is expected to wait at least 14 days after providing that notification before taking the action or making the announcement. This procedure is an important governance safeguard because it gives Congress an opportunity to exercise oversight before highly sensitive programmes move into a more public phase.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/DD/issuances/dodi/520511p.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[WHS ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">WHS ESDDoD Instruction 5205.11, &quot;Management, Administration...September 11, 2024 — 12 Sept 2024 — (1) The DoD SAPCO will notify the con...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 11, 2024</span></span></span>

## What the 14-day notice rule requires

The rule applies when the Department of Defense decides to take significant action affecting the public status of a SAP. In practice, this includes decisions to:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--decision" markdown="1">

* change a SAP's classification;
* declassify a SAP;
* move a programme towards public acknowledgement; or
* make a public announcement connected with those changes.

</div>

Current DoD policy directs the DoD Special Access Program Central Office (SAPCO) to notify the appropriate congressional defence committees—and, for [intelligence]({{ 'intelligence/' | relative_url }}) or intelligence-related SAPs, the congressional intelligence committees—once such a decision has been made. The notification must include:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--caution" markdown="1">

* a description of the proposed change;
* the reasons for making it; and
* notice of any planned public announcement.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/DD/issuances/dodi/520511p.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[WHS ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">WHS ESDDoD Instruction 5205.11, &quot;Management, Administration...September 11, 2024 — 12 Sept 2024 — (1) The DoD SAPCO will notify the con...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 11, 2024</span></span></span>

</div>

Earlier DoD SAP regulations made the timing requirement explicit by stating that no action or public announcement should occur until **14 days** after delivery of the notification unless an authorised exception applied. Although modern policy documents have been reorganised and updated, they continue to preserve the principle that Congress is informed before a SAP is deliberately moved into the public domain.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/DD/issuances/dodi/520511p.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[WHS ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">WHS ESDDoD Instruction 5205.11, &quot;Management, Administration...September 11, 2024 — 12 Sept 2024 — (1) The DoD SAPCO will notify the con...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 11, 2024</span></span></span>

## Why Congress gets advance warning

The 14-day interval is not primarily about delaying public disclosure. Instead, it creates a structured oversight window between an executive branch decision and its public implementation.

That period enables congressional committees to:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--caution" markdown="1">

* review the Department's rationale for changing the programme's status;
* seek classified briefings if questions arise;
* understand what information will become public and what will remain protected;
* evaluate whether the proposed disclosure is consistent with national security and statutory reporting obligations.

</div>

For especially sensitive SAPs—including intelligence-related programmes—the notification also extends to the congressional intelligence committees, reflecting the broader oversight framework for highly compartmented activities.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/DD/issuances/dodi/520511p.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[WHS ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">WHS ESDDoD Instruction 5205.11, &quot;Management, Administration...September 11, 2024 — 12 Sept 2024 — (1) The DoD SAPCO will notify the con...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 11, 2024</span></span></span>

The procedure also reinforces a broader constitutional balance. Classification decisions belong principally to the executive branch, but Congress has statutory oversight responsibilities for defence programmes. Advance notification helps ensure that significant changes to exceptionally sensitive programmes do not bypass legislative scrutiny.

## Why disclosure begins before the press release

One common misunderstanding is that declassification begins with a press conference. In reality, the public announcement is typically the final visible step in an internal government process.

Before any public acknowledgement, officials generally must complete several governance tasks, including:

* approving the classification change through the appropriate DoD decision process;
* determining precisely which information will become public and which information will remain classified;
* preparing the congressional notification package; and
* observing the required notification period before any announcement is made, unless an authorised exception applies.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/DD/issuances/dodi/520511p.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[WHS ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">WHS ESDDoD Instruction 5205.11, &quot;Management, Administration...September 11, 2024 — 12 Sept 2024 — (1) The DoD SAPCO will notify the con...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 11, 2024</span></span></span>

This sequence helps explain why newly acknowledged defence programmes often appear highly choreographed. The announcement is usually preceded by legal review, security review, congressional notification and communications planning rather than representing a spontaneous decision to reveal previously secret information.

## When exceptional circumstances can shorten notice

The 14-day period is not absolute. Historic DoD SAP regulations recognised that statutory exceptions could justify acting sooner than the normal waiting period. Those exceptions are intended for unusual situations rather than routine programme management.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/11908/chapter/14" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationalacademies.org">[National Academies]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationalacademies.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">1997. Information Security Program. DoD 5200.1-R. Washington, DC: Office of the Assistant Secretary of Defense/Command, Control...Read more...</span></span></span>

Publicly available guidance, however, provides relatively little detail about the circumstances in which shortened notice would be used. That lack of specificity is unsurprising because decisions involving exceptionally sensitive SAPs often occur within classified oversight channels. Consequently, while the existence of exceptions is acknowledged in policy, the public record contains few documented examples demonstrating how they have been applied in individual [SAP declassification]({{ 'declassification/' | relative_url }}) decisions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/DD/issuances/dodi/520511p.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[WHS ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">WHS ESDDoD Instruction 5205.11, &quot;Management, Administration...September 11, 2024 — 12 Sept 2024 — (1) The DoD SAPCO will notify the con...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 11, 2024</span></span></span>

## What the rule does not mean

The 14-day notification requirement should not be confused with automatic declassification or with a requirement to release all information about a programme.

Executive Order 13526 requires agencies to declassify information when it no longer meets the standards for classification, but it also allows information whose disclosure would still damage national security to remain classified. As a result, a SAP may become publicly acknowledged while many operational, technical or intelligence details continue to receive classified protection.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://obamawhitehouse.archives.gov/the-press-office/executive-order-classified-national-security-information" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: obamawhitehouse.archives.gov">[whitehouse.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">obamawhitehouse.archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">executive order classified national security information</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Order 13526- Classified National Security Information29 Dec 2009 — Agencies conducting a mandatory review for declassification shall decl...</span></span></span>

Similarly, congressional notification does not give Congress authority to declassify executive branch information directly. Instead, it ensures that lawmakers receive advance warning of significant executive decisions affecting some of the government's most tightly controlled defence programmes.

## Why the 14-day rule matters

The advance-notice requirement illustrates that SAP declassification is intended to be a governed administrative process rather than a single public event. By requiring formal notification before disclosure, DoD policy seeks to balance transparency, democratic oversight and national security.

For readers examining public declassification plans for acknowledged, unacknowledged or even exceptionally sensitive SAPs, the rule demonstrates that disclosure is generally expected to proceed in stages: internal approval, congressional notification, the waiting period where applicable, and only then any public announcement. That sequencing helps preserve legislative oversight while allowing the executive branch to manage the risks associated with revealing formerly compartmented defence programmes.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/DD/issuances/dodi/520511p.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[WHS ESD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">WHS ESDDoD Instruction 5205.11, &quot;Management, Administration...September 11, 2024 — 12 Sept 2024 — (1) The DoD SAPCO will notify the con...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 11, 2024</span></span></span>
